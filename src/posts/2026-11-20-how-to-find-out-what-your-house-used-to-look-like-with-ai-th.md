Your house has been lying to you.

Somewhere between 1973 and last Tuesday, it lost its sash windows, its chimney stacks, its front garden wall, its coloured tile path, possibly its entire front door. You moved in after all that happened. What it used to look like is a closed question.

Except it isn't.

Upload a photo of your house's exterior to Claude or ChatGPT and ask it to identify the architectural style and approximate decade of construction. Then ask the question nobody types into Google, because Google can only tell you about houses in general: *given what you can see in this photo, what original features have almost certainly been removed, and what would they have looked like?*

The assistant looks at your specific brickwork, your window proportions, your roofline, the shadow where a bay might have been. [Edwardian and Victorian houses carry the evidence of what was stripped](https://www.architecturecourses.org/architecture/edwardian-house) in the fabric itself. The brick opening reveals the original window's proportions even after a chunky uPVC replacement has been fitted inside it. The assistant reads that. It tells you what colour the front door was likely painted, what tiles the original path probably had, whether there would have been a small porch canopy.

YouTube has ten thousand videos about Victorian terraces. None of them know your Victorian terrace.

**Step 1.** Take a clear photo of the front of your house from across the street. Daylight, no filters.

**Step 2.** Upload it to [Claude](https://claude.ai) or [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com). Free tier works on both.

**Step 3.** Use this prompt:

*Look at this house carefully. What architectural style is it, and roughly when was it built? Then tell me: based on what you can see in the photo, what original features have probably been removed or replaced since it was built? What would they have looked like? Be specific about what the evidence in the photo is telling you.*

**Step 4.** Follow up: *If I wanted to restore one original feature without spending much, what would give the most back?*

**Where it goes wrong.** The assistant can misread the era if the house has been heavily altered or is a 1980s build designed to look older. It sometimes states specific period details with more confidence than the evidence warrants. Treat it as a very informed starting point, not a surveyor's report. If you're in a conservation area, [some changes to period properties require planning permission](https://www.everest.co.uk/types-of-houses/) even when they're restorations.

**What it costs.** Nothing. Under twenty minutes.

Most people who live in a Victorian or Edwardian terrace have a vague sense that something is missing without being able to name it. [A house can look wrong without anyone being able to say what changed.](https://www.architecturecourses.org/architecture/edwardian-house)

Now you can say what changed.
