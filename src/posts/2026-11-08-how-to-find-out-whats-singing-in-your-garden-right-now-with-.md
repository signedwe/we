Your garden has been making noise all morning. You've got no idea who's doing it.

In under a minute, your phone can name every bird currently singing within earshot. The actual birds. Your actual garden. Right now.

**What you'll have at the end:** a live list of every species singing around you, with photos, free, forever.

YouTube can teach you what a wren sounds like in theory. It can't tell you that the thing you've been calling "some kind of finch" has been a chiffchaff, every Tuesday, since March.

---

**Step 1.** Download [Merlin Bird ID by Cornell Lab](https://merlin.allaboutbirds.org/download/). Free on iPhone and Android. No subscription. It runs on Android 7 or newer and iOS 18 or newer.

**Step 2.** Open it and download the **Europe** Bird Pack when prompted. Takes about two minutes on wifi. Without it, you might get suggested a roadrunner.

**Step 3.** Tap **Sound ID**, the microphone icon on the home screen. Hold your phone up. Say nothing.

Merlin starts listening. Names appear on the screen in real time. <cite index="31-4,31-5">The app shows a best-match list of the birds it thinks it can hear, each with a photo, and highlights the name in yellow when that species is singing loudest.</cite>

*Try this:* leave it running for thirty seconds and count how many species are there that you hadn't noticed.

**What it costs:** nothing. The Cornell Lab built it free and [says it always will be](https://support.ebird.org/en/support/solutions/articles/48000961587-merlin-bird-id-faqs).

**How long:** under a minute to your first name.

**Where it goes wrong:** Merlin is very good on common garden birds. Robins, wrens, blackcaps, great tits. It gets shakier on species that sound alike. <cite index="28-4,28-5">It performs impressive identifications, but can also get things wrong, mistaking car alarms, frogs, and mechanical noises for actual bird species.</cite> <cite index="29-6,29-7">When it makes a mistake, it's often an issue of audio length. The app analyses sound in three-second intervals, but accurate identification sometimes requires a longer snippet.</cite> If it tells you there's a golden oriole in your Hackney yard, it's probably picked up a creak. The common birds it nails.

<cite index="31-6">It's quite thrilling when it keeps finding birds you hadn't realised were near you, and it helps with birds that are easier to hear than see.</cite>

For years, knowing what was singing required a field guide, a club membership, and a free weekday morning. Now it requires a phone.
