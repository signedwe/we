A local news editor opens her laptop on a Tuesday morning. The Competition and Markets Authority has [opened a case against Meta](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/uk-competition-watchdog-opens-investigation-into-meta-scraping-terms/). She reads it. Then she sits back.

She already signed the contract. Probably 2019, when the paper made a Facebook business account to post job listings and reach the people who'd stopped buying print.

That's what the CMA story doesn't say out loud. [The new terms let Meta scrape any website linked to a business account](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/) on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Threads. [Virtually every news organisation has one.](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/) The terms don't require you to post on Meta. They require you to have used its business tools. Ads Manager. A Facebook page. Business Suite. [Before the original 30 October deadline, there was no way to opt out.](https://www.newsminimalist.com/articles/metas-new-terms-allow-ai-scraping-from-business-accounts-d69ee4b7) The choice was: quit, or consent.

[On 16 October, Meta removed the word "train" from the terms.](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/meta-delays-rollout-of-terms-that-would-allow-broad-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-are-still-skeptical/) The new wording lets it use scraped content to "develop, improve, research and maintain advertising and business products." [News organisations said that still covers its AI services.](https://pressgazette.co.uk/news/uk-competition-watchdog-opens-investigation-into-meta-scraping-terms/) Meta said it would respect robots.txt. [That promise is in a spokesperson's statement. It's not in the terms.](https://tech-insider.org/meta-commercial-terms-ai-training-news-sites-2026/)

By the end of 2028, at least one UK news organisation will have left Meta's platforms, published six months of traffic data showing the drop, and twelve months showing the recovery. If none leave, the grip held.

[The CMA is running an abuse-of-dominance case under the Competition Act 1998.](https://courtscast.com/uk-cma-meta-publisher-ai-content-investigation-2026/) [Information gathering runs to January 2027.](https://qz.com/meta-uk-antitrust-probe-publisher-content-scraping-103026) The terms were paused, not dropped.

Here's what competition law can't quite reach. The content Meta wants is content news organisations made to find audiences on Meta's own platform. The dependency was built first, over a decade, by the organisations now asking for protection. This isn't a grab from a standing start. It's the invoice for a relationship that ran on the understanding both sides would pretend it was free.

The editor still has her Facebook page. She needs it. Her paper has been shaped for ten years around what that platform rewarded. The CMA can ask whether the terms are fair. It can't ask whether the dependency was worth building.

She signed the contract in 2019. The terms just said what it was for.
