Your council tax band was set in 1991. The bill arrives every year looking official. Most people pay it.

[A Which? survey of 15,000 people](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/how-easy-is-it-to-challenge-your-council-tax-band-aynA23q1G9pb) found 89% have never questioned their banding. Of those who did challenge, 23% only found out they could through consumer media. Almost nobody heard it from their council.

[The Valuation Office Agency resolved 39,590 cases in 2023-24](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/one-in-three-got-a-council-tax-refund-last-year-how-do-you-challenge-your-band-aMtSj5S7vwQJ). 10,530 of them got a lower band. Just 30 got a higher one. Fewer than 44,000 challenged in a country of tens of millions of households.

Drop from Band D to Band C and you save [around £241 a year nationally](https://counciltaxchecker.co.uk/blog/council-tax-band-rates-2025-26). WE's arithmetic: 10,530 winners × £241 = roughly £2.5 million in annual savings unlocked last year by households that asked. That figure appears in none of the sources; the inputs do.

A YouTube tutorial tells you the steps. It can't look at your postcode, find which neighbours sit in a lower band, estimate what your house was worth on 1 April 1991, and draft your challenge letter. An AI assistant does all four in one session, adapting to your property rather than somebody else's.

**What you'll have at the end:** a decision on whether you have a case, comparable properties as evidence, a rough 1991 estimate, and a draft letter to the VOA. About 45 minutes. Free.

---

**Step 1. Find your current band.**

Go to [gov.uk/challenge-council-tax-band](https://www.gov.uk/challenge-council-tax-band). Search your address. Write it down.

**Step 2. Look up your neighbours.**

Same site. Search two or three similar nearby properties. A house the same type and size as yours in a lower band is your starting evidence.

*"I'm in Band D in [postcode]. My neighbour at [address] is in Band C. Our houses look similar in size and age. Do I have grounds to challenge?"*

**Step 3. Estimate your 1991 value.**

[The bands were fixed on property values on 1 April 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_Tax). In England, that date has never been updated.

*"What would a three-bedroom 1970s terrace in [your town] have sold for in April 1991? What council tax band does that suggest?"*

Treat the answer as a direction. Your comparable properties matter more.

**Step 4. Choose your route.**

[Two options](https://valuationoffice.blog.gov.uk/2023/02/03/council-tax-band-challenges/): a band review (evidence required upfront; outcome cannot be appealed) or a proposal (no evidence required upfront; outcome can go to an independent Valuation Tribunal if the VOA says no). If uncertain, use the proposal.

**Step 5. Write the letter.**

*"Write a short factual letter to the Valuation Office Agency proposing a band review for my property at [address]. I'm in Band D. My comparable evidence is [neighbours' addresses and their bands]. My property is [type, number of bedrooms]."*

Submit at [gov.uk/challenge-council-tax-band](https://www.gov.uk/challenge-council-tax-band) or email [ctinbox@voa.gov.uk](mailto:ctinbox@voa.gov.uk).

---

**Where it goes wrong**

Just 30 out of 39,590 challenges got a higher band. But that risk is real: don't challenge if your band already looks low relative to your street. The AI's 1991 estimate is not a surveyor's assessment. [The VOA takes two to four months to decide](https://smartsmssolutions.com/resources/blog/uk/council-tax-band-challenge-appeal-guide); you keep paying at your current rate while they do.
