Your friends have been claiming they know you for years. Tonight you can find out.

This isn't the pub quiz you host in a room. It's a link you drop in the group chat at 9pm, and by morning someone has exposed themselves as a fraud. The old way was a Google Form you built question by question, or a BuzzFeed template so generic it could've been about anyone. AI makes the questions about *you* — the specific, embarrassing, verifiable ones — and a free tool turns them into a live link in about twenty minutes.

Here's what you'll have at the end: a scored quiz your friends take on their phones, with their results waiting when you wake up.

**Step 1. Write your facts (10 minutes)**

Open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini — free tier is fine. Don't start with the quiz yet. Tell it about yourself in bullet points: what city you grew up in, your most embarrassing hobby, what you nearly studied at university, your irrational food rule, the film you've seen more than five times, the first thing you do when you get home.

Fifteen facts is enough. The weirder and more specific, the better. The question that exposes a fake friend is never 'what's her favourite colour.' It's 'what does she always order when she can't decide.'

**Step 2. Ask for a quiz as one HTML file (5 minutes)**

Once you've put in your facts, paste this into the same chat:

*Make a ten-question multiple-choice quiz about me using those facts, with four options each and a score at the end. Build it as a single self-contained HTML file I can upload somewhere. Keep the design clean and mobile-friendly.*

Claude and ChatGPT both write working HTML. Download or copy the file.

**Step 3. Get a shareable link (5 minutes)**

Go to [convika.com](https://convika.com). Make a free account — no card needed. Upload the HTML file. Click Publish. As of October 2026, the free plan costs nothing and needs no credit card, and your players need no account at all to take the quiz. The quiz lives at an address like yourpage.convika.page. Copy the link.

**Step 4. Drop it (30 seconds)**

Send it to the group chat. Friends open it on their phones, pick their answers, get a score. You'll see who's been paying attention.

**What it costs:** Nothing.

**How long:** About twenty minutes to build; five minutes to play.

**Where it goes wrong:** ChatGPT sometimes writes HTML with small bugs — a button that doesn't advance, a score that doesn't add up. If it breaks, paste the file back and say *the Next button doesn't work, please fix it.* That usually sorts it in one go. Also: free pages on Convika are public, so search engines may find them — if your facts are very private, keep the questions light.

**What AI does here that YouTube can't:** YouTube will show you how to make a generic quiz. It won't ask about your irrational food rule.
