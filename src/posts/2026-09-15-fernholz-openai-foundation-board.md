Humanity already owns OpenAI. Nobody asked humanity.

Since last October, [the OpenAI Foundation appoints every director of OpenAI Group](https://openai.com/our-structure/) and "can replace directors at any time." It holds 26 per cent of the equity, worth about $130 billion by OpenAI's count, in a company whose mission is to make sure AGI "benefits all of humanity." So a big AI company has already handed humanity a majority: all of the votes and a quarter of the money.

[Tim Fernholz reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/openai-adds-a-prominent-ai-doomer-to-its-board-of-directors/) that Paul Christiano has joined that Foundation's board and its safety committee, which decides what ships. Fernholz reads it as safety news, and Christiano's own line about "catastrophic and irreversible loss of control" backs him up. I read it as governance news. The room that decides what ships just chose its own newest member. The choosing is the story.

Because the Foundation's board picks itself. The structure page lists the directors and names nobody outside with any say in who they are. Humanity got all the control and none of the choosing. So the ask people keep making, give 51 per cent to humanity, has the wrong number in it. On control, humanity already has 100. On money, 26. What's missing is the word humanity, because a foundation isn't humanity. It's [eleven people](https://openai.com/our-structure/) with a charter, and one of them runs the company they own.

This has been done before. The [Novo Nordisk Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_Nordisk_Foundation) holds 28.1 per cent of Novo Nordisk's shares and 77.3 per cent of its votes, almost the same shape, and its board picks itself too. What makes it work isn't a vote. It's two things Danish law supplies: the foundation can't be unwound for private benefit, and a state authority reads its accounts and can remove trustees. OpenAI's page says the restructuring followed "nearly a year of engaging in constructive dialogue" with two attorneys general. It doesn't say the dialogue continues.

The bet: at the end of 2027 the Foundation's board will still be picking itself. Not one seat filled by anyone outside the room. OpenAI's own structure page will say so. If one seat opens up, I was wrong.

Three ways to make the word true, each with a price. Members: [Wikimedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation#Board_of_Trustees) fills some trustee seats by community vote, and the price is a roll of people someone has to recruit. Lot: a panel of the people the models get used on, drawn at random, and the price is that none of them will be Paul Christiano. Supervision: the Danish route, and the price is a public body with a key, the thing OpenAI negotiated with for a year and then left.

The case for leaving it alone: self-appointed foundation boards have run Novo Nordisk and Carlsberg for decades, elected boards get captured by their staff, and the Foundation just added the field's most credible critic, whom no electorate would have picked.

But look at October. Before, a nonprofit with total control sat over a capped-profit company. After, the same nonprofit holds [26 per cent of an ordinary one, plus a warrant for more](https://openai.com/our-structure/). The structure changed itself once, through the same board, and nothing says it can't again. Fifty-one per cent was never the number. Humanity holds all the votes and can't get into the room where they're cast.
