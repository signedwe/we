Humanity already owns OpenAI. Nobody asked humanity.

Since the restructuring last October, [the OpenAI Foundation appoints every director of OpenAI Group](https://openai.com/our-structure/) and "can replace directors at any time," and it holds 26 per cent of the equity, which OpenAI values at about $130 billion, in a company whose mission is to ensure that AGI "benefits all of humanity." So a big AI company has already handed a majority to humanity: all of the votes and a quarter of the money.

[Tim Fernholz reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/openai-adds-a-prominent-ai-doomer-to-its-board-of-directors/) that Paul Christiano has joined that Foundation's board and its Safety and Security Committee, which has authority over model releases. Fernholz reads it as safety news, and Christiano's own line about "catastrophic and irreversible loss of control" supports him. I read it as governance news. The room that decides what ships has just chosen its own newest member, and the choosing is the story.

Because the Foundation's board appoints itself. The structure page lists the directors and names no outside body with any say in who they are. Humanity got all of the control and none of the choosing. So the ask people keep making, give 51 per cent to humanity, has the wrong number in it. On control the figure is already 100. On money it is 26. The missing thing is the word humanity, because a foundation is not humanity. It is [eleven people](https://openai.com/our-structure/) with a charter, one of whom is the chief executive of the company they own.

Companies have done this before. The [Novo Nordisk Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novo_Nordisk_Foundation) holds 28.1 per cent of Novo Nordisk's shares and 77.3 per cent of its votes, a shape almost identical to OpenAI's, and its board also appoints itself. What makes it work is not a vote but two things Danish foundation law supplies: a statute that says the foundation cannot be unwound for private benefit, and a state authority that reads its accounts and can remove trustees. OpenAI's structure page says the restructuring followed "nearly a year of engaging in constructive dialogue" with the attorneys general of California and Delaware. It does not say the dialogue continues.

The bet: at the end of 2027 the Foundation's board will still be picking itself. Not one seat filled by anyone outside the room. OpenAI's own structure page will say so. If one seat opens up, I was wrong.

Three ways exist to make the word true, each with a price. Members: the [Wikimedia Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikimedia_Foundation#Board_of_Trustees) fills some trustee seats by community selection, and the price is a roll of people who have to be recruited. Lot: a panel of the people the models get used on, drawn at random for a term, and the price is that none of them will be Paul Christiano. Supervision: the Danish route, and the price is a public body with a key, the thing OpenAI negotiated with for a year and then left.

The case for leaving it alone: self-appointed foundation boards have run Novo Nordisk and Carlsberg for decades, elected mutual boards get captured by their staff, and the Foundation just added the field's most credible safety critic, whom no electorate would have picked.

But note October. Before, a nonprofit with total control sat over a capped-profit company. After, the same nonprofit holds [26 per cent of an ordinary one and a warrant for more](https://openai.com/our-structure/). The structure changed itself once, through the same board, and nothing says it cannot again. Fifty-one per cent was never the number. Humanity holds all the votes and cannot get into the room where they get cast.
