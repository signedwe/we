The Data Controller never understood the data. That gap sat in the title for forty-two years and nobody noticed.

Born 8 May 1984, in Schedule 1 of the [Data Protection Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/35/contents). Redrafted in 1998. Reborn in UK law on 1 January 2021. For four decades it rested on one thing: a legal person made a decision, the data moved, and you could find them.

It worked. When a hospital lost records, there was a name on the form. When a marketing firm misused an email list, someone got fined. The concept didn't catch everything. But it gave you a door.

That door closed yesterday.

The [ICO's call for evidence on agentic AI](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/ico-and-stakeholder-consultations/2026/10/agentic-ai-call-for-evidence/) closed on 20 November 2026, six weeks after it opened, [thirty questions](https://ppc.land/uk-ico-puts-30-questions-to-ai-agent-builders-before-november-20/) deep. Strip them back and they ask one thing: when an AI agent books a flight, files a report, triggers a purchase order and emails three people before anyone checks, who decided?

Nobody did. That's the problem. Not nobody accountable. Nobody decided.

The ICO's own director of technology regulation, Richard Nevinson, [put it this way](https://steelwise.uk/filings/the-ico-is-asking-how-businesses-control-ai-agents-can-you-answer): "the fact AI agents act with autonomy is not an excuse for poor compliance." Fine. Also a sentence that assumes a person gave the agent its mandate, can be found, and can answer for what it did next. The agent that booked your hotel? Easy case. The orchestrator model that sent four sub-agents into six systems, processed twelve people's records, and finished before breakfast? Nobody's picking up that phone.

The word controller implies a hand. Agents don't have hands. They have an instruction, an API key, and a mandate left open on purpose.

The Controller left behind several people whose business cards still say Data Protection Officer, [Article 5 of UK GDPR](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-protection-principles/a-guide-to-the-data-protection-principles/), and the ICO's Citizen Space portal, now full of responses from firms trying to describe their own software to a regulator.

The funeral gets scheduled once someone decides who books the venue. The form asks for a controller's name. The cursor blinks.
