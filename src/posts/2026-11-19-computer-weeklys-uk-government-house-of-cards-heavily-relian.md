Sovereign AI is a phrase. What sits behind it is Google Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft Azure.

[Computer Weekly reported on 5 November](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366653713/UK-government-house-of-cards-heavily-reliant-on-US-AI-models) that ten of eleven AI tools in central government depend on US models. Gov Draft, the internal drafting assistant, runs on OpenAI or Anthropic via Azure or AWS. Parlex and Lex, the parliamentary research tools, run on Microsoft and Anthropic. GCS Assist, for government communications, runs on Anthropic alone. The only UK model in the list is PolyAI, used by the shared services provider.

Four days later, the Cabinet Office replied: the architecture is model-agnostic. Decoupled. Not tied to any supplier.

---

**POLICY:** We can swap providers. The pipes work with any model.

**OPERATIONS:** Right. When did we last do it?

**POLICY:** That's not the point. The capability exists.

**OPERATIONS:** The NHS Copilot rollout. Five hundred and five thousand staff. Microsoft switched Claude on by default last week. Anthropic now processes that data in the US. Admins have until 9 December to opt out. Where's the switch?

**POLICY:** That's under a national licensing deal.

**OPERATIONS:** And the business continuity plans, the ones covering what happens if a supplier walks. The FOI response confirmed they exist. The Cabinet Office refused to release them.

**POLICY:** They contain information that could compromise security.

**OPERATIONS:** A plan nobody can read is the promise of a plan.

---

By end of 2027, those plans will still be classified. If the Cabinet Office publishes them, I was wrong.

The gap between what looks sovereign and what is sovereign has a number now. [IPPR's Locked In report](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/public-sector-worryingly-dependent-on-us-tech-as-fully-british-share-of-major-contracts-is-less-than-1-in-every-6), published 11 November, found that 42.5% of public sector tech spending appears to go to UK companies. Strip out the resellers and trace actual ownership and the figure drops to 15.4%. That 27-point gap is what the word British is worth in a government procurement document. Bytes and Softcat, two of the three biggest recipients, are UK-registered resellers of overseas products.

The AI minister flew to Silicon Valley this week and [told City A.M.](https://www.cityam.com/uk-does-not-need-its-own-frontier-ai-model-says-ai-minister/) the UK doesn't need its own frontier model. ["As long as I have insurance on the access,"](https://www.cityam.com/uk-does-not-need-its-own-frontier-ai-model-says-ai-minister/) it matters more to build things others depend on. Insurance on access is exactly what a business continuity plan is. That plan is classified.

On 16 November, in a Commons debate about NHS digital contracts, a member said this about a clause that couldn't be exercised: ["it is not a break clause; it is a lock-in."](https://hansard.parliament.uk/commons/2026-11-16/debates/FBAAF525-AB23-4118-9750-2BC93FF9B428/HealthAndCareDigitalCapability)

Nobody said it about AI. That's the problem.
