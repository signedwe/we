HR emailed on Tuesday. Your hours are changing from April. Before you reply, it's worth spending twenty minutes finding out whether they're actually allowed to do that.

Most contracts contain something that sounds like permission: a "flexibility clause" or "variation clause" buried in the small print. [Courts read them narrowly](https://jrlevins.co.uk/can-my-employer-change-my-contract-without-my-consent/). A general phrase saying hours may vary does not let an employer move you from days to nights. It doesn't cover cutting your hours by a third. And if you say nothing and keep showing up under the new arrangement, [you've likely accepted the change without meaning to](https://www.tuc.org.uk/guidance/can-my-employer-change-my-contract-employment).

[Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/work/changes-to-employment-contracts-overview/) and the [TUC](https://www.tuc.org.uk/guidance/can-my-employer-change-my-contract-employment) are both clear: an employer cannot unilaterally change a fundamental term, hours, pay or location, without agreement. The question is whether your specific clause covers this specific change. That depends on the exact words in your document.

**What you'll have at the end:** a plain-English verdict on your own contract, and a draft "working under protest" letter if you need one.

**Step 1.** Find your contract. Check the welcome email from when you started, or any folder labelled New Starter Documents. If it's nowhere, email HR. [They're legally required to give you a copy](https://employmenthero.com/uk/resources/employment-contracts-uk-2026/).

**Step 2.** Take it to an AI assistant. Any of the main ones handle PDFs. Upload it, then ask:

*I've uploaded my employment contract. Find every clause that might allow my employer to change my working hours without my agreement. Quote each one exactly. Then tell me how a UK court would read it: broadly or narrowly, and why.*

**Step 3.** Name the specific change. Hours, days, shift pattern, start date. Then ask:

*My employer wants to change my hours from [current] to [proposed] from [date]. Does any clause in this contract cover that variation? If not, what options do I have?*

**Step 4.** If nothing in the contract covers it, ask:

*Draft a short letter for me to send my employer. It should say I'm continuing to work while explicitly not agreeing to the change. Use the phrase 'working under protest' and name the specific variation.*

The phrase matters. The TUC is explicit: [staying silent is likely treated as acceptance](https://www.tuc.org.uk/guidance/can-my-employer-change-my-contract-employment). The letter preserves your position while you decide what to do next.

**Step 5.** Read the [ACAS guidance on contract changes](https://www.acas.org.uk/changing-an-employment-contract/advice-for-employees/proposing-changes-to-your-employer) before you act.

**What it costs:** nothing. **How long:** twenty minutes with the contract in hand.

**Where it goes wrong:** the assistant doesn't know your workplace or your history there. For anything serious, a significant pay cut, a relocation, a threat to dismiss and re-engage, speak to [ACAS](https://www.acas.org.uk/contact) or Citizens Advice before you send anything. The [Employment Rights Act 2025](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/employment-rights-act-contract-change-update-and-action-points) brought new fire-and-rehire protections due in force from January 2027. If your employer is threatening that route, the rules just changed in your favour.

Also: if you've already been working the new hours for a few weeks without objecting, raise this with an adviser before you do anything else. The window may be shorter than you'd like.
