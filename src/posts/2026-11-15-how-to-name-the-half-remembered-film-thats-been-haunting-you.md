You remember a tunnel. Or a red coat. A girl who turned into something. A man who wasn't real, and the moment you knew it. You were eight, or twelve, and nobody you've asked since has the faintest idea what you're talking about.

There's a name for this. It ends tonight.

Search engines are useless for it. You type fragments and get millions of results, none of them right. What you need is something that can hold a vague description, ask follow-up questions, make guesses, be wrong, adjust, and narrow down. That's a conversation with [Claude](https://claude.ai) or [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com). No specialist app required.

**Step 1.** Type everything you remember. Don't try to be precise. The colour of the light. The genre feeling. Roughly when you saw it. What country it felt like. Any fragment of dialogue, even if you're not sure it's right. Whether there were animals. Whether anyone died. Whether it was funny or frightening. Five minutes of stream of consciousness.

Try this:

*I'm trying to identify something I saw as a child. Live-action, probably British. A scene involving a river or canal. Someone disappeared. Dreamlike, not horror exactly, more uneasy. I think I saw it on BBC in the early nineties. I could be wrong about all of this.*

**Step 2.** Let it guess. It will probably guess wrong. Tell it why. This isn't failure. It's the method. Each wrong guess cuts out a category and gives the AI something to narrow from. If it says *The Water Babies*, tell it no, it was definitely modern. If it says thriller, tell it it felt more like children's drama. Keep going.

**Step 3.** Ask it to question you. Say: *Ask me things that might narrow it down.* It will: Was there music you remember? Was the main character a child or an adult? Did it end happily? You answer. The circle tightens.

**Step 4.** Once it has a name, search it. Non-negotiable. AI sometimes produces a confident, specific, completely wrong title. Check it on IMDb. The feeling of recognition when you see the right poster is unmistakable.

Free on both tools. Fifteen to forty minutes depending how fragmentary your memory is. What goes sideways: the AI sometimes locks onto a wrong answer and commits hard. Start a new chat and describe the thing differently.

The old method was posting on Reddit's r/tipofmytongue, where strangers help for free but the queue is long. AI doesn't replace that community. For obscure regional TV or European children's films, the crowd probably knows more. But AI narrows the problem first, which makes the Reddit post shorter and sharper when you do need it.
