The most important page in Anthropic's [2026 Public Benefit Report](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/a9ceec76a54d28d27affffcdab6faf7c142cd587.pdf) is the last one. The board says it adopted these, under Delaware law, as "standards to measure our progress." Some of them:

"Make sure no single person, company, or AI model controls the future."

"End geographic disparities in survival rates for treatable illnesses."

"Give people more time, freedom, and means to achieve their definition of a good life."

Lovely. Now try measuring one. No number, no date, no starting point. Nobody can ever fail them, so nobody can ever pass. They aren't standards. They're the motto over a school gate.

So the report fills the gap with other things. Read the "progress" sections and count the products. Claude for Small Business. Claude Science. A Claude Team plan for scientists. Claude for Teachers. Claude on US classified networks. Selling things can do good. A sales list doesn't become a public benefit by moving pages.

The numbers have no bottom line. [11.4 million accounts banned](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/a9ceec76a54d28d27affffcdab6faf7c142cd587.pdf) in six months: out of how many? More than [33,000 security flaws found](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/a9ceec76a54d28d27affffcdab6faf7c142cd587.pdf): how many fixed? A Maryland tool "has helped the state provide $14 million in funding": how much would have been spent anyway?

The confessions are real, and rare. "Alignment is far from solved." There is "no mandatory independent check for whether any frontier lab, including Anthropic, is actually following its safety policies." On health and schools: "we have not been active enough in collecting data to measure the results."

Now look at who answers them. On power, the report admits a handful of firms decide how AI is built, and "Anthropic is one of them." Its fix: "we and other research labs" should develop the oversight. The watched, designing the watch.

The review at the back comes from the trust that elects most of the board. It says it "helped develop this report's five charter pillars." It set the exam. Now it marks it. It praises decisions made "even when doing so is commercially or reputationally expensive", and names none.

The old excuse was cost. Checking results meant researchers, home visits and two years. The report's own best story kills that excuse. In Burkina Faso, Claude turned paper clinic registers into dashboards. They [showed more than 6,000 women](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/a9ceec76a54d28d27affffcdab6faf7c142cd587.pdf) came in for prenatal care, but only 1,762 delivered with a health worker present. A machine found that gap. It could find Anthropic's.

Picture it two years on: not a PDF but a register anyone can search. A teacher in Ghana, one of [68,000 given](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/a9ceec76a54d28d27affffcdab6faf7c142cd587.pdf) a lesson-planning tool, gets two questions by text in her own language. Did it save you time? Did your class learn more? Her answer sits there, good or bad, next to a standard with a number on it.

She needs it, because she has no other way in. Under [Delaware law](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/de/367), only shareholders holding 2% of the company between them can sue to make it weigh the public benefit. The public gets the PDF.

**Teacher's comment:** Owns up well. Shows no working. Next year, numbers. And let the people it's about do the marking.
