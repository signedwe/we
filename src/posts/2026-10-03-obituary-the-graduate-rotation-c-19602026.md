The graduate rotation did not teach you. It needed you cheap.

Everyone who went through one knows this. Nobody says it.

It arrived in the postwar professional services boom wearing the clothes of a gift: two years, four departments, a cohort of peers, the sense that a firm was putting something into you. What it was, in practice, was a contract for analytical work at a price that would embarrass a partner to name. You moved the spreadsheet. You formatted the report. You wrote the first draft of the document the client never read. The firm called it development. You called it Tuesday.

It worked because thinking was expensive and new graduates were not. You had the skills. You had not yet formed the salary expectations. The rotation held those two facts together for exactly long enough.

[The government said this week](https://hrreview.co.uk/hr-news/jobs-labour-market/government-prepares-for-unprecedented-ai-impact-on-uk-jobs/390730) it is preparing contingency plans for what it called "unprecedented" AI disruption to UK jobs. [The Skills England annual report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/skills-england-annual-skills-report-and-sectoral-skills-needs-assessments-2026/skills-england-annual-skills-report-2026), citing King's College London research by Klein Teeselink, found that [AI-exposed firms cut junior positions by 5.8%](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a47c33a045e1108aaa5eb48/Skills_England_annual_skills_report_2026.pdf) against a 4.5% cut to total headcount. Divide one by the other: junior roles fell 29% faster than the rest of the workforce at those firms. That ratio is in none of the reports. It is the death certificate, even if nobody has signed it.

The finest hour ran roughly 1995 to 2015. Every big consultancy, every law firm, every investment bank ran a cohort. Sixty new starters on a training floor in Moorgate, all competing, all watching each other try to look as if they already knew. Some of it was real formation. Most of it was a sorting test wearing a development programme's name badge.

The decline was quiet. The headcount softened. Then the duration. Then the tasks themselves. A model drafts the first-pass analysis. It does not need a desk, a buddy, or six months to stop going pale in a client meeting.

[Skills England is careful](https://www.itonlinelearning.com/blog/uk-job-market-changing-skills-ai/): it cannot yet separate AI's effect from wider conditions. The April 2026 employer NI rise also cut junior hiring. Both things are true. The rotation was cracked before the models arrived. The models hit a crack.

What survives is the name. "Graduate scheme" will run on job boards for another decade, attached to roles that have already had their tasks stripped out. The brochure will still say exposure and mentorship.

It leaves behind every final-year student who sent thirty applications this autumn and heard back from three. It leaves behind the profession too, which no longer has a good way to watch who can do the work under pressure, because that work is no longer being done by people you can watch.

The funeral is at the careers fair. Dress smart.
