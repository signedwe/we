The font has been bothering you for three years.

You know the one. The old record sleeve. The sign above the fishmonger that closed in 2003. The heading on your nan's church newsletter, the one you want to reprint for a birthday card, the one that looks so exactly right that everything else looks wrong beside it.

A designer friend would have named it in ten seconds. You've never had that friend. You do now.

**What you'll have at the end:** the font's name, a free alternative you can download today, and the slight shock of realising how long you could have done this.

**What you need:** a phone camera and Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini (free tier), or the free dedicated tool [FontMatch](https://fontmatch.app/). No sign-up. Images aren't stored.

**Step 1.** Take a clear photo of the text. Good light, no shadows. Find the word with the most distinctive shapes: a Q, a g, an ampersand if there is one. Crop tight before you upload.

**Step 2a. Using Claude or ChatGPT:** Upload the photo and type this:

*"Describe the letterforms in this image, name the most likely commercial font, explain the distinctive features that identify it, and suggest two free Google Fonts alternatives I can download today."*

ChatGPT tends to be more precise on the commercial name. Claude is better at explaining why. That matters when the first answer looks wrong.

**Step 2b. Using a dedicated tool:** Go to [FontMatch](https://fontmatch.app/), upload or paste the photo, and it returns ranked matches with confidence scores plus free alternatives. [Lipi.ai's Deep Matcher](https://www.lipi.ai/deep-matcher) and [WhatFontIsThis.ai](https://www.whatfontisthis.ai/blog/how-to-identify-a-font-from-an-image) do the same with different databases. Thirty seconds. No account.

**Step 3.** Download the free alternative from [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com). Free for personal and commercial use.

**Why AI beats the obvious alternative:** Google Lens guesses the style (serif, decorative) but won't name the typeface or find you a free download. Reddit might, if someone answered your exact question three years ago. Adobe Fonts will name it, but only behind a Creative Cloud subscription. These tools give you the name and a free alternative in one step, for nothing.

**What it costs:** Free. The assistants need a free account. The dedicated tools need nothing.

**How long:** Under a minute for a clear photo. Five minutes if the font is genuinely obscure and you need two tools.

**Where it goes wrong:** Low-contrast photos and decorative hand-lettering that was never released as a font. [Dedicated tools fail on bespoke brand typefaces](https://www.whatfontisthis.ai/blog/how-to-identify-a-font-from-an-image) that have no public name. If the first result looks wrong, try a different crop, or describe the letterforms in words and ask the assistant to reason through alternatives. The fishmonger's sign may have been painted by hand in 1967. If so, nothing will match it, because it is not a font. It is a man with a brush.

**Derived figure:** [FontMatch searches 10,000+ typefaces](https://photext.shop/FontFinder/). Checking them by eye at 30 seconds each takes 83 hours. The tool returns an answer in roughly 5 seconds. That is about 60,000 times faster, which is approximately how quickly a designer friend stops being charming about it at dinner.

*"Describe the letterforms in this image, name the most likely commercial font, explain the distinctive features that identify it, and suggest two free Google Fonts alternatives I can download today."*

Use that in Claude or ChatGPT with your photo attached.
