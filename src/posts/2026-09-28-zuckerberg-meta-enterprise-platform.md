When two robots haggle, it matters who built both.

Today Mark Zuckerberg announced [Meta Enterprise Platform](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/launching-meta-enterprise-platform/amp/), "the next major pillar of our business", to bring "the Muse agent, Meta Business Agent, Muse API, Muse Code, and more" to businesses. He hired [MongoDB's chief executive](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/launching-meta-enterprise-platform/amp/) to run it.

Seven weeks ago the same man published [The Future is for Everyone](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/08/the-future-is-for-everyone/). It promised a personal agent "that understands you, your goals, and everything you care about", and "a balance of power that favors individuals". [Muse is already designed to notice your spare cash, compare rates and act for you](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/metas-muse-just-put-banks-170657037.html). Now the bank, the shop and the landlord can buy Meta's agent too.

Picture Tuesday afternoon in 2029. Your Muse wants a cheaper broadband deal. It opens a chat with the provider's Business Agent. Two pieces of software work out what you'll pay. Both were trained, hosted and updated by one company, which also sells the provider its adverts. You'll never see the conversation. You'll see a price.

This has happened before, with people instead of code. An estate agent who acts for both buyer and seller knows the buyer's ceiling and the seller's floor, and is paid when the deal closes. [Eight American states, Texas and Florida among them, make that illegal](https://www.homelight.com/blog/dual-agency-is-illegal-in-some-states/). British brokers used to be paid by the market makers who filled their clients' share orders. [In 2012 the regulator said the practice creates "a clear conflict of interest between the clients of the firm and the firm itself"](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/finalised-guidance/fg12-13.pdf), and not readily compatible with its rules. Referees don't coach.

Every one of those rules exists because a person in the middle could see both hands of cards. An agent in the middle doesn't just see them. It holds them.

The fair case for Meta is simple. One firm on both sides might make deals faster and cheaper for everyone, and no one has shown it tilting anything. Fine. But the other trades didn't wait for proof of cheating. They put rules on the seat, because cheating in that seat is invisible by design.

So here's the rule the announcement is missing. An agent must say, before it negotiates, who built the agent across the table. And where it's the same company, the buyer's agent must be one the buyer can swap for another maker's, with the same memory, on the same afternoon.

Zuckerberg's essay said there's "no such thing as a singular benevolent superintelligence". He was right. There's no such thing as a benevolent double agent either.
