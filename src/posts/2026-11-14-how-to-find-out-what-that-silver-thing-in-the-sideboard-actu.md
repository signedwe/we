Your aunt left you a sugar bowl. Heavy, slightly tarnished, with a row of tiny stamps on the base. The charity shop offered three pounds. Don't take it yet.

[British silver hallmarks have been legally required since 1478.](https://antiquesilverhallmarks.com/blog/silver-date-letter-hallmarks/) Four small punches, [each answering a different question: what the metal is, where it was tested, when, and who made it.](https://www.anyident.app/silver-hallmark-identifier) A five-hundred-year paper trail, pressed into the bottom of a teapot.

The problem is reading it. [The marks are typically 1.8 to 3 millimetres tall.](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/best-apps-to-identify-silver-hallmarks/) A book helps. You don't have one. A dealer helps. They're buying, not advising. AI, used in two steps, is now a reasonable shortcut.

**Step one: photograph the marks.**

Turn the piece over. Hallmarks sit on the base of a teapot or bowl, on the back of a spoon handle, or inside a ring shank. Get your phone as close as it will focus and still hold sharp. Then angle a lamp across the marks rather than shining it straight on. [Side light throws the stamped impressions into relief.](https://www.anyident.app/silver-hallmark-identifier) Take the whole row in one frame. The sequence matters.

**Step two: use a dedicated hallmark app, not ChatGPT.**

This is where the tools split. ChatGPT and Claude can explain what hallmarks mean. Reading them from a photo is different. [An independent test of twelve pieces found ChatGPT 5 correct on five and partial on four.](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/best-apps-to-identify-silver-hallmarks/) Purpose-built apps that pre-process the image do better. The same test found [dedicated apps returning a full correct identification on eleven of twelve pieces.](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/best-apps-to-identify-silver-hallmarks/) Try [925s.ai](https://925s.ai/appraise/silver-hallmark-identifier/) or the Antique Identifier App, free tier on iOS and Android. Both have free tiers. So do Claude and ChatGPT.

Upload your photo. It should come back with something like: anchor (Birmingham), lion passant (sterling), a date letter, maker's initials.

**Step three: paste what it found into Claude or ChatGPT.**

*Something like: "I have a Birmingham hallmarked sterling silver piece from [year]. The maker's mark appears to be [initials]. What do you know about silversmiths registered in Birmingham then with those initials? What might a piece like this sell for at auction versus a dealer?"*

That's what general AI does well: cross-referencing maker records, explaining why the Birmingham anchor differs from the London leopard's head, telling you that [from January 1975 all UK assay offices unified their date letter system](https://925s.ai/blog/how-to-read-british-silver-hallmarks/), so pre-1975 pieces need the office identified first and post-1975 pieces are straightforward.

**How long:** under twenty minutes for a clear piece.

**Where it goes wrong:** [worn or heavily polished marks drop accuracy below 50 percent.](https://antiquesilverhallmarks.com/blog/ai-silver-hallmark-identifier-accuracy/) If the stamps have been polished flat over decades, neither app nor human can read them from a photo. You need a specialist with a loupe and raking light. And [AI cannot detect forgeries. A cast copy of a genuine mark reads as genuine to pattern-matching software.](https://rockidentifier.io/silver-hallmark-identifier/) For anything that might be worth real money, get a human appraiser.

The charity shop buys at melt weight. The lion passant on your sugar bowl means somebody with a silver-spot calculator is not what you need first. Try the app, find the maker, then decide.
