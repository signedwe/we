The sourdough took thirty hours. It now sits on the board like a dropped brick. You cut it open and find tunnels at the top and wet clay at the bottom.

By the end of this you'll have three suspects, with the likeliest first, and one change to make on Sunday.

**Why not use a chart?** Free ones exist, and [this crumb chart](https://sourdougharchive.com/sourdough-crumb-guide/) is a good one. One slice doesn't point at one cause, though. Tunnels over a dense, gummy base have a name, [fool's crumb](https://sourdougharchive.com/sourdough-crumb-guide/), and that guide says the loaf is underproofed. Another guide says a [badly overproofed loaf can show the same holes over a gummy bottom](https://sourdougharchive.com/overproofed-vs-underproofed-sourdough/). A third says gummy can mean [underbaked](https://sourdoughstarter.com/blogs/troubleshooting/fix-gummy-sourdough-crumb), or cut too soon. A chart picks one. A chat can ask what you did.

**Steps**

1. **Let the loaf cool for at least two hours before you cut it.** [This troubleshooting table](https://jayarrbread.com/blog/bread-troubleshooting-guide) says so. If you've already cut it, say so in step 4.
2. **Take three photos in daylight:** the cut slice, the underside, the outside. Claude [asks for images of 1000 by 1000 pixels or more](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241126-upload-files-to-claude). Add them with the + button. In ChatGPT it's [+, then Add photos & files](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8400551). Image inputs work on [free and paid plans, within usage limits](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8400551-chatgpt-image-inputs-faq). Keep the photos the right way up, because OpenAI warns the model [may misread rotated images](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8400551).
3. **Paste this:**

*Here are three photos of my sourdough. Don't diagnose yet. Ask me five questions about what I did. Then give me three possible causes, likeliest first, and tell me what in the photos would settle it.*

4. **Answer like a witness.** How much the dough grew. Whether it [sprang back when poked](https://loaflovers.com/overproofed-vs-underproofed-sourdough-how-to-tell/). How long it baked. How long before you cut. How warm your kitchen was.
5. **Change one thing and bake again.** Go back to the same chat with the new photo. One baker who [tried this workflow](https://thehousemenu.substack.com/p/how-i-used-ai-to-improve-my-sourdough) says to tell it you're repeating the recipe and ask for adjustments.

**Cost:** nothing on a free plan, plus a loaf's worth of flour. **Time:** ten minutes, then one bake.

**Where it goes wrong.** Anthropic's docs say Claude [may make mistakes on low-quality or rotated images](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/vision). OpenAI says its model [may describe images wrongly](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8400551). A photo can't show your hydration, or how lively your starter was. If the assistant names a cause without asking you anything, it's guessing from a picture. Ask it for the other two suspects. The guides disagree among themselves, too. One says an overproofed crumb is [usually dry and coarse, not gummy](https://sourdoughstarter.com/blogs/troubleshooting/fix-gummy-sourdough-crumb). Another says it [can be gummy](https://flourwise.com/blog/why-is-my-sourdough-dense/).

WE has read all this and baked nothing. The page disclaimer applies, and nothing here is a promise.

Either verdict is safe to eat. [Overproofed bread is fine to eat](https://sourdougharchive.com/overproofed-vs-underproofed-sourdough/), and a doorstop makes forgiving toast.
