The Chancellor said AI will not "leave workers behind." That phrase needs a second half, and the Budget published on [24 October](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/chancellor-unveils-britains-ambitious-plan-for-ai) doesn't have one.

Studying a thing and doing something about it are two different acts. Everyone sensed this. Nobody wrote it down.

Here is the scene. A researcher at the [AI Economics Institute](https://www.cityam.com/healey-vows-ai-will-not-leave-workers-behind-in-600m-budget-package/) opens a spreadsheet in 2028. She wants to count how many jobs AI cut in financial services during 2026. She can't call Barclays. She can pull vacancy data, but firms cutting through attrition don't post the jobs they chose not to fill. ONS figures lag six months. They don't separate automation from a bad quarter. She has [£23 million over three years](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/chancellor-unveils-britains-ambitious-plan-for-ai) and a team. There's also [£10 million a year](https://defendintelligence.com/en/news/news-le-royaume-uni-prevoit-10-m-par-an-pour-etudier-les-effets-de-l-ia-sur-l-cace4e4f) announced alongside it, but the Treasury hasn't named who gets it, when it starts, or how long it runs. She produces good work. The report lands. Nobody with a legal duty to act on it reads it.

The package puts [roughly £415 million into security](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/chancellor-unveils-britains-ambitious-plan-for-ai): a biosecurity lab, more money for the AI Security Institute, a National Cyber Shield. It puts roughly £33 million over three years into understanding what AI does to employment. That split isn't indefensible. Know before you spend on remedy. But "leaving behind" has to cash out somewhere, and right now there's no named body that reads a bad finding and must do something about it.

By the end of 2028, the AI Economics Institute will have published a report naming real job losses in a specific sector. No UK body will hold a legal duty to respond to that report. If one does by then, this prediction is wrong and the second half of the promise turned up after all.

[A government assessment published in January 2026](https://fish4news.co.uk/chancellor-621m-uk-ai-investment-plan/) found sharper hiring falls in occupations highly exposed to AI: admin, customer services, parts of professional work. Those workers didn't get more of the £621 million. They got the part of it that counts what happened to them.

The researcher closes the laptop. The data holds up. Her phone sits quiet on the desk.
