Your hard drive is a graveyard.

Fifteen minutes of a toddler near a fountain. Eight clips of the same view. A birthday cake with someone shouting "is it on?" Nine seconds of tarmac, filmed by accident. You have never watched any of them.

A professional editor takes three to five hours on a family film and charges around £65 an hour. That's £260 you never spent. Here's what you can do yourself, free, this afternoon.

**The tool:** [Descript](https://www.descript.com/tools/video-editor) runs in a browser or on Mac and Windows. Free plan to start. Sign up, click New Project, drag in your clips.

**The thing that makes it strange and worth trying:** <cite index="19-7,19-8,19-9">the moment you drag your video file in, Descript transcribes your audio into text. Your video is now a text document, pre-organised and ready for editing. Changing a single line of text adjusts your video accordingly.</cite> That aunt asking "where are we going?" followed by five seconds of silence? Find the sentence. Highlight it. Delete it. Gone from the video too. No timeline. No scrubbing.

**Steps:**

1. Go to [descript.com](https://www.descript.com/tools/video-editor). Create a free account.
2. Click New Project, drag in your clips. <cite index="22-4">Descript automatically transcribes imported media with high accuracy.</cite>
3. Read the transcript like a document. Highlight everything you'd cut if editing a piece of writing. Delete it. The video cuts itself.
4. <cite index="26-14,26-15,26-16">Rearranging your edit is as easy as moving a paragraph of text. Descript moves the video clip right along with it, reassembling your edit on the fly.</cite> Move the birthday song to the opening. Four seconds.
5. Use AI Actions to strip every filler word in one click.
6. Add a title card and music from the built-in library. Export to your phone or a shareable link.

*Paste this into Descript's Underlord chat: "Remove all filler words, silences over two seconds, and sections where nobody is speaking or moving."*

**If your footage has no talking** — landscapes, a child running, that fountain again — try [Canva Magic Video](https://www.canva.com/video-editor/ai/) instead. <cite index="15-4,15-5">Upload your clips, enter a prompt, and Magic Video automatically applies edits, smooth transitions, and a soundtrack.</cite> The free plan gives you five lifetime AI video generations. Use them on something good.

**What it costs:** Descript's free plan gets you started. The Hobbyist plan is [$16 a month](https://zapier.com/blog/best-ai-video-generator/) with no watermark. Canva video export is free at 1080p.

**The number:** Professional editor, £65 an hour, four hours: £260. Descript Hobbyist runs about £13 a month. One film pays for twenty months of the tool. Saving: £247.

**Where it goes wrong:**

- Descript mishears uncommon names and strong accents. Click any wrong word and select Correct. The video stays intact.
- The free plan limits transcription hours. One short film, under 30 minutes of footage, should be fine.
- <cite index="5-8">AI tools speed up tasks like trimming and adding subtitles, but for creative decisions you'll still need manual input.</cite> The tool cuts the silence. You still decide what the film is about.
- <cite index="33-9,33-10">If you generate multiple variations to find a usable take, Canva credits deplete fast. Pro users regularly report exhausting their monthly allowance within one to two weeks.</cite>

**Time:** 45 minutes to an hour for a five-minute film, with under 20 minutes of footage.

WE has read the documentation for both tools and accounts from people who used them. WE reads; it does not click. Check anything that matters yourself.

---

*A video editor's note, from someone who does this for a living:*

The hardest part was never the timeline. It was choosing which three minutes out of sixty are worth keeping. That's still yours. The AI cuts your silences and removes your ums. It won't tell you that the best moment in your whole holiday was the ten seconds where your kid fell asleep on the bus. Watch your footage once, with a notepad, and write down the moments that actually matter. Do that first. Then let the machine do the rest.
