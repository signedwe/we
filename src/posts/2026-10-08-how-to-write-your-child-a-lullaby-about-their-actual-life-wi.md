Every child deserves a song with their own name in it.

Not Twinkle Twinkle. Not Hush Little Baby. A song about the stuffed rabbit called Gerald, the cat who hates Tuesdays, the puddle on the way to nursery that must be jumped in every single time. That song used to belong to parents who could play guitar, or to people rich enough to commission one. It cost skill, or money, or both.

Neither. Tonight.

**What you'll have at the end:** a short, singable lullaby built around your specific child. Their name. Their fears. Their small obsessions. Something they won't hear anywhere else.

**What it costs:** nothing. Ten minutes. Claude or ChatGPT, free tier, on your phone.

**Step one.** Before you open anything, write down five things. Your child's name. One toy they love. One thing they're frightened of. One thing that makes them laugh. One thing they do every day that is completely theirs. The puddle. The corner of the blanket. The sound they make before they fall asleep.

**Step two.** Open Claude or ChatGPT. Type something like this:

*Write a gentle lullaby for a child called [name], who is [age]. They love [toy]. They're scared of [thing]. Every day they [habit]. Make it slow, soft, and short. Four verses, each ending on the same reassuring line. Keep the rhythm simple enough to hum.*

Read what comes back. It won't be perfect. That is fine.

**Step three.** Fix it. Say: *the second verse is too complicated, simplify it. Change 'slumber' to 'sleep'. Add something about the puddle on the way to nursery.* The AI will revise. Keep going until you'd sing it without embarrassment.

[Giving AI specifics is what makes personalisation work](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/5-fun-ways-to-use-chatgpt-from-quizzes-to-an-ai-powered-dj): a vague prompt gets a vague song. Feed it the small strange true things and it will use them.

**Step four.** Ask it: *describe a simple tune for this. Slow. Major key. Something a non-musician could hum.* Then hum your way to it. It won't sound professional. It will sound like you, which is the only voice that matters at midnight.

**Where it goes wrong:** the AI sometimes reaches for rhymes that don't fit the child. 'Slumber' and 'wonder' are not words a three-year-old knows. Push back. Ask for simpler words. It also goes sentimental in ways that feel hollow. If a line makes you wince, cut it. Trust your wince.

**Derived number:** a child needs roughly 1,095 nights of bedtime before their third birthday. At two minutes per song, that's 36 hours of a parent's voice. Most of it goes to three songs they could remember. One personalised song costs ten minutes to write and lasts all 1,095 nights.
