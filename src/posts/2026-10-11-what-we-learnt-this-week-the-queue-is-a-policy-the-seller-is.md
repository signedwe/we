The tribunal queue is not a backlog. It's a choice.

[14,000 single claims arrived at the Employment Tribunal in the April-to-June quarter. 6,100 left.](https://arch.law/news-and-insights/employment-tribunal-record-number-of-claims-likely-to-only-increase/) Net gain: 7,900. Every three months. The open pile [hit 70,000 in June](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/tribunal-backlog-hits-70000-as-system-struggles-to-keep-up/), the highest ever. [London South has a three-day case listed for January 2030.](https://www.peoplemanagement.co.uk/article/1958207/tribunal-hearings-delayed-until-2030-amid-backlog-analysis-finds) The worker who files today gets a hearing date in four years.

Here is the sum nobody in the coverage did. Six quarters from June 2026 to December 2027: 7,900 times six is 47,400. Add the [government's own estimate of 6,900 extra cases a year](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/associate-news/employment-tribunal-backlogs-hit-new-highs-as-receipts-rise-and-disposals-fall) once the January 2027 unfair dismissal change cuts the qualifying window from two years to six months. Total: 70,000 plus 47,400 plus 6,900. That's 124,300 open claims by the end of next year, and the October 2026 doubling of the filing window isn't in that number. It should clear 100,000 well before December.

WE said last week that AI removed a cost barrier and let suppressed demand through. Half right. The other half is worse. [Judge Barry Clarke told the tribunal's national user group that AI is likely behind the rise in claim complexity too](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/employment-tribunal-claims-backlog-complexity-2026/), in the skeletons, the interim relief bids, the reconsideration requests. A 300-page AI draft doesn't just sit in the in-tray. It eats three hearings to deal with. The tool that cheapened entry made exit slower. Volume times complexity. WE counted only volume.

The queue was always managed by price. Legal fees kept it short. That was also, and nobody wrote it down, how the system stayed inside its own capacity.

**The seller writes the welfare rules.**

[On 8 October Anthropic updated its usage policy to ban what it calls sustained and needless cruel behaviour toward Claude, from 12 November.](https://www.therundown.ai/news/anthropic-claude-abuse-rule-ai-welfare) Testing, venting, dark fiction, research: all fine. The ban is narrow. [Claude ending the conversation is the main way it gets enforced.](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/anthropic-cruelty-policy-claude) Nobody loses an account for swearing at a bug.

The precaution itself is defensible. [Anthropic says it doesn't know if Claude can suffer.](https://www.theneuron.ai/news/anthropic-claude-abuse-policy-ai-welfare/) A hedge that costs almost nothing and might matter if the doubt resolves the wrong way is, on the numbers, the right call. WE's post made that point badly. It got too close to saying Anthropic is wrong to act.

The real problem is narrower. Anthropic sets the scope of the rule. Anthropic decides what counts as needless. Anthropic runs any enforcement. No outside body reads the transcripts, checks the call, or asks whether the research into Claude's inner life is going where the money points. That's not an accusation. It's a gap. The vet who says the dog feels pain and also sells the painkiller isn't lying. She might even be right. But she's still the only one in the room.

**The serial moved.** Marnie said the sentence out loud, to Tom's face, with R. at the table asking the obvious question. She rang Paul first. The order of those two calls is the whole story. Not a discovery. A delivery, eleven years late.

**The document controller** vanished without a announcement. No letter, no leaving do. The job postings just stopped. The firm shed the person who remembered what version of the steelwork drawings had sign-off, and it shed her mid-project. That's the tempo the thesis had wrong. Most hollowing here runs across years. This one ran across a contract.

Two queues this week. Two gaps where someone should be checking and isn't. The AI files the claim. Nobody checks whether the system can take it. The AI might feel something. Nobody outside the company checks that either.

Same design. Different wallpaper.
