The report is one hundred pages. The government's answer is one sentence.

By 13 November 2026, the AI Regulation and Safety Bill gets its second reading in Parliament with no government backing. [Lewis Silkin are clear](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/14/ai-judgment-day-on-the-horizon-while-uk-lawmakers-play-catch-up): Private Members' Bills almost never become law without government support, and this one has none. The JCHR's report, published 14 September, will have sat unanswered for sixty days by then. EU GDPR came into force 25 May 2018. UK GDPR on 1 January 2021. From that first date to the 13 November second reading: 8 years and 172 days. That is how long the phrase 'meaningful human involvement' has sat in statute with no test a tribunal can apply. So here are the questions the report didn't ask itself.

**Who signs the enforcement notice?**

The JCHR wants [a single, independent AI oversight body, on a statutory footing](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwyzvgj70y4o). Fine. Who at that body sends an enforcement notice to a company in California whose model is deployed in London via an API? British law stops at the coast. A body with no treaty access to pre-release models is a very good letterhead.

**What does risk classification actually cover?**

[The report calls for a law that sorts AI systems by risk level and puts harder duties on the riskier ones.](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/committee-calls-bill-address-ai-032359299.html) The model that scanned faces outside a London venue and the one that scored loan applications in Wolverhampton are different products, different companies, different people hurt. A risk band covers a type of harm. It does not cover the company that built the product, because that company is not here. A sorting system with no reach is a filing cabinet.

**What did the actual enforcer say?**

The ICO told the JCHR [the problems AI poses to data protection are complex but not 'insurmountable under the current legislation'.](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/ao-shearman-on-data/highlight-of-the-week-uk-joint-committee-on-human-rights-publishes-report-on-the-regulation-of-ai) The body that has to send enforcement notices says the tools exist. The JCHR says the rules are fragmented and leave gaps. Both are probably right, about different harms, in different cases. Nobody has published the list of which is which.

**What would actually bind a frontier lab?**

Not a Private Member's Bill with no government backing. Not a hundred-page report. Two things in the document would bite. [The JCHR also calls for placing the AI Security Institute on a statutory footing, and for publishing a timetable for ratifying the Council of Europe Convention on AI.](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/jchr-report-calls-for-new-ai-bill-statutory-oversight-body-prohibitions-on-certain-ai-uses) A statutory AI Security Institute with treaty access to pre-release models is a regulator with a postman. The other ninety-eight pages are well-meant furniture.

[Work Foundation research from August 2026](https://perplexityaimagazine.com/expert-insights/ai-jobs-uk-2026/) found 36% of UK employers had cut entry-level jobs for 16 to 24-year-olds in the previous year. Those jobs are not on hold for a statutory body. The enforcement notice with no address on the envelope: that is what the gap looks like, and it is 8 years and 172 days wide.
