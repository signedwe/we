By February 2027, when the Bank's next report lands, watch whether it separates this number or keeps it whole. If it stays whole, every policy built on it will keep describing a hiring freeze as a productivity win.

Here's the number. The Bank of England's November report says UK firms think AI will lift their productivity by 0.9 points a year for the next three years. [That figure is split roughly equally between faster output and slower hiring](https://ppc.land/ai-accounts-for-nearly-a-fifth-of-uk-growth-in-2026-bank-of-england-finds/). Half is making more. Half is taking on fewer people.

Both halves lift output per worker. That's how the maths works. But they're not the same thing, and the report runs them as one.

Here's Box D of that report in the form it deserved.

---

**Finance director:** 0.9 points a year. That's the AI gain.

**Bank economist:** That's what your firms told our survey. Half from faster output. Half from hiring less.

**Finance director:** Hiring less still lifts the ratio.

**Bank economist:** It does. Same output, fewer people, ratio goes up.

**Finance director:** So we get more productive.

**Bank economist:** You do the same work with fewer people on the payroll.

**Finance director:** That's a productivity gain.

**Bank economist:** Yes. It's not the same as getting faster. You didn't find a quicker way. You just stopped hiring.

**Finance director:** The number goes up either way.

**Bank economist:** The number goes up. The person who didn't get the junior role doesn't.

---

The word does it. "Productivity" holds two things: making more, and cutting staff. [The Bank found close to zero AI effect on jobs so far](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-06-boe-ai-slowing-hiring-not-firing/), but firms told its survey they plan to take half the future gain through slower hiring. That plan goes into the same box as the output gain.

Neither the Bank nor its Decision Maker Panel hides this. It's in the footnote. But a single number goes into rate decisions and spending reviews as proof AI is working. It is working. Not for everyone it's working on.

The past figure is clean. [The 0.5% lift over three years](https://completeaitraining.com/news/ai-delivers-just-05-percent-uk-productivity-gain-over-three/) came from real output gains. The forward number adds the headcount half. Two different futures, one clause.

The person who reads that clause at the Treasury doesn't have to pick which one.
