Your flight landed four hours late. The airline gave you a voucher for a sandwich. You said thank you and went home. Under [UK261](https://www.skycop.com/uk-261-flight-delay-cancellation-compensation/), you were owed [£520 per passenger](https://stoneyport.co.uk/flight-delay-compensation-uk/) for a long-haul delay of four hours or more.

The law has been there since Brexit, almost unchanged from the EU original. [£220 for short flights under 1,500km, £350 for medium, £520 for long-haul delays of four or more hours](https://stoneyport.co.uk/flight-delay-compensation-uk/). Per person. Nothing to do with what the ticket cost. [A family of four on a long-haul flight delayed five hours could be owed £2,080](https://stoneyport.co.uk/flight-delay-compensation-uk/). [You have up to six years to claim](https://stoneyport.co.uk/flight-delay-compensation-uk/). The claims companies who advertise on the page where you found this take [25 to 36 per cent of your money](https://tripbuffer.com/delayed-flight-compensation-uk/). You don't need them.

Here's what AI does that a search can't. It reads your situation. Your delay, your route, the reason the airline gave. It tells you what you're owed, writes a letter with the right number and the right regulation cited, and drafts the escalation when they say no.

**What you'll have at the end:** a finished letter, ready to send, citing UK261 by name and the correct pound figure. Twenty minutes.

**Before you start:** grab your booking reference, flight number, departure and arrival airports, the date, and how late you arrived. Not departed. [UK261 measures delay at your final destination](https://www.skycop.com/uk-261-flight-delay-cancellation-compensation/), not the gate.

---

**Step 1.** Open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. Free tier works. New chat.

**Step 2.** Paste this in and fill the brackets.

*"I had a flight from [departure airport] to [arrival airport] on [date], flight number [X], with [airline]. It arrived approximately [X] hours late at my final destination. The airline's explanation was [what they told you]. I booked directly. Please: (a) confirm whether I'm owed compensation under UK261, (b) state the exact amount, and (c) write me a claim letter I can send today."*

**Step 3.** The assistant drafts a letter citing UK Regulation 261/2004 and the fixed sum. Before you send it, ask the assistant to explain the extraordinary circumstances defence. Weather, air traffic control and security events can let airlines off. Ask whether the reason you were given falls inside it. It won't give you a legal opinion, but it'll tell you whether the reason looks contestable.

**Step 4.** [Send the letter directly to the airline's passenger rights address](https://nationalexecutivetransfers.co.uk/uk261-flight-compensation-rules-explained/), on their own website. No claims company. [Quoting the exact delay and the exact pound figure tends to get a faster response than a general complaint](https://stoneyport.co.uk/flight-delay-compensation-uk/).

**Step 5.** If they refuse: paste the refusal into the assistant and ask for a draft response. Then ask whether to escalate to [CEDR](https://www.cedr.com/consumer/aviation/), the Civil Aviation Authority's approved dispute resolution scheme. It's free, and binding on the airline.

---

**Cost:** nothing.

**Time:** twenty minutes to write. Airlines have up to eight weeks to respond before you can escalate.

**Where it goes wrong:** AI is reliable on the fixed amounts and the structure of the claim. It's weaker on whether a specific delay cause counts as extraordinary. Airlines know that's where passengers give up. If they're refusing on factual grounds about what caused the delay, Citizens Advice or a free consumer solicitor appointment is better than a fourth draft from Claude.

[Compensation is never paid automatically](https://nationalexecutivetransfers.co.uk/uk261-flight-compensation-rules-explained/). You have to claim it. Most people don't.
