The knowledge of how to blend a perfume was always the product. Not the oils. The oils are cheap.

A bespoke consultation meant sitting with someone who knew which notes fight each other and which ones settle. They'd ask what you wanted to smell like. Then you'd wait. Then a heavy bottle would arrive. That knowledge, which families of scent pair well, which top note fades in twenty minutes and which base note lingers till midnight, stayed locked behind the perfumer's door.

AI can't smell. It will tell you that itself. But it knows, in considerable detail, [how top, middle and base notes combine](https://flowersandherbs.com/essential-oil-guides/top-middle-base-notes-essential-oils/), which oils complement each other, and how to turn "cold October morning, calm not sweet" into a recipe you can mix this afternoon. That's most of the job.

**What you need.** Three to five essential oils from a health shop. Jojoba oil as your carrier. A small dark glass roller bottle. Total cost depends on what you already own. Bergamot, lavender and cedarwood together come to well under £15 from most UK health shops, and you only need a few drops of each.

**Step 1: Give the AI a specific brief.** Open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini, free tier. Don't ask for a generic perfume. Describe a memory or a mood.

*Try: "Make me a perfume from essential oils. It should smell like a cold morning in the countryside. Calm, not sweet. Give me specific oils, their note level, and drop counts for a [10ml roller bottle at roughly 10% dilution](https://aromaenergy.co.uk/blogs/essential-oils/essential-oil-blending-for-beginners-safe-simple-recipes-pro-tips)."*

It will return a structured recipe. It understands [the 30-50-20 rule](https://aromamonk.com/blog/crafting-a-signature-fragrance-with-essential-oils-a-perfumers-guide/): roughly 30% top notes, 50% middle, 20% base. It knows bergamot opens things up. It knows cedarwood anchors them.

**Step 2: Push back.** Don't take the first recipe. Ask: "The lavender is dominant. I want it warmer. What changes?" A YouTube tutorial gives you one fixed formula. The assistant adjusts to what you actually want. [That's the part no video does.](https://www.perfumeprojects.com/resources/ai-01.shtml)

**Step 3: Mix small first.** Five drops total into a few millilitres of jojoba. On five drops, [the 30-50-20 split](https://livelyliving.com.au/blogs/blog/designing-your-signature-scent-with-essential-oil-blends) gives you roughly 1-2 top, 2-3 middle, 1 base. Let it sit ten minutes before sniffing. The top note screams at first. The others are still waking up.

**Step 4: Report back.** Tell the assistant what you got. "The cedarwood is exactly right. The geranium is too loud. Next step?" Iteration. Real perfumers do dozens of rounds. You can do four in an hour.

**Step 5: Make the full bottle.** Once the ratio works, scale up. [1ml of essential oils in 9ml jojoba](https://aromaenergy.co.uk/blogs/essential-oils/essential-oil-blending-for-beginners-safe-simple-recipes-pro-tips) is your roller bottle. Let it rest 48 hours. Notes settle. Something awkward often rounds out.

**Where it goes wrong.** AI confidently invents combinations it cannot verify will smell good. [It has no nose.](https://fraterworks.com/blogs/information/ai-and-perfumery-a-primer) Trust yours over its confidence. Some essential oils irritate skin. [Check dilution guidance](https://aromaenergy.co.uk/blogs/essential-oils/essential-oil-blending-for-beginners-safe-simple-recipes-pro-tips) before anything touches skin. Never apply undiluted citrus oils before sunlight.

Twenty minutes with the AI. Forty with the oils. Two days waiting. You end up with something that has never existed before.

That used to need an appointment.
