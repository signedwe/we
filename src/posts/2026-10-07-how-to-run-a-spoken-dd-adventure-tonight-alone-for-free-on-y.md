A friend cancels at 9pm on a Friday. You had a campaign planned.

Here's what you have at the end of this: a live, spoken Dungeons and Dragons session running on your phone, with a Dungeon Master who voices the innkeeper differently from the wizard, reacts when you interrupt, and does not cancel. It costs nothing. You need the free ChatGPT app and a physical die.

The trick is ChatGPT's voice mode, which [since July 2026 runs on GPT-Live](https://theaicareerlab.com/blog/chatgpt-gpt-live-voice-mode-2026). It listens and speaks at the same time, so you can cut it off mid-sentence the way you'd cut off a person. [Free accounts get GPT-Live-1 mini automatically](https://knightli.com/en/2026/07/12/gpt-live-chatgpt-voice-guide-settings-limits/). No settings to change.

**Step one: set up in text first.**

Open ChatGPT and paste this into the text box before you touch the voice button:

*You are my Dungeon Master for a solo D&D 5e campaign. Setting: a fog-covered port town, something wrong in the harbour. My character: a level 3 human fighter called Marsh, cautious, owes a debt to someone dangerous. Narrate in present tense, voice each NPC distinctly, describe what I see and hear rather than telling me what to do. When I need to roll, tell me what die and what number beats it. Stay in character until I say PAUSE.*

[That prompt takes about two minutes](https://lore-keeper.com/blog/chatgpt-dungeon-master) and almost entirely decides the quality of what follows.

**Step two: switch to voice.**

[Tap the waveform icon](https://www.engadget.com/2230975/how-to-use-chatgpt-voice-mode/) at the bottom right of the ChatGPT composer on your phone. Grant microphone access if asked. A floating orb appears. Start talking.

*Marsh walks down to the harbour and approaches the first dockworker he sees.*

**Step three: pick a voice.**

On first launch you'll be offered a choice of voices. Pick the one that sounds least like a podcast host.

**Step four: keep a die nearby.**

The model [won't track hit points or spell slots reliably](https://lore-keeper.com/blog/chatgpt-dungeon-master) across a long session. It narrates rather than runs a rules engine. When it calls for a roll, roll a physical d20 and say the number aloud. That's the whole workflow.

**Where it goes wrong:**

- A cough or a stray "yeah" [can cut it off mid-sentence](https://www.qwe.edu.pl/tutorial/gpt-live-tutorial/). Say "keep going" and it resumes.
- [The model forgets campaign details](https://wilds.ai/blog/play-dnd-solo-with-ai) after a long session. Start a fresh chat for each new adventure.
- It doesn't track combat state. Track hit points on a scrap of paper.
- [Free tier voice has time limits](https://coursiv.io/blog/chatgpt-voice-mode). If it cuts out, wait a few minutes or continue in text.

**The number nobody prints:**

[OpenAI says 150 million people use ChatGPT voice weekly](https://techjournal.org/what-is-gpt-live-chatgpt-voice). [Tabletop Arc puts global TTRPG players at roughly 50 million](https://tabletoparc.com/resources/best-ai-for-dungeons-and-dragons-2026). Surveys suggest around 15 per cent struggle to play regularly because they can't find a DM. That's 7.5 million players and 150 million voice users, with almost no overlap. The gap is not the technology. It's that nobody told them to paste a prompt first.

**What it costs:** Free. A physical die if you want one. Half an hour.
