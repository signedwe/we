Most magic tricks are simpler than they look and harder than they sound.

The instructions are everywhere. The problem is the instructions are written for people who already have the feel for it, and every tutorial assumes you'll just get it after watching twice. You won't. You'll watch it seventeen times and still drop the card.

What was missing was a patient teacher. One who would answer the same question eleven times, watch you describe what went wrong, suggest a smaller version of the move, and not sigh. That teacher now exists and costs nothing.

A private magic tutor charges [£40 to £80 an hour](https://www.bark.com/en/gb/magic-lessons/). The AI version charges nothing and will stay up until 2am going through the same pinky break with you until your fingers understand it. Three hours of that, at the [National Living Wage of £12.21](https://www.british-business-bank.co.uk/business-guidance/guidance-articles/staffing/national-living-wage-and-minimum-wage), is £36.63 in labour you no longer have to spend on someone else's schedule.

**What you'll have at the end:** one trick you can perform tonight. Not a shuffling flourish. Something with a reveal. Something that makes a person say: how did you do that.

**Step 1.** Pick one trick. Not a category. One specific trick. Good beginner options: the 21-card trick, the double lift, the French drop (coin vanish). Ask Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini: *"Teach me the French drop coin vanish. Assume I have never done any sleight of hand. Start with the grip before anything else."*

**Step 2.** Do not watch a video. Read the explanation. Then try it with a coin. Then come back and describe exactly what went wrong.

*Try this:* *"I'm holding the coin between thumb and first finger of my right hand. When I go to pretend-take it with my left hand, the coin falls. What am I doing wrong?"*

The AI will diagnose it. It will suggest gripping lower, or slowing the left hand, or dropping the right hand faster. Keep going like this.

**Step 3.** Ask for the psychology. The trick is half mechanics, half misdirection. Ask: *"Where should the spectator be looking when I drop the coin, and how do I make sure that's where they're looking?"* That answer is the part no YouTube tutorial includes.

**Step 4.** Perform it badly to someone you live with. Ask them what they saw. Report back to the AI. Iterate.

**Step 5.** Perform it to someone who doesn't live with you. That's the trick done.

**Where it goes wrong:** [AI cannot see your hands](https://quizquiz.co/blog/using-ai-to-generate-pub-quiz-questions). It diagnoses from your description, which means your description has to be precise. "It doesn't look right" gets you nothing. "My left hand moves before my right hand releases" gets you a fix. Also: one trick. People who try to learn three tricks learn none.

**What it costs:** Nothing. One coin.

**How long:** Two hours of practice, spread over an evening. Possibly less.
