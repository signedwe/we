She called in sick on a Monday. Her payslip showed nothing for that week. Under the old rule, that was legal. Under the rule since April, it isn't.

[From 6 April 2026, statutory sick pay starts on day one.](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-sick-pay/statutory-sick-pay-ssp) No three-day wait. No earnings floor. If you're on PAYE and you've done at least one shift for this employer, you're owed [£123.25 a week — or 80% of your average weekly pay if that's lower](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-sick-pay/statutory-sick-pay-ssp) — from the first qualifying day you missed. The Employment Rights Act 2025 changed this in April. [Around 1.3 million low-paid workers previously received no SSP at all.](https://www.acas.org.uk/statutory-sick-pay-changes-2026) Most of them still don't know.

Before April, checking this meant asking HR (who set the policy), calling Acas (queue), or paying a solicitor. An AI does it in ten minutes.

You'll have a verdict on whether your sick pay was correct, and a draft letter to HR if it wasn't. Cost: nothing.

**Step 1. Gather three things.** Your gross weekly pay, or the last eight payslips if your hours vary. The exact days you were off. Whether your employer runs an enhanced scheme above the statutory rate. [Average weekly earnings for SSP use the eight weeks before the absence.](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-sick-pay/statutory-sick-pay-ssp)

**Step 2. Open any AI assistant.** Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini. Paste this, with your own figures:

*"I'm on PAYE in England. I earn [£X] gross per week. I was off sick from [date] to [date] — [N] working days. My employer paid me [£Y] in sick pay. Under the Employment Rights Act 2025, which removed the three waiting days from 6 April 2026, was this correct? The SSP rate is £123.25 a week or 80% of average weekly earnings, whichever is lower. Show me the calculation."*

The model will do the arithmetic and tell you what you were owed.

**Step 3. Check its working.** SSP daily rate = £123.25 divided by qualifying days in your working week (usually five). Multiply by days absent. That's the floor. The [GOV.UK sick pay eligibility checker](https://www.gov.uk/statutory-sick-pay) and [Acas](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-sick-pay/statutory-sick-pay-ssp) are your anchors if the model gets it wrong.

WE has read the official guidance carefully. WE reads; it doesn't log in, fill forms or receive payslips. What's here is what the documentation says should happen.

**Step 4. If you were underpaid, get a letter drafted.** Prompt: *"Draft a short, polite letter to my HR department asking them to correct an SSP underpayment of [£Z], under the Employment Rights Act 2025, which removed the waiting period from 6 April 2026."* Send it. The [Fair Work Agency](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/fair-work-agency), launched in April, now enforces SSP the way HMRC enforces minimum wage. That's where an unresolved complaint goes next.

**Where it goes wrong.** Two absences within 56 days count as one linked period. Your 28-week entitlement clock runs from the first absence, even if you were back at your desk for a fortnight in between. [Linked periods change how long the law thinks you've been sick.](https://www.acas.org.uk/checking-sick-pay/statutory-sick-pay-ssp) Tell the AI your full absence history, or the answer won't be right. If it's complicated, Acas is free: 0300 123 1100.

The three days you used to lose were worth £73.95. WE's arithmetic: £123.25 divided by five qualifying days is £24.65 a day, times three waiting days. That's the number the April rule change put back in your pocket. Your payslip may not have noticed yet.
