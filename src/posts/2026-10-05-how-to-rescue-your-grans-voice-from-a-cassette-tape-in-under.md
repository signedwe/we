Somewhere in your parents' house there's a box of cassettes. One of them has your gran's voice on it.

That tape is dying. [Magnetic signal on cassette tape degrades 30–40% over 20–30 years](https://wefixsound.com/blog/how-to-restore-old-cassette-tape-recordings). A recording from 1990 is 36 years old. At the midpoint of that loss range, you've already lost 35% of the signal. Roughly 65 units remain in every hundred. Still recoverable. Not for much longer.

This used to be a professional job. A specialist, a studio, a wait. Now it's a Sunday afternoon and a free browser tool.

By the end of this, you'll have a clean digital file. Good enough to hear. Good enough to share.

**What you need**

- A working cassette player (or a [USB cassette converter, £15–£50](https://www.lalal.ai/blog/how-to-repair-old-cassette-recordings/))
- A computer
- [Audacity](https://www.audacityteam.org/) — free, for Mac, Windows, Linux
- A free account at [podcast.adobe.com/enhance](https://podcast.adobe.com/enhance)

**Step 1: Get the tape onto your computer**

Plug your USB converter in. Open Audacity. Set the input to your USB device. Press record in Audacity, then press play on the tape.

[It records in real time. A 60-minute tape takes 60 minutes.](https://heirloom.cloud/blogs/blog/how-to-digitize-cassette-tapes-step-by-step-guide) Make tea.

[If you have an old tape deck, connect it to your computer using a standard audio cable.](https://memorymurals.com/journal/how-to-digitize-audio-cassettes) Same process. Export the recording from Audacity as MP3 or WAV.

**Step 2: Let Adobe fix it**

*Go to podcast.adobe.com/enhance, drag in your file, click Enhance Speech, download.*

[Adobe Podcast Enhance Speech is free for files under 30 minutes, browser-based, nothing to install.](https://www.clarigital.com/ai-atlas/specialist-tools/voice-audio/adobe-podcast/) It strips background hiss, tape noise, room echo. [It's trained on thousands of hours of clean studio audio](https://www.aitoolcurator.com/ai-tools/audio/adobe-podcast/) and rebuilds the voice track against that standard.

[The free tier costs nothing and processes up to one hour of audio per day.](https://dominikgabor.com/blog/free-ai-voice-enhancer-adobe-podcast.html) For a tape over 30 minutes, split it first. In Audacity: File, Export, Export Multiple.

**Where it goes wrong**

[The enhancement can produce a slightly over-processed, robotic tone on already-clean recordings.](https://www.aitoolcurator.com/ai-tools/audio/adobe-podcast/) It's a rescue tool, not a polish. If the result sounds hollow, compare it to the original. Pick the one you can bear to listen to.

[Free-tier users get no strength slider.](https://aiunpacking.com/review/adobe-podcast/) You're at the default, all the way up. If it sounds overcooked, that's what you have on the free tier.

If the tape squeals badly, [it likely has sticky-shed syndrome](https://wefixsound.com/blog/how-to-restore-old-cassette-tape-recordings). Don't play it again. That one needs a specialist.

One thing the post doesn't flag: the digitising step is where most rescues fail. [Dirty heads cause more bad transfers than tape degradation does](https://wefixsound.com/blog/how-to-restore-old-cassette-tape-recordings). Wipe them with 99% isopropyl alcohol before you start. A cotton swab and thirty seconds. That saves the recording more reliably than any AI tool.

**What it costs**

Free, if you already own a player. A USB converter runs [£15–£50](https://www.lalal.ai/blog/how-to-repair-old-cassette-recordings/). [Adobe Podcast's Enhance Speech feature is free, with no account required for basic use.](https://www.buildfastwithai.com/ai-tools/adobe-podcast)
