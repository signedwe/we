Sam heard it every morning through the kitchen window. Sharp, repetitive, slightly annoying. Three years she'd assumed it was a pigeon.

It was a great tit. She found out in forty seconds.

Here's what you'll have at the end: a name for every bird singing near you right now, plus enough to talk about them at dinner.

**Step 1. Download [Merlin Bird ID](https://merlin.allaboutbirds.org/download/).** Free, available on iPhone and Android. Made by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, which runs the world's largest database of bird sightings.

**Step 2. Add a Bird Pack for your region.** Open the app, tap Explore Birds, then Download Bird Packs. It suggests the right one. For Britain, grab Europe. About a minute on wifi.

**Step 3. Tap Sound ID, then the big green button.** Go outside, or open a window. Hold the phone up. The app draws a live picture of the sound and drops names onto it as it recognises each bird. A blackbird. A robin. Whatever's out there. It [catches several at once](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2021/06/what-bird-singing-merlin-bird-id-app-offers-instant-answers).

*Then try this. Copy it into Claude or ChatGPT: "I just heard a [bird name] in my garden in [your town]. What does it eat, and what is it actually saying?"*

That second step is the one most people skip. Merlin gives you the name. The general assistant tells you why it's doing that, what it's warning off, whether a rival is nearby. The name alone is a label. The label plus the behaviour is a thing you didn't know this morning.

**What it costs:** [Nothing](https://support.ebird.org/en/support/solutions/articles/48000961587-merlin-bird-id-faqs). The Cornell Lab says it will never ask for your credit card. Claude and ChatGPT both run on a free tier.

**How long it takes:** Under two minutes to set up. Leave the recording running. [It goes for ten minutes before asking if you want to continue](https://birdseedandbinoculars.com/wordpress/bird-identification-using-merlin-sound-id/).

**Why AI beats YouTube here:** A tutorial tells you what birds sound like in general. Merlin listens to your actual garden, right now, and names the specific bird making the specific noise outside your specific window. It works [offline too](https://support.ebird.org/en/support/solutions/articles/48001185783-sound-id), so no signal needed.

**Where it goes wrong:** Merlin gets confused by mimics. The common starling does a convincing buzzard. If a result looks exotic, tap the species and play the reference recording back. If it sounds nothing like what you heard, it wasn't.

One number: [Merlin covers over 10,000 species globally](https://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/2023/05/merlin-milestone-app-now-helps-id-birds-worldwide). Britain has roughly 600 recorded species. That's about 16 times more coverage than you'll ever need standing in a British garden. The limiting factor is never the software. It's whether you went outside.
