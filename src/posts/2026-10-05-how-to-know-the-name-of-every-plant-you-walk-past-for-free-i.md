The hedge you walked past every day for twenty years has a name. Today you can know it.

Not the hedge. Every individual plant in it. The white-flowered thing that comes out in May. The spiky one. The one that smells like almonds and is probably toxic. All of them.

For most of human history this was a specialist skill. You needed a field guide, patience, and the kind of attention that botanists develop over years of crouching in ditches. [Over 1,600 native flowering plants grow across Britain](https://gardenuk.co.uk/plants/wildflower-identification-guide-uk/), but most walkers ever meet about forty of them. That means 97.5 per cent of Britain's native flowering plants are invisible to most people. Not because they're rare. Because naming things used to be a job.

Now it takes your phone and a minute.

**What you'll have at the end:** the name, the story, and for hedges, the age. That last one is the surprising bit.

**Step 1.** Open [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com) or Gemini on your phone. Free tier works. [ChatGPT's image upload button is the paperclip icon in the prompt box](https://www.ai-toolbox.co/chatgpt-management-and-productivity/chatgpt-vision-image-analysis-guide-2026). Gemini has the same. Claude does not currently analyse uploaded photos in the same way, so use one of the other two for this.

**Step 2.** Stop at a plant. Photograph the leaves and the flowers separately if you can. Bright light. One plant filling the frame. The more distinctive the feature, the better the result.

**Step 3.** Upload the photo and type:

*"What plant is this? Give me the common name, the Latin name, and one thing about it that most people don't know."*

The answer comes back in seconds. The almond-smell one in the hedge is likely cherry laurel, *Prunus laurocerasus*, introduced from south-east Europe in the sixteenth century. It produces hydrogen cyanide when crushed. Your walk just got more interesting.

**Step 4.** For the hedge itself, try this:

*"I can see the following woody plants in a 30-metre stretch of hedge: [list them]. How old is this hedge likely to be?"*

[Country folklore holds that each woody species in a 30-metre section represents roughly 100 years of age](https://www.countryfile.com/wildlife/trees-plants/hedgerow-plants-flowers-guide). The AI knows this rule and will apply it. A hedge with hawthorn, blackthorn, elder, field maple and dog rose is probably 500 years old. The same lane your car takes every Tuesday.

**Where it goes wrong.** General AI assistants are less accurate than specialist tools. [One comparison found GPT-4 wrong on its top-guess species 36 per cent of the time, versus 8 per cent for a dedicated plant model](https://www.kindwise.com/post/the-plant-identification-battle-gpt-4-vs-plant-id). For common plants on a familiar walk, fine. For anything you're uncertain about, cross-check with [PlantID](https://plantid.online/en) or the [Woodland Trust's guides](https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/trees-woods-and-wildlife/plants/wild-flowers/). And [never eat a plant based only on an app result](https://lensapp.io/plant-identifier/). That rule costs nothing.

**The free upgrade.** After you've named a few, ask the AI to write you a five-minute route description of your usual walk, noting what should be in flower right now. A walk you've done a hundred times becomes a different walk.

[Hedgerows host over 600 plant species and are the main habitat for 47 species of conservation concern in the UK](https://www.field-studies-council.org/shop/publications/hedgerows-guide/). Most people walk past them looking at their phones. Which is fine, as long as you're looking at the hedge.
