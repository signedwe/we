The document controller was not the person who knew things. She was the person who knew where things were.

Born around 1970, when construction projects grew too large for one foreman to hold in his head. Site drawings multiplied. Contracts split into sub-contracts. Specifications changed and had to be tracked. Someone had to sit at the intersection of all of it, stamp the revision letters, log the transmittals, chase the returns. That person got a job title. The job title got a salary band.

At its peak the role ran every large UK building project. The document controller held the drawing register, the RFI log, the submittal tracker. She knew which version of the steelwork drawings the contractor had signed off. She knew which consultant still owed a response and how many days past the contractual deadline they were. Without her, the site met the wrong spec and nobody found out until the concrete was poured.

By the end of 2028, at least one major UK contractor will report its document control headcount at a third of its 2022 level while handling the same project volume. If headcount holds, I was wrong.

The cause of death: AI document processing, deployed across UK construction in 2025 and 2026. [London Construction Magazine reported in October 2026](https://www.constructionmagazine.uk/2026/10/uk-construction-jobs-ai-replace-before-2030.html) that document control, junior estimating and procurement administration faced the sharpest automation pressure of any office function on a building site. The AI reads the drawing, logs the revision, flags the discrepancy, chases the return. It does not take a lunch break. It does not forget which consultant it was waiting on.

Here is the sum. A senior document controller cost around £38,000 a year in 2024. A mid-size UK contractor running ten live projects might carry eight of them. That is £304,000 a year in salaries, plus on-costs of roughly 25 per cent: £380,000 total. AI document management software runs at well under £50,000 a year for an estate that size. The saving is £330,000. One firm. Ten projects. No redundancy announcement, because you do not replace the people. You just stop posting the vacancies when they leave.

That last sentence is the whole story. [Nobody Was Made Redundant](https://economy.ac/review/2026/09/202609295608) is the title of a September 2026 analysis of AI's hidden effect on UK hiring: assignments stop, postings vanish, the role empties without a press release.

Refutation: [construction AI research from October 2026](https://www.constructionmagazine.uk/2026/10/uk-construction-jobs-ai-replace-before-2030.html) notes that senior quantity surveyors, engineers and site managers are not exposed in the same way. The document controller died. The person who reads what the document means did not. So the argument holds for the role and not for the profession.

Survivors: the contracts manager who relied on her to know what he'd signed. The subcontractor who rang her instead of reading the spec. The junior who was going to learn the project by sitting next to her for six months.

Funeral arrangements: the role is still on the org chart of every firm that has not yet deployed the software. The software is already sold. The two facts will meet sometime next quarter, and there will be no announcement.
