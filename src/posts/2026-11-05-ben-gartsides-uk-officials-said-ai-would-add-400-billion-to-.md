The government built its AI policy on a number that Google paid for. Here's what the reporting doesn't quite say.

In a written parliamentary answer reported by multiple outlets this week, AI minister Kanishka Narayan put the government's own estimate at £120 billion by 2030, a range of £55 billion to £140 billion. [The £400 billion figure came from a 2023 report commissioned by Google](https://www.superpowerdaily.com/posts/uk-minister-contrasts-120-billion-ai-estimate-with-google-funded-400-billion-figure) and produced by the consultancy Public First. This is the first time the government put both numbers side by side. It did not withdraw the £400 billion. It did not say it was wrong.

**1. The number wasn't cited. It was used to build things.**

This wasn't a speech. [Skills England's GOV.UK press release on AI apprenticeships](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/ai-apprenticeship-to-close-digital-skills-gap-holding-back-millions-of-workers) states that "AI has the potential to add £400 billion to the economy by 2030" as the reason for a new training programme. [A second Skills England press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/help-for-uk-businesses-to-fill-400bn-ai-skills-gap) is titled 'Help for UK businesses to fill £400bn AI skills gap'. The number became the floor under a workforce strategy. That is not rhetoric. That is a budget rationale.

**2. The government had the smaller number and kept using the bigger one.**

[Public bodies used the £400 billion figure for almost two years](https://www.superpowerdaily.com/posts/uk-minister-contrasts-120-billion-ai-estimate-with-google-funded-400-billion-figure), including Skills England four months after ministers had the lower internal estimate. The gap between what officials knew and what official documents said was months, not days.

**3. When you don't name the sponsor, you're not repeating their finding. You're endorsing it.**

Public First's report was public. Nobody hid it. But a GOV.UK press release citing £400 billion reads as the government's own view. The citation makes the sponsor's number the state's number. That is what a citation does.

**4. Lord Holmes asked in January 2025. Nobody answered.**

[A Conservative peer asked ministers where the £400 billion figure came from](https://themondonews.com/technology/ai-could-add-400-billion-to-the-uk-economy-british-officials-say-estimate-draws-mainly-on-google-data/) in January 2025 and got no reply. The question had an answer. The answer was in writing. It sat there for twenty-one months.

**5. The minister called his own lower number "highly uncertain".**

[Narayan described the £55 billion to £140 billion range as "hugely uncertain"](https://www.superpowerdaily.com/posts/uk-minister-contrasts-120-billion-ai-estimate-with-google-funded-400-billion-figure). Fair. But £400 billion, which assumes much faster adoption, titled government press releases for two years with no such qualifier. The uncertainty went on the honest number. The confident headline went on the borrowed one.

The apprenticeship is real. The training places are real. The rationale behind them was always a Google contractor's optimistic scenario. That's on GOV.UK. It just took a parliamentary question to get anyone to look.
