The best cookbook in Britain is in a shoebox. It's written in biro, it's splashed with gravy, and one card just says: Nan's scones, the usual but hotter.

By Sunday night you can have that box as a printed book, her handwriting on the left page and a version anyone can follow on the right. A phone, a free app, an AI that reads joined-up writing, and about an hour for twenty cards.

**Step 1. Photograph the cards without the shine.** Old cards are glossy, greasy or both. [PhotoScan, Google's free scanning app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.apps.photos.scanner&hl=en_US), makes you point the phone at four dots in turn. [It takes several pictures from different angles and keeps the clearest bit of each](https://research.google/blog/photoscan-taking-glare-free-pictures-of-pictures/), so the glare falls out. Do both sides. The back is where she wrote "don't tell your aunt".

**Step 2. Let the machine read her writing.** Upload each photo to ChatGPT or Claude. [One family historian did exactly this with old handwritten records in both](https://familylocket.com/transcribing-document-images-with-chatgpt-and-claude/). Tell it what it's looking at. Then paste this:

*This is my grandmother's handwritten recipe card from about 1970. Transcribe it exactly as written, spelling and all. Where you can't read a word, write [?] and don't guess. Then, separately, write a modern version with metric weights and a fan oven temperature, and list every word you weren't sure of.*

**Step 3. Check every number against the card.** This step isn't optional. [That same historian found ChatGPT read a 9 as a 5, and Claude added a detail that wasn't on the page at all](https://familylocket.com/transcribing-document-images-with-chatgpt-and-claude/). On a recipe, that's the difference between scones and a doorstop. Read the transcript with the card in your other hand.

**Step 4. Sort out the oven.** Most of these were written for gas. [Gas mark 4 is 180C, or 160C in a fan oven; gas mark 6 is 200C, or 180C fan](https://www.which.co.uk/reviews/built-in-ovens/article/oven-temperature-conversion-table-aA5Ol9b157On). "The usual, but hotter" you'll have to work out with whoever was in the kitchen.

**Step 5. Ask the family for the bits that aren't written down.** Send the transcripts round the group chat. Ask one question per card: who made it, when, and what went wrong the time it went wrong. Paste the answers into the AI and ask for two lines of story to sit under each recipe. That's the bit people will actually read.

**Step 6. Print it.** [Blurb has free cookbook templates](https://www.blurb.co.uk/cookbooks). Put the card photo on the left page and the tidy version on the right. [A small square softcover starts at £11, plus 21p a page over the base](https://www.blurb.co.uk/pricing). Order one per branch of the family and watch the arguments start.

**Where it goes wrong.** Old measures. A "cup" might be her teacup. "Moderate oven" isn't a number. The AI will quietly convert these into something confident and wrong. Keep her words and put your guess in brackets. And it'll smooth out her spelling unless you tell it not to, which is how you lose the card that says "custerd".

**Who this replaces.** Somebody who typed it all up one Christmas and never finished. Probably you.
