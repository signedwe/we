That carved face above the church door has been staring at you for eight hundred years. You've walked past it a hundred times. You still don't know what it is.

Bring your phone. You're about to find out.

By the end of this afternoon you'll know the difference between a gargoyle and a grotesque, what a Green Man is doing on a Norman arch, and roughly what century the tracery in that east window was carved. No guidebook. No specialist.

**What you'll need**

A phone with Claude or ChatGPT on it. Both have a free tier that handles photo uploads. An old church. [Britain has about 16,000 medieval churches still standing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_(building)), so you're probably within twenty minutes of one.

**Why this beats YouTube**

A video about Gothic architecture covers everything and your church in particular. AI looks at the specific carved face above your specific door and answers about that one.

**Step one.** Walk close. Pick one carved detail: a face on a corner, a figure on the tower, a pattern in a window. Photograph it from about a metre away, in good light, without zoom.

**Step two.** Open Claude or ChatGPT. Upload the photo using the paperclip icon.

**Step three.** Ask:

*"This is a carved stone detail on an old church in Britain. What is it called, what did it represent to the people who made it, and roughly when was it carved?"*

**Step four.** Follow up with: *"Could this be Victorian restoration rather than original medieval work?"*

This matters. Thousands of British churches were restored in the nineteenth century by architects who used the same motifs as the medieval carvers. [The AI won't distinguish Victorian from medieval without prompting.](https://www.buildinglores.com/blog/app-to-identify-buildings)

**Step five.** Point the camera at a window. Ask which period of Gothic the tracery represents. There are three: Early English (narrow pointed lancets, plain), Decorated (flowing curves, branching), Perpendicular (vertical panel grids, strong horizontals). They span roughly 1200 to 1540, with overlaps. The AI will date the style.

**Step six.** Ask about the tower. Battlements, pinnacles, corner figures: all datable. What does this design tell you about when that part of the building went up?

**A note on gargoyles**

[A gargoyle carries water.](https://cathedral.org/discover/art-architecture/gothic-architecture-101/) It's a carved drainpipe. The mouth is a spout and there's a channel cut through the stone. What most people call a gargoyle is actually a grotesque. Purely decorative, no water function at all. In Somerset, the decorative carved figures on church tower corners have their own name: [hunky punks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunky_punk). The AI will tell you which is which. This is not a small distinction. It's the difference between a drain and a joke, made in stone, eight hundred years ago.

**Where it goes wrong**

Victorian restoration carvings used identical designs to medieval originals. [Even experts find them hard to separate.](https://www.buildinglores.com/blog/app-to-identify-buildings) AI is stronger on Perpendicular Gothic than on Decorated, so early fourteenth-century work gets the least certain answers. Take two photos: one tight on the detail, one showing the surrounding wall. If the answer sounds very certain, ask it to show its working.

**Cost:** nothing on the standard tier of both Claude and ChatGPT, as of November 2026. Tools change, so check.

**Time:** twenty minutes in the churchyard, then as long as the conversation holds you.

The vicar was the last person who knew what the carved face above the door meant. AI is now the second.
