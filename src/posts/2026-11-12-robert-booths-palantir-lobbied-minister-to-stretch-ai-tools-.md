By the end of 2027, the debate over whether Parliament needs to pass a law before GP records can go into the NHS Federated Data Platform will have happened in a Commons committee. If it hasn't, the exclusion held by GP non-consent alone, and nobody in Parliament noticed the difference.

The contract said GP data was out of scope. Everyone treated that as a decision. What it actually was: a promise the government made to doctors because it couldn't deliver the data anyway.

When the previous government [signed a £330 million deal with Palantir in 2023](https://finwire.io/news/world-news/revealed-palantir-lobbied-minister-to-stretch-ai-tools-reach-to-nhs-gp-records) to run the NHS Federated Data Platform, it announced GP records would not be part of it. Critics called that a win. The clause went into the press release and the parliamentary reassurances.

Foxglove obtained WhatsApp messages under the Freedom of Information Act. The Guardian reported on 9 November that those messages [show Palantir pressing then-health minister Zubir Ahmed](https://www.nationaltechnology.co.uk/Palantir_lobbied_minister_to_expand_AI_reach_into_GP_records.php) to bring GP records in anyway. The company said ["the logic of joining up primary and secondary care data is unarguable"](https://www.nationaltechnology.co.uk/Palantir_lobbied_minister_to_expand_AI_reach_into_GP_records.php) and urged Ahmed to ["get out ahead of"](https://sharedsapience.substack.com/p/the-century-report-november-10-2026) opposition from doctors.

Every account frames the lobbying as the problem. The odder fact sits one level down.

[England has around 6,170 GP practices](https://businessmodelanalyst.com/palantir-nhs-gp-records-lobbying-law/). Each controls its own patient records. So far, five of them feed the platform. Five. The government excluded GP data from the contract not because a clause stopped Palantir wanting it. It excluded GP data because it couldn't get GPs to hand it over. The clause was a press release built around something the government couldn't do.

**A civil servant. A GP. This week.**

*Civil servant:* The contract says GP data is out of scope.

*GP:* Yes. That's why we didn't opt in.

*Civil servant:* So you're protected.

*GP:* We protected ourselves. Refusing to sign is what held the line. Not the clause.

*Civil servant:* Surely the clause stops ministers from just saying yes?

*GP:* Read the messages. The clause is a political promise the previous government made when it couldn't get the data anyway. Palantir is asking if the politics have changed. I'd rather it hadn't happened over WhatsApp.

*Civil servant:* What actually stops this?

*GP:* [A change in the law](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-10-palantir-lobbied-minister-nhs-gp-records/), according to the NHS chief data officers' network. And 6,165 practices still not signing up.

---

The [BMA has warned](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-10-palantir-lobbied-minister-nhs-gp-records/) that GP records contain identifiable personal data and that bringing them in could break patient trust. That's the right objection. But what's holding the door shut is GP practices not consenting. The clause is a description of that fact, not the cause of it.

Palantir asked a minister whether the politics had shifted. That's a legal thing to do. The honest answer to that question should come from a Commons committee, not from a WhatsApp thread that only surfaced because Foxglove filed a Freedom of Information request.
