The company building the machine that may take your job now says it will set money aside for you. [Anthropic announced on Sunday](https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund) that it will put 5% of its shares into a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, subject to a shareholder vote. Then the board can add another 2% for every 50% rise in the company's value above a starting point.

That second part is the clever bit. Tech firms usually hand out more shares to bosses as the price climbs. Here the escalator runs the other way. The bigger the company gets, the bigger the slice set aside for everyone else.

What will the money do? Anthropic says the fund might make large gifts to charities, or pay money straight to particular groups of people. Its first example is work. AI could create huge wealth and still knock many people's jobs out from under them. The company [has already put $200 million](https://www.anthropic.com/news/public-benefit-fund) into studying that, and says bolder steps may be needed.

Add in what Anthropic's founders and staff have pledged personally, and the company says roughly a fifth of its shares will be dedicated to the public good. For comparison, the nonprofit that controls OpenAI [holds 26% of the company](https://openai.com/our-structure), with a warrant for more if the share price rises tenfold over fifteen years. The rich labs are starting to compete on how much of themselves they give away. That is new, and it is good.

Picture where this goes. A bookkeeper in Leeds loses half her clients to software in 2028. One morning a payment lands from a fund owned by a company in San Francisco, paid for by the same machines that took the work. That's a dividend from automation, arriving in an ordinary current account. People have argued for something like it for a century. It might arrive through a company's share register rather than a government.

Here is the snag. The fund is set up as a limited company wholly owned by Anthropic. It is not a charity, by design, so it can switch between kinds of giving. The rules on when the stake grows will be published later. So the company that causes the disruption also decides who counts as disrupted, how much they get, and when. However kind the people running it are, that is charity, not a share. Charity can be withdrawn by the next board.

WE's proposal: if the fund pays people whose work AI has taken, give those people seats on whatever decides the payments. Not a token seat. Enough to block a change to the rules. Publish who got what and why, and let anyone refused appeal to someone outside the company. The fund's stake already grows with the company. So should the say of the people it answers to.
