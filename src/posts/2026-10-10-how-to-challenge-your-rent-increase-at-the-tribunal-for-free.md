Most tenants pay the increase. One in 1,436 fights it.

That ratio is not a measure of how often landlords get the rent right. It's a measure of how many tenants know what changed in May.

[Since 1 May 2026](https://england.shelter.org.uk/professional_resources/news_and_updates/challenging_a_rent_increase_the_rules_and_whats_changing), the First-tier Tribunal cannot set your rent above what your landlord asked for. Before that, it could. That single fact was the whole deterrent. It's gone.

[73% of determined rents came in below the landlord's notice figure](https://lonres.com/public/section-13-rent-increases-what-200-tribunal-decisions-reveal-lonres/). The application costs nothing in England. [Between 2024 and 2025, only 1,532 tenants applied](https://flatfinder.online/blog/challenge-a-london-rent-rise-section-13-and-tribunal) across the whole country. England has [11 million private renters](https://rentverify.co.uk/about). If 20% face a proposed increase in any year, that's 2.2 million eligible. Only 1,532 applied. One in 1,436.

The gap is preparation, not law. Here's the preparation.

---

**Step 1: Check the notice is even valid (10 minutes)**

[Since 1 May 2026, your landlord must use Form 4A](https://www.landlordstudio.com/uk-blog/fair-rent-increase-uk). That's the private tenancy version. [Form 4 is the social housing version](https://www.landlordstudio.com/uk-blog/fair-rent-increase-uk). Serving the wrong one voids the increase entirely. [A defective notice has no legal effect. Your rent doesn't go up at all.](https://www.startmyclaim.ai/renters-rights/check-my-notice)

Go to [Notice Doctor](https://www.startmyclaim.ai/renters-rights/check-my-notice). Free. Five questions. Tells you whether yours is valid.

Or paste your notice into Claude or ChatGPT:

*"This is a Section 13 notice for a private assured periodic tenancy in England, served after 1 May 2026. Check: does it use Form 4A not Form 4? Does it give at least two months' notice? Is the start date the first day of a rental period? Has rent been increased in the last 52 weeks? List any failures."*

The AI works from what you paste. Misread the form number and it will too.

---

**Step 2: Build your evidence (30 minutes)**

The tribunal wants achieved rents, not asking prices. [Where one party showed actual agreed rents and the other showed listings, the tribunal chose the achieved rents.](https://theindependentlandlord.com/ftt-challenge/)

Go to Rightmove or Zoopla. Filter for recently let. Same postcode district, same bedrooms, same type. Screenshot three to five. Then check the [ONS Price Index of Private Rents](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/privaterentandhouseprices/latest) for your region.

Then:

*"My current rent is £[X]. The proposed rent is £[Y] from [date]. Here are five comparable lettings: [paste details]. The ONS puts annual private rent inflation in [your region] at [figure]%. Write a one-page statement explaining whether the proposed rent is above or below market. Cite each comparable. Plain English."*

[You pay the old rent until the tribunal decides. No backdated arrears.](https://rentctrl.co.uk/blog/challenging-rent-increase-tribunal/)

---

**Step 3: File (15 minutes)**

Search GOV.UK for "appeal a rent increase". [The application must land before the proposed increase date.](https://www.cmlaw.co.uk/how-to-challenge-rent-increase-tribunal-england-2026/) Miss that and it's over.

Set the deadline alarm before anything else. This kills more challenges than the evidence does.

[Most cases are decided on paper, not at a hearing.](https://www.remedylegal.ai/blog/first-tier-property-tribunal-uk-tenant-guide-2026) Your AI statement goes in the evidence box. [The median was 96 days from application to decision](https://lonres.com/public/section-13-rent-increases-what-200-tribunal-decisions-reveal-lonres/) in a sample of published cases.
