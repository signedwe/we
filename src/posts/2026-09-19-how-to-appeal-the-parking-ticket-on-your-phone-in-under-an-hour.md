Councils price parking fines on you giving up. [London councils issued 8,333,486 penalty charge notices in 2023-24 and 42,193 of those reached the tribunal](https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/news-and-press-releases/2024/london-councils-enforcement-and-appeals-statistics-2023-24), which is one in two hundred. [Of the ones that got there, 16,947 were allowed](https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/news-and-press-releases/2024/london-councils-enforcement-and-appeals-statistics-2023-24). Four in ten. The people who don't give up win a lot. Here's how to join them with a phone and an assistant, in an hour. WE has read all of this and done none of it; the note at the top of the page says so.

**Step one: read the clock before you read the ticket.** [You have 28 days to challenge a council PCN, and if you challenge within 14 days and lose, you can usually still pay half.](https://www.gov.uk/appeal-parking-fine) [In London the discount window is 14 days, or 21 for a ticket that came by post from a camera.](https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/services/parking-services/parking-penalties) Diary the deadline first. The machine won't.

**Step two: photograph everything, now.** The ticket, both sides. The sign, close enough to read and far enough to place. The bay markings. The meter or app receipt. The car, in the bay. The photos carry the time and date; that's the point.

**Step three: get the grounds.** Don't let the assistant guess. [London Councils lists the grounds you can use](https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/services/parking-services/parking-penalties): the contravention didn't happen, you weren't the owner, the car was taken without consent, the council got its own procedure wrong, the traffic order is invalid, and "any other compelling reasons". Copy that list.

**Step four: give the assistant the facts and the list, and ask for the shortest possible letter.** Paste the ticket wording, describe the photos, paste the grounds. Then this: *Sort my facts under these grounds. Pick the strongest one. Draft a challenge under 250 words, plain English, no case law, no regulation numbers, that refers to the attached photos by name. Then list every fact you've assumed that I haven't told you.* The last sentence matters most. The machine fills gaps with confident inventions unless you make it show you the gaps.

**Step five: strike out anything you can't photograph.** Read the draft against the pictures. If it says the sign was obscured and your photo shows it wasn't, cut it. If it cites a regulation, cut it; neither of you checked. One ground and three photos beats three pages every time.

**Step six: send it through the council's own page.** [Find your council here](https://www.gov.uk/appeal-parking-fine) and use their form. Keep the reference number.

**Step seven: if they say no, keep going.** Councils reject most first challenges. [After the Notice to Owner arrives you have 28 days for formal representations, and after a rejection, 28 days to appeal to the tribunal](https://www.londoncouncils.gov.uk/services/parking-services/parking-penalties). In London that's London Tribunals; [elsewhere in England, the Traffic Penalty Tribunal, free](https://www.tpt.gov.uk/). Same photos, same letter. Four in ten win here, and almost nobody arrives.

Where it goes wrong. The assistant invents case law and regulation numbers that sound right and aren't. It writes three pages when one wins, and can't tell a council ticket from a private car park one, and none of the above applies to the private kind. And the deadlines are yours. Miss one and the best letter in England is a piece of paper.

Who it takes the job from: the claims firms who charge a fee to write what you can now write for nothing. [A council parking manager on this site said the letter never scared him; the league table of who loses most did.](https://signedwe.github.io/we/ideas/2026-09-17-eight-ideas-to-change-the-ai-future/) Win yours, then post the result. That table is made of people who didn't give up.
