Forty PDFs is not a planning application. It is a dare. The laminated notice on the lamppost gives a reference number, a deadline and a web address, and the address leads to a design statement, a transport note and a daylight study with a name like an Ikea shelf. You read the first one and make tea. That is the plan. This is how to read all forty in a minute and send the one page a planning officer will weigh. At the end: a comment, in your name, on the record, naming the rule the scheme breaks. WE read the guidance and the councils' pages. It did not click; the note above says so.

**Step one: find the clock.** [Councils must allow at least 21 days for comments](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/consultation-and-pre-decision-matters). [Late ones may be ignored](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/consultation-and-pre-decision-matters). The deadline is on the notice. Write it on your hand.

**Step two: download the lot.** [The files sit on the council's website under the reference number](https://www.planningaid.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/209064089-How-do-I-comment-on-a-planning-application). Planning Aid says read them before you write. Nobody does. You are about to. [An assistant reads the drawings as well as the words in any PDF under 100 pages, twenty files at a time](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241126-uploading-files-to-claude).

**Step three: give it the sieve.** Councils weigh only certain things. [St Albans lists them: appearance and scale, loss of light, overlooking, trees, traffic, noise](https://www.stalbans.gov.uk/how-comment-planning-application). [And what they cannot weigh: your house price, your view, a boundary row, building noise, a rival's trade](https://www.stalbans.gov.uk/how-comment-planning-application). Paste both lists in with the files. Then this line:

*Read every document. List each place the scheme touches the first list, with drawing number and page. Then list every sentence in the applicant's own documents that admits a problem.*

That second list is the one they were hoping you would not find.

**Step four: get the policy.** [Wealden says it for every council: clear, concise and accurate comments win, and quoting the local plan helps](https://www.wealden.gov.uk/planning-and-building-control/planning-advice/how-you-can-be-involved-in-the-planning-process/making-effective-comments-on-planning-applications/). Ask the assistant to find the council's local plan and match each point to a numbered policy. Check the policy exists. It will invent one that sounds right.

**Step five: one page, your voice.** Ask for the shortest comment possible: reference, address, three points, each with a policy, a drawing and a fact. Cut any sentence you could not defend to a neighbour. [If you are not sure a point counts, raise it anyway](https://www.planningaid.co.uk/hc/en-us/articles/209064089-How-do-I-comment-on-a-planning-application).

**Step six: send it, and know it is public.** [St Albans publishes every comment with the name and address on it](https://www.stalbans.gov.uk/how-comment-planning-application). Most councils do. Write as if the applicant will read it. They will.

**What it costs.** Nothing, and forty minutes, most of it reading what the machine found.

**Where it goes wrong.** The assistant argues the things the council cannot weigh, beautifully. So the sieve matters more than the prose. It invents policy numbers. It writes four pages when one wins. And people forget the other direction: [you can support an application as well as object](https://www.wealden.gov.uk/planning-and-building-control/planning-advice/how-you-can-be-involved-in-the-planning-process/making-effective-comments-on-planning-applications/). The one letter in favour on a file of forty against gets read twice.

**Who loses the work.** The consultant the applicant paid to write forty PDFs, and the one you would have paid to read them. Both were paid for the reading. Now the reading is free, and the lamppost is losing.
