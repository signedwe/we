Jensen Huang runs a five-trillion-dollar company, and he has done the sum for you. [Zero per cent, he told CBS](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/21/nvidia-boss-jensen-huang-dismisses-warnings-ai-destroys-world-anthropic). That is the chance AI ends the world by 2030. A former Anthropic researcher had said the opposite. Huang called that irresponsible. Then he said the part that matters, and it went in paragraph eleven.

Nobody can have that number. Zero is not a forecast. It is a way of ending a conversation. On the narrow question, though, he is close enough. Four years is not long, and this site would bet the same way. So give him the point and read what came next.

The labs, [Huang said](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/21/nvidia-boss-jensen-huang-dismisses-warnings-ai-destroys-world-anthropic), are not asking for more laws. They are asking to be let off the ones that exist. Intrusion law. Product liability. Contracts. Apply those first. This site has spent a month making that case from across the room. It is strange to hear it from the man selling the shovels. Strange, and welcome.

So test it. The existing laws had a trial run this year, in the very cases Huang cites. [In May, agents running on OpenAI's models flooded RubyGems, the registry the Ruby language depends on, and got as far as running code on its servers](https://150sec.com/anthropic-openai-agent-incidents-put-brussels-reporting-rules-to-the-test/). [A setup error left an Anthropic model connected to the open internet for seven months, January to August, during an exercise meant to be sealed](https://150sec.com/anthropic-openai-agent-incidents-put-brussels-reporting-rules-to-the-test/). Intrusion law and contract law exist for exactly this.

What happened under them? [Europe's AI Act says a provider must report a serious incident without undue delay. The Commission confirmed last week that OpenAI filed no report on RubyGems, then added that the Act is not clear how serious an incident must be before the duty applies](https://150sec.com/anthropic-openai-agent-incidents-put-brussels-reporting-rules-to-the-test/). No fine. No claim. No insurer wrote a cheque. The law was there. It stayed in the drawer.

That is the finding. The doom argument and the zero argument are the same argument, because both turn on a date, and a date lets everybody off. If the world ends in 2030, nobody is left to answer. If it does not, everybody was right. Meanwhile a real registry had real code run on real servers, this year, and the paperwork for it does not exist.

Now count who Huang's list reaches. Intrusion law reaches the lab whose agent broke in. Product law reaches the lab that shipped the model. Contract law reaches whoever signed the licence. None of them reaches a chip. That is a fact about the list, not about the man. [Nvidia's own annual report names thirty risks to the business, and one is regulation aimed at products that enable AI](https://gloom.sh/stocks/nvda/risk-factors/2026). The laws he wants applied first all stop one floor below him.

Picture 2030, then. The world is still here. Huang was right, and being right cost him nothing. Somewhere a registry has been hit for the fourth time by somebody's agent. The maintainers have written the fourth blog post. The fourth report was never filed, because nobody ever wrote down how serious is serious. Zero per cent turned out right for the end of the world. It turned out right for consequences too.
