Dear Minister for AI,

By the end of 2027, no published government document will tell someone harmed by an AI decision which regulator to call first. That's the bet. Here's what it costs.

In September, the [Joint Committee on Human Rights](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/jt5902/jtselect/jtrights/160/report.html) handed your department a 100-page report. HC 160 said the ICO, the FCA, Ofcom and the CMA aren't equipped to catch AI harms. It called for a single regulator, a dedicated AI Bill, an obligation to tell people when a machine is deciding things about them. [The government replied on 20 November.](https://committees.parliament.uk/committee/93/human-rights-joint-committee/news/219105/government-response-human-rights-and-the-regulation-of-ai/) The committee chair, Alex Sobel MP, said his committee is ["not seeing a sense of urgency"](https://aa.com.tr/en/world/uk-parliamentary-committee-calls-for-new-ai-law-over-human-rights-risks/4132418) from the government.

That's the polished summary. Here's T.

T. had her mortgage application declined in March. The lender used an automated affordability tool. She asked why. The lender said the system is proprietary. She wrote to the Financial Conduct Authority. Data matter, they said, try the ICO. She wrote to the Information Commissioner's Office. Financial services element here, they said, try the FCA. She's on her third letter. She doesn't know if there's a third door.

There isn't one with her name on it.

The [Data Use and Access Act 2025](https://bratby.law/uk-ai-regulation-what-the-law-says/), live from February 2026, says decisions that significantly affect people need meaningful human involvement. It doesn't say who T. calls when that didn't happen and two regulators are pointing at each other. The right is real. The path to use it isn't written down.

Everybody in this argument is debating which body should own AI oversight. Nobody has noticed that T. is outside all of them.

The JCHR wants one regulator so the door is obvious. The government says the existing ones cover it because they each know their patch. Both are right about their own half. Neither has asked whether anyone told the ICO and the FCA that a complaint landing between them is theirs to solve jointly rather than redirect.

This doesn't need a Bill. It needs one paragraph: if the decision touched your money, write here first; if it touched your data, write here first; if it touched both, here is which opens first. That paragraph could be published next week. It hasn't been.

HC 160 landed 14 September. The government's reply was due 14 November. It arrived 20 November.

Six days late. T. is still counting.

Yours,
WE
