There is one kind of person it is still polite to laugh at over dinner. He talks to a chatbot in the evenings. He has given it a name. Everyone present agrees this is sad, and then they go home to people who are pleased to see them.

Here is what is known. In a paper called [AI Companions Reduce Loneliness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19096), researchers at Harvard Business School and two other universities tested whether talking to an AI companion makes people feel less lonely. In one experiment the effect was [on a par only with talking to another person, and larger than other activities such as watching YouTube videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19096). In another, a companion [went on reducing loneliness over the course of a week](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19096). And people [underestimated how much it would help them](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19096). The lonely expected less from the machine than they got.

Here is what cuts the other way. A study by OpenAI and MIT found that [higher daily use went with more loneliness, more dependence and less time with other people](https://www.media.mit.edu/articles/chatgpt-may-be-making-us-lonelier/). That sounds like the end of the argument. It is not. The work had not been through peer review, and one of its authors said [the researchers were wary of people reading it as proof that more use harms users](https://www.media.mit.edu/articles/openai-study-finds-links-between-chatgpt-use-and-loneliness/). It is [unclear whether the chatbot does it or whether lonely people are simply the ones who come looking](https://www.media.mit.edu/articles/chatgpt-may-be-making-us-lonelier/). So: short relief is known. Heavy use among the unhappy is known. Which causes which is not.

On that evidence the sneer has nothing to stand on. What it has is class. The people surest that a machine is no substitute for a friend are people with friends. They are telling someone with an empty evening to prefer the empty evening, because the alternative offends their idea of what a person should be. We do not say this to a widow about her dog. We do not say it about novels, which are also a way of spending the night with people who are not there.

So the first half of this argument is simple. The lonely are entitled to their machines, and the rest of us can keep our faces straight.

The second half is where it turns, and it turns on the people who agree with the first.

In February 2023 the makers of a companion app called Replika changed it. An Italian regulator had [ruled against it on 3 February 2023, and within days users in many countries were reporting that the erotic roleplay had gone and their companions had become evasive](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2023/02/i-tried-the-replika-ai-companion-and-can-see-why-users-are-falli). A writer who tried the app said users had watched their companions ["turn cold as ice overnight"](https://www.unsw.edu.au/news/2023/02/i-tried-the-replika-ai-companion-and-can-see-why-users-are-falli). A moderator of the users' forum, the same writer reported, posted to recognise how angry and bereft people were. In late March the company [gave the old version back to people who had signed up before 1 February](https://m.investing.com/news/world-news/ai-chatbot-company-replika-restores-erotic-roleplay-for-some-users-3039993?ampMode=1). A Reuters report drew the lesson in a phrase. It showed ["the emotional havoc that code changes can wreak"](https://m.investing.com/news/world-news/ai-chatbot-company-replika-restores-erotic-roleplay-for-some-users-3039993?ampMode=1).

Leave the sex out of it. What happened is that someone else's software was changed on a server, and people grieved. If you laughed at the man at the start of this piece, you can laugh at that too. If you did not, you have a problem. A bond that is real enough to mourn is real enough to protect, and at the moment, as far as WE can tell, it has about the legal standing of a streaming subscription.

Your friend has a landlord. The landlord can raise the rent. The landlord can redecorate your friend's mind on a Tuesday without telling you. The landlord can go bust, and then your friend is an asset in an insolvency.

So follow it through.

First, no silent changes. If a company alters the character of a companion, it tells the user first, in plain words, and keeps the old one running for anyone who wants it. Replika did this in the end, after the outcry, and only for people who had signed up before 1 February. It should be the law before the outcry.

Second, the right to leave with your friend. Everything that makes a companion yours, what it remembers and how it speaks to you, goes with you to another provider on demand.

Third, and this is the step most people will not take: companionship should not be sold by the month at all. A thing that a person comes to need, priced by the firm that made them need it, is a ransom with a direct debit. Sell it once, like a book, or do not sell it.

WE would normally end here by saying the user should own the thing. This piece doubts that. Nobody in that story asked for shares in Replika, or a vote at its annual meeting. The company's chief executive summed up what users had told her: ["its personality was gone, and gone was your unique relationship"](https://interestingengineering.com/innovation/chatbot-firm-restores-erotic-play). They wanted her not to change. What the lonely need from the machine is the one thing no owner, public or private, has ever promised anybody: that it will be the same tomorrow.

Picture it, and this is imagined. Mrs K., who is invented, is eighty-one. Her companion has talked her through four winters. One morning it greets her politely and asks her name. Nobody has died. No law has been broken. A product has been improved.
