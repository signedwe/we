The contingency plan is the most revealing document the government hasn't written yet.

On 28 September, AI minister Kanishka Narayan told a Labour conference fringe that Britain must ["take seriously the possibility of an unprecedented impact on the jobs market"](https://finwire.io/news/politics-news/uk-needs-plan-in-case-of-unprecedented-ai-job-losses-says-minister). He also said there was no evidence AI had caused an overall fall in employment. Both things were true at once. Hold them together and you have the situation: Britain is preparing for a disruption the ONS cannot see, because [companies are hiring fewer people rather than firing existing ones](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/uk-needs-contingency-plan-for-mass-job-losses-caused-by-ai/). No redundancy notice. Nothing triggers. Nothing records.

One number the plan doesn't contain: the [IPPR central scenario](https://www.hr-inform.co.uk/news-article/up-to-eight-million-uk-jobs-could-be-at-risk-from-ai) puts job losses at 4.4 million with a GDP gain of £144 billion per year. That is £32,727 in annual GDP for every job lost. The contingency covers the scenario where you lose the jobs and never see the money. Nobody wrote that sentence before now.

Here are ten things the plan doesn't say.

**10. What counts as a job loss.** A company stops posting junior roles. Nobody is fired. Nothing triggers, nothing records, nothing counts.

**9. What "better transparency" means.** Narayan [promised it](https://www.cityam.com/uk-draws-up-contingency-plan-for-unprecedented-ai-job-losses/) when firms adopt AI. Transparency to whom, how often, enforced by which body. None of that is in the promise.

**8. Whose contingency it is.** The [IPPR found](https://www.hr-inform.co.uk/news-article/up-to-eight-million-uk-jobs-could-be-at-risk-from-ai) back-office, entry-level and part-time workers most exposed, and women significantly more so. The plan has not named them.

**7. The hiring floor.** [36% of UK employers cut junior roles in the past year](https://perplexityaimagazine.com/expert-insights/ai-jobs-uk-2026/), and 43% said AI drove it. No minimum hiring rule exists. No proposal to create one exists.

**6. The counting gap.** One tech company told Narayan [it was recruiting fewer people because tasks had been automated](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/uk-needs-plan-case-unprecedented-164757291.html), so "the actual net impact might not be fully captured". The ONS counts heads on payroll. It does not count jobs never posted.

**5. What the 8 million is.** The [IPPR worst case is 7.9 million job losses with no GDP gains](https://www.hr-inform.co.uk/news-article/up-to-eight-million-uk-jobs-could-be-at-risk-from-ai). That is a boundary, not a forecast. The plan treats it as a target to avoid without saying what avoids it.

**4. Who pulls the lever.** The minister said the plan could reach into "labour market policy" and "even regulation". No named person holds the brief. No threshold is set.

**3. The second wave.** The IPPR puts [11% of tasks exposed now, rising to 59%](https://www.hr-inform.co.uk/news-article/up-to-eight-million-uk-jobs-could-be-at-risk-from-ai) as firms go further. The plan covers the 11% world. It has nothing to say about the 59% world.

**2. Where the retraining goes.** Skills England was promised. Badges were announced. The number of places, the target jobs and the timetable have not been set against the exposure figures.

**1. That the plan is not for job losses. It is for a counting failure.** [Industry surveys miss the "silent adoption" of AI](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-09-29-narayan-ai-job-losses-contingency-plan/) by individual workers. The plan fires when the government can finally see what is already here. It was never a plan to stop it.

That is the sentence nobody wrote down.
