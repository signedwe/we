The UK government's Sovereign AI Fund joined the Fractile funding round last week. The round values Fractile, a London chip startup that has never shipped a chip, at $6.5 billion.

By the end of 2027, that stake will have produced no board seat, no UK supply clause and no preferential pricing that public money couldn't have bought more cheaply through a procurement contract. If Fractile publishes what the state actually got and it includes any of those things, I was wrong.

---

**May 2024.** The government says Britain will be an "AI maker, not an AI taker."

**April 2026.** [The Sovereign AI Fund launches with £500m of public money.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_AI_Fund) Typical cheque: £1m to £10m per deal.

**May 2026.** [Fractile closes a $220m round](https://tech.eu/2026/05/13/uk-ai-chip-startup-fractile-raises-220m-to-tackle-the-growing-inference-bottleneck/) at roughly a $1bn valuation. Chips: still unshipped.

**29 September 2026.** [OpenAI launches Dots at DevDay:](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-dots-always-on-ai-agents-cloud-computers-devday) always-on personal agents, each with its own cloud computer, connecting to over 4,000 apps. [UK Pro users are excluded at launch.](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-launches-dots-always-on-chatgpt-agents-at-devday) No timeline. No reason.

**1 November 2026.** [The Sovereign AI Fund joins the Fractile round,](https://dealroom.co/news/168114-uk-taxpayers-buy-into-the-ai-chip-boom-as-the-sovereign-ai-fund-joins-fr/) reportedly at a slightly lower valuation than the headline $6.5bn. That's a sixfold rise from earlier this year. [Fractile has a reported $250m supply deal with Anthropic.](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-uk-ai-fund-backs-fractile-anthropics-250m-chip-supplier/) OpenAI's main rival.

---

Those two events landed in the same week. Every piece about them treated them as separate stories. They're not.

OpenAI shut UK Pro users out because it hadn't sorted its position on always-on agents here. The Sovereign AI Fund bought into Anthropic's chip supplier because Britain has no frontier model of its own. Both are the same admission in different fonts. Britain is too tangled to receive the frontier product and too far behind to produce one.

The making and the taking swapped places last week and nobody announced it.

[The fund's top cheque is £10m.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_AI_Fund) [The round is around $700m,](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-uk-ai-fund-backs-fractile-anthropics-250m-chip-supplier/) roughly £550m. At those numbers, the state owns about 1.8% of a round into chips it hasn't seen, at a valuation that rose sixfold in months, in a company whose main customer hasn't yet floated.

That's a bet on Anthropic winning. It's not a chip strategy.

The thing Britain actually held last week was the rule book OpenAI wanted to avoid. A market of millions of paying users, with the power to say what ships before it ships, has something real to offer. A £10m cheque at a $6.5bn valuation has a certificate.

Making AI means owning the call on what ships and what doesn't. Last week, OpenAI made that call about Britain. Britain bought into the supply chain of the company that lost.
