You bought it for four pounds at a car boot sale, or inherited it from someone who never explained it, or pulled it from a bin bag at the back of a wardrobe. The label is still in it. You have no idea what you're holding.

Vintage clothing identification used to mean finding a specialist dealer who'd squint at your lapels and either help you or make you feel stupid. Now it takes about eight minutes on a phone.

AI does this better than YouTube because YouTube tells you about eras in general. Claude or ChatGPT can look at the specific thing in your hands, the label you describe, the zipper, the stitching, the lining, the buttons, and tell you what it probably is.

**What you'll have at the end:** a rough decade of manufacture, what the label means, how to care for it properly, and whether it's worth more than four pounds.

**Step 1.** Open [Claude](https://claude.ai) or [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com). Upload a photo of the label, or type out exactly what it says, including any country of origin, fibre content, or union marks. Then describe the garment: the zip brand if there is one, the button material, the lining colour, any unusual stitching details.

Paste this in:

*I have a vintage garment and want to identify it. I'll describe the label and construction details. Tell me: the likely decade of manufacture, what the brand or label indicates, whether any details are useful dating clues, how to care for it correctly, and roughly what this type of piece sells for now. Ask me questions if you need more detail.*

**Step 2.** Answer what it asks. Zipper brand matters: Talon, Lightning, and Conmar are American and date to specific eras. A union label with a number can place a garment within a few years. Missing fibre content labels in the UK suggest pre-1971, when labelling became compulsory.

**Step 3.** Ask it to identify the best place to sell if the news is good. Depop, eBay, a specialist vintage fair: it'll tell you which market suits the piece.

**What it costs:** nothing. Both assistants do this on the free tier.

**How long:** ten minutes, including the time you spend looking at the buttons.

**Where it goes wrong:** AI cannot physically feel the fabric, so fibre identification from touch alone has to come from you. Describe it: does it feel synthetic, does it pill, does it drape heavily? The more specific you are, the better the answer. Also, pricing is rough. A confirmed rare piece needs a specialist to confirm value before you sell it cheap.

The union label clue is the one that surprises people. A ACWA or ILGWU number stamped on a tiny woven tag is a postal address to a decade. Most people cut those labels out. The ones that survive are the whole dating mechanism.
