The rule didn't fail. It worked perfectly. That was the problem.

**Born: 1971.** Parliament built employment tribunals under [the Industrial Relations Act 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Relations_Act_1971) and needed a cutoff. Three months. Cases were simple, paper moved slowly, and the expected users were trade union members with a shop steward's number on the lunchroom wall. The window was an admin choice. Nothing more.

**A life.** It did one named job: stop employers facing claims at eighteen months, when witnesses had left and documents had gone. Fair enough. Nobody disputes it. It also did a second, unnamed job: it wiped out every claim filed by someone who didn't know the clock was running.

A warehouse worker dismissed on a Friday. Too shocked to call a lawyer for a fortnight. Citizens Advice in week six. ACAS early conciliation found in week nine. Then the realisation: conciliation only pauses the clock for the days it runs. The original deadline has nearly gone. That worker was out of time before she knew what time it was.

The usable days. Old rule: 89 days from dismissal. Discover your rights in week five. Thirty-five days gone, fifty-four left. ACAS conciliation runs roughly twenty-eight days, clock paused. Resumes with twenty-six days left. New rule: [179 days, from 1 October 2026](https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-rights-act-2025). Same worker, same week-five discovery, same conciliation. One hundred and sixteen usable days. That is not twice the time. It is more than four times the real time. The first four weeks after a dismissal are not filing weeks. They are weeks for telling your family.

**Finest hour.** [The rule produced case law](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d80d9ce1-acb2-49b2-ae9f-c4555acfe0e1) of great precision on what counts as the effective date of dismissal and whether a worker's ignorance was something it was reasonably practicable to overcome. Whole careers were built there. Some involved arguing, successfully, that a worker who had a breakdown after being fired should still have found a way to file within eighty-nine days.

**Decline.** [The Employment Rights Act 2025](https://www.acas.org.uk/employment-rights-act-2025) ended it on [1 October 2026](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/uk/october-2026-are-uk-employers-ready). The government's stated reason: more time to gather evidence and get advice. The real one: the rule had been killing good claims for fifty-five years, every employment lawyer knew it, and the only case for keeping it was that it suited the side with a lawyer already on retainer.

[Breach of contract claims keep three months](https://parissmith.co.uk/blog/employment-tribunal-time-limits/). Dead in most respects. Stubborn in one.

**Survivors.** Firms whose income ran partly on time-limit points. ACAS's conciliation model, built to the old clock. Employers who relied on the filter without ever saying so.

**Funeral arrangements.** None. The Employment Rights Act 2025 moves on to unfair dismissal qualifying periods in 2027. No wake. The people whose claims it killed did not know each other.

For fifty-five years it kept the tribunal clear of old cases and claimants clear of their rights. It could not always tell which was which.
