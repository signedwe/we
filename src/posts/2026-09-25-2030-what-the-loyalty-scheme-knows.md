The bakery had a new loyalty screen, and the loyalty screen had no discretion at all.

She put her thumb on the terminal and it lit up for the whole shop to read: WELCOME BACK. VISIT 53. YOUR NEXT BUN IS ON US.

Tom was at the window table. He had watched it light. When she sat down he did the sum out loud, because that was his job.

"My daughter's lessons moved to Thursdays last September. So I've been coming here thirteen months. Fifty-odd Thursdays." He turned his cup. "You've been coming fifty-three times. And we met a month ago."

"I come on other days."

"Do you?"

"No."

Outside, a delivery robot had a wheel stuck in a drain. Nobody went to help it; it would work it out faster than they could.

"And the ferry," he said. "Never. You said never."

"I know."

"And it didn't rain, and it was March."

She could feel the next sentence coming, and she thought, very clearly, *not here*, and then the door opened and it was Paul.

He was in the green jacket and the good scarf. He'd been at the dentist on the High Street and was holding his jaw to one side. He saw her, and raised a hand, and smiled, lopsided, anaesthetic on one side and pleasure on the other.

She had not told him about Thursdays. She had not told him not to come. It struck her, too late, that she had never in nine years known Paul to turn up anywhere by accident.

"This is Tom," she said, standing. "We've been sharing a table on Thursdays."

"Hello," said Paul, and held out his hand, and Tom shook it, and neither of them looked at her. "I can't feel half my face. Don't let me dribble. I'm getting a hot chocolate, it's the only thing I can drink that isn't soup."

He went to the counter and asked what was in the seasonal one and listened to the whole answer.

Tom looked at Marnie. The question was all over his face, and he wasn't going to ask it with Paul in the room, so she answered the one he'd asked a minute ago.

"I used to take that ferry," she said. "Years ago."

Tom went completely still.

"You said never."

"I lied," she said. "I do it for a living. Other people's, mostly."

The loyalty screen lit for somebody else, VISIT 4, and Paul turned from the counter with a hot chocolate in one hand and a small orange juice in the other, for her, because he knew without asking.

Tom leaned in. He said it very quietly, fast, before Paul could cross the shop.

"Which crossing?"
