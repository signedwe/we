By end of 2027, at least one FCA enforcement notice will cite a bank or broker for an AI agent acting outside its authorised scope. The company that wrote the agent's code won't be named in that notice. That's the bet, stated early.

[The FCA published its agentic AI statement on 24 November.](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai) [The Next Web covered it the same morning.](https://thenextweb.com/news/fca-agentic-ai-authorisation) Seven hundred words. Here's what they say.

**When an AI agent moves your money, who signs for it?**

The regulated firm. Bank, broker, insurer: whoever deployed the agent. [The FCA is explicit](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-24-fca-agentic-ai-firms-stay-responsible/): a firm that buys a third-party agent off a shelf stays on the hook for every transaction it makes. The company that built the agent is a "tech provider."

**When does the builder need a licence?**

When the agent moves money, arranges a loan, or makes an investment call. Until that moment: speak to us. There's no line drawn in the document. The line appears when the agent crosses it.

**Four UK digital regulators wrote to ministers on 16 November.** They said personal agents were nearing launch. [Some builders hadn't spoken to any regulator.](https://thenextweb.com/news/fca-agentic-ai-authorisation) This statement arrived nine days after that letter. How many builders have engaged since?

The statement doesn't say.

**What if the agent buys something a consumer didn't want?**

Complain to the firm. The firm bought the agent. The firm answers for it. The firm then argues with the builder under whatever contract they signed — a commercial dispute, not a regulatory one. When a customer's agent arranges the wrong product, every enforcement notice goes to the firm that deployed it. The company that decided what the agent does gets a contract argument, not an FCA letter.

**Isn't Consumer Duty enough?**

This is worth taking seriously. Consumer Duty already requires firms to prevent foreseeable harm, act in good faith, help people reach their financial goals. The FCA isn't inventing new law. Existing rules apply and a statement saying so is reasonable.

But Consumer Duty reaches the firm. What the firm can do to the builder who caused the problem is a contract question. The contracts were signed before anyone knew Consumer Duty was the right frame. The customer gets a complaints route. The builder gets a clause.

**What is the FCA planning next?**

An Agentic Policy Sprint. [No date set.](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai)

One sentence in the statement: ["We will assess the services firms provide through agentic products, including how they develop over time."](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/ai-approach/agentic-ai) Assessment as the products develop. The agent ships. The assessment follows. The compliance officer at the firm that bought the agent is already reading the indemnity clause.
