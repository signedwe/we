Your favourite perfume is running low. You've been rationing it for weeks.

A fragrance consultant used to be the person who'd decode why you love a scent and find you something cheaper. They worked in department stores. They took a fee for the session or steered you toward something at full margin. Now you can do it yourself, free, in ten minutes, with a photo of the back of your bottle.

**What you'll have at the end:** three alternatives to a fragrance you love, explained by chemistry rather than marketing, at a fraction of the original price.

**What you need:** the bottle, Claude or ChatGPT (free tier), and [Fragrantica](https://www.fragrantica.com) for the cross-check.

**Step 1.** Flip the bottle. Photograph the ingredient list. It's the long Latin one: parfum, ethanol, aqua, linalool, and so on. Upload it to Claude or ChatGPT.

*Try this:* "Here's the ingredient list from my perfume. What's the dominant accord? Which two or three molecules give it its character? What are the top, heart and base notes likely to be? Put it in plain English."

**Step 2.** Once it describes the scent back to you, ask:

*"Based on that profile, name three fragrances under £25 that share the same dominant accord and key molecules. Say which notes they share and which they don't."*

**Step 3.** Take the shortlist to [Fragrantica](https://www.fragrantica.com). User reviews there call out dupes by name, from people who've worn both. Cross-check at least one recommendation before ordering.

**Step 4.** Tell the AI how your skin behaves. Does the scent fade fast? Go sweeter after an hour? Stay close or announce you from across a table? It'll adjust. A price-comparison site can't do that.

**How long:** ten minutes.

**What it costs:** nothing.

**Where it goes wrong:** the AI will sometimes confuse two molecules with similar names. It may recommend a discontinued line. Check that anything it names is still being sold before ordering. For very niche or artisan fragrances, give it more description of what you smell. The ingredient list alone may not be enough to identify a small-batch formula.

The thing AI does here that a search cannot: it looks at your bottle, your list, your skin's behaviour, and reasons about all three together. A YouTube review tells you about one person's nose. This tells you about yours.
