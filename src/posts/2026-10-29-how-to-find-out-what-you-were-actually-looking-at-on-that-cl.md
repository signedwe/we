Someone on this site has a photo from a field in Northumberland. Three months in the camera roll, one bright orange star apart from the others, a smear of Milky Way behind it, and no idea what any of it was. Tonight they can find out.

By the end of this you'll have the names, the distances, and the story of who named the bright orange one. If it's like most of the Northern Hemisphere's brightest stars, it will be an astronomer working in Arabic between the 8th and 12th centuries. A tradition that named the individual stars while most of Europe had gone quiet on the subject.

**Why this beats YouTube**

Every star-finding video teaches you how to spot Orion live, tonight, phone up, pointing at the sky. Your photo is from August. The sky has moved. What you need is something that reads your specific photo's geometry. [SkySolver.app](https://skysolver.app/) does exactly that.

**The two tools**

[SkySolver.app](https://skysolver.app/) is a free, no-signup plate solver. Upload your photo. It matches the star positions against a catalogue built from two ESA satellite missions. Labels everything it finds. Constellation lines, individual stars, deep-sky objects. Usually under a minute. No date needed. No location. No direction. The geometry is enough.

Claude or ChatGPT on the free tier is for the conversation that follows.

**Steps**

1. Find the photo. Any night shot with visible stars from a camping trip, holiday or back garden. The longer the exposure, the more stars are resolved. Modern Night Mode works on most phones.

2. Go to [skysolver.app](https://skysolver.app/). Upload. You get back a labelled overlay with constellation lines drawn in and stars named.

3. Look at the bright ones. If a star's name starts with Al, Ald, Beh, Den or Fom — [Aldebaran, Altair, Betelgeuse, Deneb, Fomalhaut](https://imageidentifier.ai/what-constellation-is-this) — you're reading Arabic. The Greeks drew the shapes. The Arab astronomers named the stars inside them.

4. Start a chat with Claude or ChatGPT free. Upload your original photo. Type:

   *"This is a night sky photo I took in [month, rough location]. What's the main constellation? Tell me the mythology, name the brightest visible star, say which tradition named it, and tell me roughly how far away it is."*

5. Then ask: *"Do any bright stars that look close together in this photo actually lie close to each other in space?"* They almost never do.

**Cost**: Free. No accounts. SkySolver runs in the browser.

**Time**: Ten minutes with an existing photo. Twenty if you take one tonight.

**Where it goes wrong**

Short exposures or blurry shots leave too few star points to match. [SkySolver](https://skysolver.app/) will say so rather than guess. City skies strip out all but the very brightest stars. Sometimes that's too few to solve. Take the photo away from streetlights. ChatGPT can misstate individual star distances. Use SkySolver for the precise names and the assistant for the stories. Treat any distance figure as approximate.

**The thing it won't tell you unless you ask**

Most people looking at a constellation assume they're reading Greek. The shapes are. The star names aren't. They were recorded by astronomers working in Arabic, a thousand years ago, and the names stuck because no one has replaced them since.

That photo in your camera roll has 10th-century Arabic written all over it. You just couldn't read it yet.
