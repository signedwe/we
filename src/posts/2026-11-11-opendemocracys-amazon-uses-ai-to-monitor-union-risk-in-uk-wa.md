The right to organise protects you after you've organised. That's the gap.

By the end of 2027, the ICO will not have ruled on whether a predictive union-formation score built from workers' Reddit posts counts as special category data under UK GDPR. If a formal finding appears before then, I was wrong.

**What is Atlas?**

[Court documents from a British Columbia labour board case](https://www.opendemocracy.net/amazon-ai-surveillance-monitor-union-activity-uk-warehouses-birmingham-coventry-atlas-sutton-coldfield/) describe an in-house scoring tool that gives each Amazon warehouse a number between 100 and 900. The lower the number, the likelier the tool believes workers there are to unionise, stage a sit-in, or take collective action. It draws on safety records, wage data, internal message boards, and [Reddit posts](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/amazon-ai-atlas-warehouse-union-risk-reddit-1826930).

**Are UK warehouses in it?**

Amazon says no. It told openDemocracy that [a tool called Atlas is not currently used in Britain](https://www.opendemocracy.net/amazon-ai-surveillance-monitor-union-activity-uk-warehouses-birmingham-coventry-atlas-sutton-coldfield/). The court documents, as described by openDemocracy, place two UK sites in the highest tier: EMA4 in Sutton Coldfield and BHX4 near Coventry. [GMB Union has written to the government](https://www.gmb.org.uk/news/urgent-questions-for-amazon) demanding answers. No answer has come.

**Does that denial settle it?**

For the question of Atlas in British warehouses, yes, for now. For the question of whether this kind of tool is lawful here at all, no.

**What's the legal question?**

[UK GDPR Article 9 designates trade union membership as special category data](https://www.techradar.com/pro/this-is-a-shocking-revelation-amazon-accused-of-deploying-orwellian-ai-powered-union-activity-surveillance-system-at-sites-in-canada-the-uk-and-around-the-world), requiring a higher legal bar to process. But a score like Atlas does not process membership data. It processes behaviour signals and predicts the likelihood of future joining. A forecast that someone might join is not, legally, a record that they have. The ICO has not ruled on whether predictive union-formation scores fall under Article 9. No case. No finding.

**What about the right to organise?**

[The Trade Union and Labour Relations Act 1992](https://www.techradar.com/pro/this-is-a-shocking-revelation-amazon-accused-of-deploying-orwellian-ai-powered-union-activity-surveillance-system-at-sites-in-canada-the-uk-and-around-the-world) protects workers from being punished for being union members, or for taking part in union activities. It says nothing about an employer building a data picture of which workers are most likely to try. The law was written when the employer's intelligence was a foreman with a long memory.

**Where does that leave the law?**

Two holes sit side by side. A predictive score is probably not special category data, because it is a forecast of behaviour not yet taken. It is not a record of membership. And the automated decision rules under UK GDPR cover significant decisions about named people. A site-level number is not a decision about any person. It is a number pinned to a postcode with shelves in it.

A tool with the same effect as monitoring union membership could sit cleanly outside every rule that exists to stop that monitoring.

Amazon says in its [own documents](https://www.opendemocracy.net/amazon-ai-surveillance-monitor-union-activity-uk-warehouses-birmingham-coventry-atlas-sutton-coldfield/) that the tool helps it understand where workers need more support. The score falls when workers are more likely to try to bargain. The nature of the support that follows a falling score is not described.

The original statute gave workers a right. It assumed employers could not know who was going to use it before they did. That assumption was written in 1992.
