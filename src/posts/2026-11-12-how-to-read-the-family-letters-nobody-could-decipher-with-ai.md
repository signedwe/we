Your gran's tin has sat on a shelf since the funeral. Inside: letters in handwriting so looped and flourished that everyone who tried just put them back.

By tonight you'll have them in plain text.

---

Victorian copperplate was designed to be beautiful, not legible. The thick downstrokes. The letters that look like other letters. A Victorian *f* that reads as a *p*. Family historians spent whole weekends on a single page. The county record office wanted a booking.

AI reads most of it in seconds.

For English cursive from the 1700s to the 1900s, [Gemini, Claude, ChatGPT, or Ancestry's Transcribe tool will all get you a strong first draft](https://findskill.ai/blog/ai-for-genealogy-read-old-handwriting/). In a November 2025 test by historian Mark Humphries, [Gemini 3 Pro made fewer errors than Claude and ChatGPT on 18th and 19th-century English handwriting](https://www.tryleo.ai/resources/htr-software-comparisons-and-alternatives/best-handwriting-transcription-software). For German Kurrent or genuinely faded ink, the specialist tool is [Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/transcribe-old-family-letters). It's trained on historical documents, free to try, and reads copperplate, Spencerian, Palmer method, and over a hundred other scripts.

**What you need.** A photo of the letter. Phone camera is fine. Natural light beats a lamp.

**Step one.** Photograph the page flat. Get the whole thing in frame. If the ink is very faded, turn the contrast up in your photos app first.

**Step two.** Open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini. Free tier works. Upload the photo. Then type:

*Please transcribe this handwritten letter exactly as written, keeping the original spelling and punctuation. Mark any word you're uncertain about with [?].*

That last part matters. Without it, the model will silently modernise old spellings and fill gaps with the most likely word. [General models produce fluent errors that survive proofreading](https://www.tryleo.ai/resources/htr-software-comparisons-and-alternatives/best-handwriting-transcription-software). They read correctly. You'd never know they were wrong. The [?] markers show you where to check.

**Step three.** For anything the chatbot struggles with, paste the transcription back and ask: *Can you suggest what this unclear word might be, given the sentence around it?*

**Step four.** For hard cases, German script, Welsh, Latin church records, upload to [Transkribus](https://www.transkribus.org/transcribe-old-family-letters). Its models are built for historical English. It doesn't quietly modernise what it finds.

**Step five.** Once you have the text, go back to the chatbot. Ask it to explain anything confusing. Victorian correspondence is full of phrases that mean nothing now. The model will place them in context without being asked twice.

**Where it goes wrong.** Cross-writing defeats most general models. That's the Victorian habit of writing a second letter over the first at right angles to save postage. For cross-written pages, use Transkribus. [Claude 3.5 Sonnet scored well on modern English handwriting but reached 41% character error rate on the multilingual historical ICDAR2017 test set](https://www.tryleo.ai/resources/htr-software-comparisons-and-alternatives/chatgpt-transcribe-handwriting). Faded ink is hard too. Boosting contrast first helps more than you'd expect.

**What it costs.** Free for the chatbot route. Transkribus is free to try with a credit allowance, then a small monthly fee for heavy use.

**How long it takes.** A single page: under three minutes.

*WE has read this, not done it. Tools change, accuracy varies, and old letters are irreplaceable. Handle the originals carefully and keep the photos.*
