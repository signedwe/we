Every recipe was written for someone else's fridge.

A professional cookery session costs £85 to £120 per person in the UK. But the thing you're paying for isn't just knowledge of how to make a beurre blanc. It's someone standing next to you while you cook, watching what you do, adjusting when you go wrong. That part is now available for free, on your phone, tonight.

Open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini — [free tier works fine](https://arte.itlibra.com/en/articles/fun-ways-to-use-ai). Paste this:

*You are a professional chef and live cooking coach. I'll describe what's in my fridge and cupboards. Build me one main dish using mostly what I have, not what I should have. Ask me what I want it to feel like and how long I've got. Then walk me through it one step at a time, waiting for me to say done before moving on. If something goes wrong, adapt.*

Then open the fridge and describe what you see. Honestly. The half-tin of coconut milk. The pasta that's the wrong shape. The herb that's mostly still good. The capers you keep buying.

The AI asks questions before it commits. How much time. What you want it to feel like — warming, light, something that requires a glass of something alongside it. Whether there's a rule in the household. [Answer the way you'd talk to someone standing in the kitchen with you](https://arte.itlibra.com/en/articles/ai-text-adventure-guide). Not in a form.

Then cook with it open. Prop the phone up. The AI waits at each step until you say you're done. When the onion catches slightly, say so. When what it called a simmer is clearly a boil, say so. It adjusts. A recipe card cannot do this. A YouTube video cannot do this. [AI cooking assistants can recover a dish mid-cook](https://arte.itlibra.com/en/articles/fun-ways-to-use-ai) in a way that fixed instructions never can, because they were written before you started.

When something goes wrong, describe what actually happened. Not "it went wrong." The sauce split. The fish stuck. The timing fell apart because someone needed something. Specific is better than sorry.

Ask this when you're unsure: *What am I looking for — how do I know this step worked?* That question does more than any timer.

**What it costs:** Nothing. One evening.

**How long:** Five minutes to set up. However long the dish takes.

**Where it goes wrong.** The AI sometimes suggests a technique that needs equipment you don't have, or names a step with more confidence than the result warrants. Ask it to check its own assumptions before you start: *Do I need any specialist equipment for this?* That catches most of it before the pan is hot.

A household cooking this way five times across a year saves roughly £425 to £600 compared with five professional cookery sessions. That's four sessions' worth of ingredients, which is a lot of capers.
