Your grandmother's piano book is in a box in the spare room. You can play four of the pieces — badly — because you learned them as a child. The other forty you've never heard.

You could look them up on YouTube. But YouTube plays somebody else's performance at somebody else's tempo. It doesn't follow your score. It doesn't highlight each note as it lands. It won't let you loop the four bars in the middle that keep going wrong.

[PlayScore 2](https://www.playscore.co/) does all of that. Free, on your phone.

Point your camera at a page of printed music and it reads the notation: notes, dynamics, repeats, chords. Then it plays it back, scrolling measure by measure. You set the tempo. You pick the sound. You can mute the right hand and play along with the left. [Everything runs on your device.](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.organum.playscore&hl=en_US) Nothing leaves your phone.

**What you'll have at the end:** any page of printed sheet music, played back at whatever speed you choose, with every note highlighted as it sounds.

**How long it takes:** about three minutes the first time.

**What it costs:** nothing for a single page from the camera. Multi-page scores, PDF import, and export need a paid subscription. [That starts at around £5.99 a month.](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/playscore-2)

**Steps**

1. Download [PlayScore 2](https://www.playscore.co/) — free on [iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/playscore-2/id1449591118) and [Android](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.organum.playscore&hl=en_US).
2. Open it. Tap the camera icon. Point it at your sheet music. Good light, steady hand.
3. It scans, reads the notation, and opens a playable version. Press play.
4. Use the tempo slider to slow it down. Tap any bar to jump to it. Tap the sound icon to swap piano for strings or anything else.
5. To loop a section, drag the yellow brackets around the bars you're working on. It repeats until you stop it.

*Try: tap a note you don't recognise while it's playing. The app highlights where it sits on the staff.*

For anything puzzling on the page — what does this marking mean, why does this key signature have four sharps — take a photo and ask Claude or ChatGPT. They'll explain it plainly and can suggest fingering. No lesson required.

**Where it goes wrong**

[PlayScore needs clear, well-printed music in good light.](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.organum.playscore&hl=en_US) Handwritten scores, faded photocopies, or unusual notation may misread. Scan a tatty page and the playback will be wrong in places. Check it against what's on the page.

The free tier covers one page at a time from the camera. A four-page sonata is four separate scans. If your whole book is a PDF on a tablet, [you'll need the paid tier.](https://blog.flat.io/best-sheet-music-scanner-apps-android/)

The app is clear: sharing copyrighted music without permission is your responsibility, not theirs.

**What beats it:** nothing, for this specific job. YouTube has a better recording of the Moonlight Sonata. It cannot slow down bar seventeen of your photocopy and loop it while you figure out the chord.
