The gap is the size the government chose.

By the end of 2027, the AI Security Institute will have the legal power to compel a safety test, or it will be the world's most expensive reading group. That's not set by ministers. It's set by whatever happens next on British soil that they can't hold in committee.

**1. Thirty-five months. No power to compel anything.**
The AISI [launched on 2 November 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Security_Institute). The [Commons Library briefing of 14 October](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-10003/CBP-10003.pdf) puts it plain: the institute has no legal power. The labs show up when they want to. When they don't want to, nothing happens.

**2. Anthropic didn't want to.**
The AISI asked to see Mythos 5.1. [Anthropic said no.](https://aifront-page.com/uk-ai-regulation-parliament-committee-ai-safety-institute/) No fine. No hearing. No letter with teeth. Every other lab clocked the answer and filed it.

**3. Parliament wrote to four companies and asked what rules they'd accept.**
The Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee [sent four identical letters](https://www.pk-sharma.com/briefing/uk-committee-invites-ai-labs-ten-questions-no-power-to-compel) to Meta, Google DeepMind, OpenAI and Anthropic. Invitations, not summons. Ten questions about rules the UK doesn't have yet. Nobody had agreed to come. That's where we are: MPs asking the regulated to draft the regulation.

**4. The government's reply to fourteen months of work is: we're still thinking.**
The [Joint Committee on Human Rights](https://committees.parliament.uk/publications/54971/documents/304882/default/) spent over a year on this. It published 233 paragraphs on 14 September saying British law can't protect people from AI harm. The government got two months to reply. That window closes in 27 days. On 14 October, a civil servant told a committee that ministers are being informed by [an analysis that isn't finished yet](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/artificial-intelligence/articles/2537666).

**5. Not deciding is a decision.**
Every month without a legal power to compel, the AISI sends the same message to every lab: you can say no and nothing follows. This isn't a slow bureaucracy. It's what you get when the people who could write the law have decided that keeping the labs happy matters more than being able to stop them. The government calls this approach ["Regulating for Growth."](https://www.twobirds.com/en/capabilities/artificial-intelligence/ai-legal-services/ai-regulatory-horizon-tracker/uk) What it currently regulates is the choice not to.

The committee spent fourteen months saying British law can't protect people. The government has spent at least as long not writing any. The deliberation has eaten as much time as the investigation, and produced a civil servant with an unfinished spreadsheet.

"Remains up in the air." Fourteen months in. Thirty-five months in. Twenty-seven days left. Quote it and stop.
