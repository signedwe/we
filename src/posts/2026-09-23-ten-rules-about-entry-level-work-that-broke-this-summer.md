By the end of 2028, at least one UK professional body will revise its training requirements not because it wants to but because it can't find anyone who did the entry-level work the old way. The Law Society or the ICAEW will move first, and neither will have an honest answer to what replaced it.

[The Work Foundation surveyed 1,001 UK businesses in May](https://sea.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/one-in-three-uk-employers-cut-entry-level-jobs-as-ai-and-automation-reshape-hiring-51727): 36% had cut entry-level roles. Among large employers, six in ten blamed AI. Graduate vacancies on Adzuna [fell 49% in July](https://globalbusinessoutlook.com/economy/ai-effect-one-in-three-british-employers-have-cut-entry-level-jobs-says-survey/). [Over a million 16-to-24-year-olds](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-08-26-entry-level-jobs-work-foundation/) sat outside education and work in Q1 2026.

If 46% of large employers cut entry-level roles and 60% of those blame AI, roughly 28% of large employers have made AI-attributed cuts: 0.46 × 0.60 = 0.276. That figure is in none of the surveys.

Here are the ten rules that summer broke, ranked by how long anyone thought they'd hold.

**10. Experience comes before competence.** You start bad and get good. AI starts bad in private and improves without a salary.

**9. The CV proves what you did.** When AI produces the same outputs without the history, the document stops being evidence of a person. It becomes a list of tasks.

**8. Internships teach.** They select, mostly. They always did. The fiction that they teach justified the unpaid labour. Now the sorting happens earlier, by algorithm, before the internship is posted.

**7. The application pool is human.** [Employers screen applications by AI](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/14/ai-judgment-day-on-the-horizon-while-uk-lawmakers-play-catch-up). A human reads the shortlist. Nobody is clear on who read what the AI wrote.

**6. Large employers hire more juniors than small ones.** Until this year. [46% of large employers cut entry-level roles, against 24% of small ones](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-08-26-entry-level-jobs-work-foundation/). The firms with the biggest automation budgets are running the experiment on someone else's children.

**5. NEET is a personal failure.** [Over a million young people outside education and work in Q1 2026](https://www.financialnewstoday.co.uk/one-in-three-uk-employers-cut-entry-level-jobs-survey-finds/) — the worst reading since 2013. Nobody's short of young people who want work. The shortage is of jobs built on the idea that a human has to fill them.

**4. Probation tells you who can do the work.** Three months, a review, a decision. That rule assumed the new hire was the variable. Now the variable is which AI the team adopts in January, and neither side of the probation has a name for that.

**3. Junior roles exist to train the junior.** You watched, you were corrected, you learnt what the work felt like from inside. The economics were simple: cheap labour, real learning. [Remove the cheap labour](https://sea.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/one-in-three-uk-employers-cut-entry-level-jobs-as-ai-and-automation-reshape-hiring-51727) and the profession loses the way it copies itself. The title survives. The afternoon beside the desk does not.

**2. The profession reproduces itself through its juniors.** This is Rule 3 one generation on. A senior who was never a junior doesn't know what they don't know. Fine, until something goes wrong and the form asks who signed off.

**1. NEET is temporary.** The bottom of the market always recovered. This time the contraction has a cause that won't reverse when the economy picks up.

The rule everyone expects to break last is the one that already has.
