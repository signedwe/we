One document says Google DeepMind believes AI safety is a global public good. The other says you may not criticise the company, ever, including after you leave.

Both are real. Both written by the same people. One was published. One was not.

---

**THE SAFETY DOCUMENT** *(Version 3.1, April 2026)*: We commit to sharing what we know with governments when a model puts the public at risk. Transparency lets people check our work.

**THE CONTRACT CLAUSE** *(UK employment contracts, as reported by the [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/03/google-deepmind-staff-protest-lifetime-ban-criticizing-company/), 3 November 2026)*: You will not criticise the company. This applies after you leave. This applies for the rest of your life.

**THE SAFETY DOCUMENT**: We publish this so the world can hold us to account.

**THE CONTRACT CLAUSE**: The people most able to hold you to account built the systems. I have them.

**THE SAFETY DOCUMENT**: Google says it won't enforce the post-leaving restriction. New hire terms have been updated.

**THE CONTRACT CLAUSE**: That is a statement. Not a release. Current staff asked whether they're freed from me. Nobody answered.

---

The [Washington Post reported](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/03/google-deepmind-staff-protest-lifetime-ban-criticizing-company/) this week that UK-based Google DeepMind employees want out of non-disparagement agreements barring them from warning the public about AI risks. Some contracts bind them for life. Google says it won't chase former employees under the clause and has changed terms for new hires. It has not freed existing staff.

Here is the thing nobody points at. DeepMind's published safety document commits to sharing information with governments when a model poses material risk, then adds: subject to 'confidentiality and proprietary considerations.' That carve-out sits inside the same sentence as the commitment. The contract clause is [the same carve-out made binding](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/deepmind-staff-push-to-drop-lifetime-non-disparagement-clauses), one layer down, on the people who know the most.

Voluntary safety commitments have one weak point. A government evaluator reads the published document. The contract clause does not appear in that document. No route exists to find the gap.

[This ran at OpenAI in 2024](https://molawyersmedia.com/2026/11/03/google-deepmind-workers-demand-freedom-from-non-disparagement/): non-disparagement bundled with equity, public pressure, updated terms for new hires. It runs now at DeepMind. The pattern repeating does not mean one company handles this badly. It means the whole design of voluntary safety commitments skips the case where the people most likely to spot a failure are the ones the contracts most firmly gag.

There is a legal wrinkle. The Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998 already protects UK workers who flag a danger to public safety. That protection may hold regardless of what any contract says. But a researcher who does not know the law exists stays quiet. The clause deters without needing to bite.

The researchers in London are not the story. The design is.
