The bay window on your street wasn't put there for the light. It was the builder's way of charging three shillings more a week in rent. You probably walk past it every day.

**By the end of this, you'll have** the ghost history of a building you've never really looked at: who it was built for, what's probably behind the plasterboard, and what the facade was designed to say.

**What it beats.** A search gives you a style name, if you know what to search for. YouTube explains Victorian architecture in general. Neither one can look at your specific fanlight. The AI adapts to what you photograph.

**Step one.** Stand back far enough to get the roofline, windows and front door in one shot. Straight-on, daylight, no filter.

**Step two.** Go to [Facade](https://facade-recognizer.com/) — free, no account — and upload the photo. In about thirty seconds it names the style, the approximate era, and the features it used: the cornice line, the window-arch shape, the brickwork course.

**Step three.** Open Claude or ChatGPT on your phone. Free tier works. [Claude reads the photo and gives you a detailed analysis](https://planner5d.com/blog/claude-for-interior-design/): what to keep, what the layout probably was, what's likely hiding. Upload the photo and type:

*"This has been identified as [style and era]. Who was this built for originally? What would the interior layout have been? What original features are most likely still there, behind the plasterboard or under the carpets?"*

**Step four.** Ask the follow-up nobody thinks to ask:

*"What did this frontage signal to the street when it was new? Was the bay window an upgrade? Was the tile path a status marker?"*

This is where it gets strange. Victorian and Edwardian speculative builders coded aspiration into brick. A single terrace could run four social grades across one street. Bay-windowed with decorative corbels at the top. Flat-fronted and plain-corniced at the bottom. The hierarchy was in the brickwork the whole time.

**Step five.** Try it on three buildings in a row. You're reading the street now.

**Where it goes wrong.** [AI style tools frequently mislabel Edwardian terraces as Victorian](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jiec.13591) — one 2025 study on building attribute prediction from street-view images found era classification correct in roughly seven out of ten cases, with errors heaviest in mass-market housing where styles overlap. [ChatGPT will also move windows, change ceiling heights, and quietly invent architecture that was never in your photo](https://www.meltflexai.com/blog/chatgpt-prompts-interior-design) if you ask it to generate an image rather than describe one. Stick to description. Treat the decade as a starting point. The conversation is the point, not the label.

**What it costs.** Nothing. Facade is free. Claude and ChatGPT free tiers handle photo uploads.

**How long.** Twenty minutes the first time. Then you probably won't stop.
