A rule born as a price is under different pressure than one born as a principle. Here are ten that started as prices.

By 2028, the [Legal Services Act 2007](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/29/contents) won't have been reviewed for which parts AI can do without a qualified lawyer. If a government review opens, I was wrong.

**10. The law degree for barristers**
Solicitors dropped the law degree in 2021 when the SRA launched the [SQE](https://www.sra.org.uk/become-solicitor/admission/pathways-qualification/). Barristers kept it. The [Bar Standards Board proposed scrapping even the grade floor](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/bsb-to-scrap-requirement-for-barristers-to-have-a-degree). The [Bar Council said no](https://www.barcouncil.org.uk/resource/bar-council-resists-bar-regulator-s-proposal-to-remove-degree-requirement-for-barristers-in-training-press-release.html): lowering standards isn't the answer. The rule is 1971 vintage. The profession argues about the grade. Not the question.

**9. The registered office**
Every company at Companies House needs a physical UK address. Courts have served documents electronically since 2006. The registered office is a price on chasing companies that owe money. Most of that price now goes to an agent holding a postcode.

**8. Drawn plans for planning applications**
Council planners need scaled drawings to set tolerances. That rule exists because a sketch on a napkin told them nothing. AI tools now turn a photo and a description into a compliant plan. The requirement prices software, not skill.

**7. CPD minimums**
Every regulated lawyer, doctor and adviser logs hours each year. The rule was born when knowledge went stale fast and professional bodies were the only patch point. The knowledge half-life fell. The hours did not.

**6. The witness for a statutory declaration**
You need a solicitor or notary in the room. Remote video checks have been legal for years. The person who works nights and can't reach a high street office in business hours is paying for a rule from 1835.

**5. The seven foundation subjects**
The Bar's [seven foundation areas](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/03069400.2024.2433327) go back to 1971. They were the minimum shared vocabulary for common law work. An AI holds all seven. It pulls case law faster than any clerk. This rule now prices the act of reading something the system already has.

**4. Reserved legal activities**
The Legal Services Act 2007 put six jobs — court advocacy, conveyancing and four more — behind a licence. The gap between lawyer and layperson still holds for judgment under fire. It collapsed, quietly, for research and drafting. The licence now guards the last five minutes.

**3. The GP referral**
You need a GP's say-so before you see a specialist on the NHS. GPs exist because a generalist with broad knowledge is cheaper than giving everyone direct specialist access. AI tools now pass clinical licensing exams. The referral was always a price on specialist time. It's not obvious whose.

**2. Two years' supervised practice for solicitors**
The SRA made [two years of qualifying work experience](https://www.sra.org.uk/become-solicitor/admission/pathways-qualification/) the entry price in 2021. Same year the first plausible AI legal drafts appeared. The profession put up a new gate the moment the old one stopped working. What those two years buy now is a partner's eye on you. Not law.

**1. Human review under the [Data Use and Access Act 2025](https://deployflow.co/blog/regulated-industries-uk-getting-ai-wrong/)**
In force 5 February 2026. Any automated decision with a legal or significant effect must let a human review it. Newest rule on the list. Built for the AI era. Already a price. It assumes a human reviewer adds something the machine can't. That costs money every time it runs. Automated decisions multiply fast. When checking gets cheap too, this rule stops being a safeguard. It becomes the most expensive one here. And the one everyone defends hardest.

None of these rules admits it was born as a price. They all say public protection. That's not a lie. It's what a price sounds like after fifty years.
