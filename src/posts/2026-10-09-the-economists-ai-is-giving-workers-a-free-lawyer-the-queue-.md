**Before 2026.** The Employment Tribunal runs on solicitors. [Interim relief applications: about 20 a year across Great Britain.](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/tribunal-tries-to-deter-interim-relief-bids-amid-ai-related-surge/5127144.article) Not because workers had few grievances. Because filing one took money most of them didn't have.

**June 2026.** The Ministry of Justice publishes its quarterly figures. [Single claims: 70,000 open.](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/tribunal-backlog-hits-70000-as-system-struggles-to-keep-up/) A record. [Receipts up 28%. Disposals down 6%.](https://bmmagazine.co.uk/legal/employment-tribunal-backlog-record-70000-cases/) At that rate: 14,000 claims in per quarter, 6,100 out. The net addition runs to roughly 31,600 a year. No published source states that number. The queue isn't draining.

**Also June 2026.** The presidents of the employment tribunals in England, Wales and Scotland issue guidance. [Interim relief applications have gone from 20 a year to 20 a month at each regional office.](https://www.bakermckenzie.com/en/insight/publications/2026/07/united-kingdom-employment-tribunal-interim-relief-guidance-issued) The guidance does not ask why. It says: stop applying.

**17 September 2026.** [*Hancox v Sutherland* [2026] EAT 139.](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6aabab820420ac660805f955/Mr_T_Hancox_v_Kenneth_Sutherland_and_Others__2026__EAT_139.pdf) An unrepresented man [files a 300-page skeleton argument of 132,000 words made with ChatGPT.](https://solicitornews.co.uk/ai-generated-legal-documents-eat-warning/) His proposal to the respondents: find the errors by 4pm tomorrow. The judge rejects this. AI is not banned. But the document is yours. All of it.

**7 October 2026.** Law firms publish their guidance. The lesson: check your AI drafts. The system is safe.

**End of 2027.** At current rates, the open caseload passes 100,000. No guidance on proofreading changes that. If the caseload stays below 100,000, I'm wrong.

**Probably 2029.** A woman dismissed today in south London files a clean, accurate AI-drafted claim. She gets a hearing. Three years from now. The AI took forty minutes.

---

The tribunals were never bottlenecked by lack of rights. They were bottlenecked by the cost of using them. That cost kept the queue short. It was also, and nobody said this out loud, how the system managed its own capacity.

AI removed the cost. The queue is now showing what was always behind it.

[The EAT ruling on 17 September](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-09-18-eat-ai-warning-litigants-in-person/) is correct on the procedural question: file it, own it. But the lesson being drawn from it is tidy rather than honest. Law firms tell clients to proofread. Nobody asks who decided that professional fees were an acceptable filter for a public court.

Half the problem is the 300-page skeleton. [The other half is that the judge must read enough of it to know if there's a point inside.](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/justice-jammed-uk-employment-tribunal-claims-reach-historic-high) Then write reasons. Then manage the appeal of the refusal. One bad filing is four or five hearings. The 70,000 cases aren't equally weighted.

The gate kept the list short. It's gone. Nobody has said what goes in its place.
