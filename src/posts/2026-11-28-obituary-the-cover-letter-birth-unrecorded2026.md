The cover letter died this autumn, age unknown, of being too good. It took the people who still wrote theirs by hand down with it.

It lived under the CV. One page, three paragraphs, one message: I want this job, this one. People believed it because it hurt to write. Three drafts. A Sunday night. A call to your mum about whether "keen" sounds desperate. The effort was the message.

What it gave you besides: the only writing a twenty-two-year-old did all year that nobody marked. The small thrill of swapping in the right company name, and the small horror of finding you hadn't.

What nobody will miss: "Dear Sir or Madam." "Passionate." The paragraph about teamwork.

The cause of death is on record. Freelancer.com gave some of its workers a one-click letter writer. Their letters matched the job ads more closely, and the authors see [signs that callbacks rose for the people who used it](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.25054). Then three Yale economists found that [the link between that match and getting a callback fell by 52%](https://cowles.yale.edu/research/cfdp-2577-signaling-age-ai-evidence-cover-letters). The fall also hit letters written without the tool. The ones still done by hand on a Sunday night lost their worth along with the rest, and employers moved their trust to [workers' past work on the platform](https://jingyi-cui.github.io/Signaling_AI_Cui_Dias_Ye.pdf). That is one freelance platform, not graduates, so the number stays there.

A Wharton economist [told Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/03/23/is-the-cover-letter-dead-ai-writing-for-ai) that the best letters in his fifteen years of choosing research assistants all arrived in the last twelve months, and every one cites his papers.

In the Institute of Student Employers' 2026 survey of 155 employers, [64 per cent now accept some use of AI in recruitment, up from 46 per cent](https://hrreview.co.uk/hr-news/recruitment/ai-misuse-in-graduate-recruitment-reaches-record-high-employers-report/391806), generally for CVs, cover letters and written applications. The employer who asks for the letter now lets a machine write it.

**Survivors:** the box on the form, still there, still ticked. Wild specimens may remain wherever one person reads every application. None confirmed here.

**Funeral:** Sunday evening, as ever. No flowers. Please send one sentence that could not be about any other company.
