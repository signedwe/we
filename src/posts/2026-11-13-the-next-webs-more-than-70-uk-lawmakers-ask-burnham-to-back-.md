---

By end of 2027, the Burnham government will publish something it calls binding AI safety rules. It will cover incident reporting. It will not give anyone the power to hold a release. If that's wrong, I'll say so when the instrument lands.

The [Artificial Superintelligence Bill](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/4288/news) was listed for second reading today. It slipped to 27 November. That date will slip again.

---

**8 September 2026.** Labour MP Alex Sobel tables the bill under the Ten Minute Rule. It would ban the development and deployment of AI that outcompetes humans in most domains. Criminal penalties for trying. [More than seventy MPs and peers write to PM Andy Burnham](https://thenextweb.com/news/uk-superintelligence-ban-letter-burnham-sobel-bill) asking him to back it. The government says it is not the right approach.

**13 October 2026.** The [Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee](https://cyphrex.io/news/uk-bist-openai-anthropic-binding-rules-aisi-voluntary-incident-reporting) takes evidence from four frontier labs and senior civil servants. Anthropic's Liam Booth-Smith tells MPs: "The rules should be binding." OpenAI agrees. Google DeepMind and Meta say no to new laws. Then Henry de Zoete, head of the AI Security Institute, says his outfit runs entirely on company goodwill and has no legal power to stop a dangerous model from shipping.

That last sentence is the one that matters. Not the bill. Not the seventy names on a letter.

**13 November 2026.** Second reading listed. Parliament's own page shows 27 November now. Private Members' Bills slip.

**Meanwhile.** [Anthropic has hired its first UK lobbying firm](https://cryptobriefing.com/anthropic-hires-first-uk-lobbying-firm/) and placed a former Downing Street chief of staff in its external affairs team. A company asking Parliament to bind it is also paying people to shape what the binding says. That's not surprising. That's just how it works.

---

Here is what a ban actually needs: a body that can read a model before it ships and hold the door shut if the model fails. The AI Security Institute cannot do that. It said so in October, in a committee, on the record.

So the bill proposes to ban something that an institution without a legal hold cannot stop. And two of the labs asking for binding rules are the same labs paying staff in Westminster to draft what those rules look like.

The seventy MPs got the problem right. They got the lever wrong.
