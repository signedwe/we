Your courgette looks terrible. Yellow patches, white powder, one corner going brown. You search "why is my courgette dying" and get fourteen articles describing every disease in equal detail, none of them looking at your actual plant. That's the bit AI can fix.

Here's what you'll have at the end: a name for what's wrong, a treatment plan, and a rough idea of whether the plant can be saved.

**What you need.** A phone and your suffering plant. [PictureThis](https://www.picturethisai.com/) has a free Health Check feature. Claude or ChatGPT with photo upload work too if you already use those.

**How long.** Under five minutes for a diagnosis.

**Step 1.** Get close to the worst-affected part and shoot it. Not the whole plant from a metre away. The leaf underside is where the evidence lives: spider mites, fungal spores, early rot. Use natural light. Aim at the edge of a damaged patch, not the middle.

*Say this to Claude or ChatGPT:* "Here's a photo of my [plant type]. Something's wrong with the leaves. What disease, pest or deficiency is this? How confident are you, and what should I do?"

**Step 2.** Upload it. In PictureThis, tap the camera icon and choose Health Check rather than Identify. It returns a name, a confidence score, and a treatment list. Claude and ChatGPT accept the photo directly in the message.

**Step 3.** Ask a follow-up. "Could this be a nutrient deficiency instead?" or "Is it fixable today?" The first answer is pattern-matching from your photo. The second draws on things you can add: how much you've been watering, what the soil is, how long the damage has been spreading.

**What it costs.** PictureThis gives two free Health Checks a day. Claude and ChatGPT's free tiers handle photos. Free for occasional use.

**Where it goes wrong.** A [Michigan State University plant diagnostics evaluation](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/plant-identification-theres-an-app-for-that-actually-several) tested identification apps in 2025. [PictureThis came first at 76% accuracy](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/plant-identification-theres-an-app-for-that-actually-several); ChatGPT placed second at 54%. One wrong answer in four on common species. Blurry shots in bad light make it worse. [Rare diseases may not be in the database at all](https://www.omdena.com/blog/ai-crop-disease-detection). When a plant has two things wrong at once, all of them struggle.

This is a first opinion. A garden centre, or someone who can crouch down and look properly, is the second.

**The thing it does that YouTube can't.** A video about powdery mildew shows you somebody else's powdery mildew. This looks at your leaf, your particular shade of wrong, and says what it sees. That's it. That's the difference.
