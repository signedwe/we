It came in a pot with no label. It's been sitting there for two years, getting larger, quietly judging you.

By the end of this afternoon, it has a name. You'll understand what it wants and whether that trailing bit draped over the radiator is slowly killing it.

The old way: reverse-image-search a photo. Wade through forty forum threads. Wait for someone in the Netherlands to say *Epipremnum aureum*. The new way: upload the photo to Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini and ask it. [For common houseplants, accuracy sits at 85 to 95 per cent from a single clear photo](https://www.theplantindex.com/articles/ai-chatbots-plant-identification-test). That covers most things people actually own. The danger zone is cultivars. [ChatGPT and Claude both regularly invent plausible-sounding cultivar names with high confidence](https://www.theplantindex.com/articles/ai-chatbots-plant-identification-test), when the actual plant is something else entirely. Trust the species. Treat the variety name as decoration.

**Step 1.** Take three photos: the whole plant from a metre back, one leaf from above with the veins clear, and the same leaf flipped over. [The best approach combines a wide shot showing how the plant grows from the pot with a close-up of a mature leaf](https://futuresbytes.co.uk/how-ai-plant-identification-works-from-a-single-photo/). Natural light. No flash.

**Step 2.** Open Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini on your phone. Free tier works on all three. Attach the photos. Then type:

*Please identify this plant. It lives indoors in [your city], in [bright or low] indirect light, and it's about [height]. Give me your best species-level name, say how confident you are, and tell me which features you used to decide.*

The confidence question matters. It forces the assistant to flag when it's guessing. Skip it and you get a crisp answer whether it's earned or not.

**Step 3.** Once you have a name, ask a follow-up:

*If this is [species name], what are the three most common ways it dies in a UK flat? What does a happy one look like, and what does a struggling one look like?*

This is what no YouTube video gives you. A video describes the average. The assistant looks at your photo, your window, your flat, and answers that specific question.

[A specialist app like PictureThis gets around 75 to 80 per cent accuracy on common species](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/plant-identification-theres-an-app-for-that-actually-several). It then pushes you toward a subscription for care advice. Claude and ChatGPT hand over the care advice in the same message, free.

**What goes wrong.** [Cultivar-level hallucination is the biggest failure mode](https://www.theplantindex.com/articles/ai-chatbots-plant-identification-test). If the assistant says *Monstera deliciosa* and you can see the holes, believe it. If it says *Monstera deliciosa* Thai Constellation, that's a dressed-up guess. Species level is the useful output. Everything after the second Latin word is decorative.

Also: a stressed plant in dim light is harder to read. [Accuracy drops for damaged plants and poor photos](https://everyfreetool.com/blog/how-to-identify-any-plant-from-photo). Take the photo next to the window in daylight.

**What it costs.** Nothing. Free tier on all three assistants handles image uploads.

**How long.** Ten minutes once you have the photos.
