The country's largest sovereign AI data centre is a field in Essex with no electricity.

By the end of 2027, the UK government will have stopped calling any specific site its flagship sovereign AI data centre. When Loughton gets a real connection date before 2030, I was wrong.

**Autumn 2025.** The government names Nscale's planned Loughton site the UK's [largest sovereign AI data centre](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/microsoft-quits-nscale-s-essex-ai-data-center-deal-over-power-concerns). Goes into the AI strategy. Not labelled a hope.

**January 2026.** NESO, the body that runs Britain's electricity connections, [stops taking new applications](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329207/20261019/microsoft-exits-nscales-uk-flagship-grid-cant-supply-power-until-2030s-ipo-risk.htm) entirely. The queue isn't clearing. It's growing so fast the regulator shuts the door.

**February 2026.** About [140 data centre projects](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/27/datacenter_uk_grid_demand/) are waiting for a connection. Together they want 50 GW of power. Britain's peak demand that month: [45 GW](https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/27/datacenter_uk_grid_demand/). The queue wants more power than the whole country draws.

**March 2026.** [Ofgem counts around 315 projects](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329207/20261019/microsoft-exits-nscales-uk-flagship-grid-cant-supply-power-until-2030s-ipo-risk.htm) in the connection queue, wanting 73 GW between them.

**18 October 2026.** UK Power Networks tells Nscale that Loughton won't get its [90 MW feed](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329207/20261019/microsoft-exits-nscales-uk-flagship-grid-cant-supply-power-until-2030s-ipo-risk.htm) until the early-to-mid 2030s. Microsoft, the anchor tenant, [walks](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/oct/18/microsoft-drops-huge-ai-datacentre-project-in-essex-over-power-supply-concerns). The flagship loses its only named customer.

50,000 MW in the queue, divided by Loughton's 90 MW, is 555 sites the same size as Loughton. All waiting. None of the coverage ran that number. It should have.

Here's what the coverage gets wrong. This isn't a grid story. It's a queue story. The queue has a manager.

[NESO changed the rules in 2025](https://datacentreaxis.com/uk/intel/uk-grid-connection-queue/). Old rule: first come, first served. New rule: first ready, first connected. You now need planning consent, land rights, and money in place to keep your spot. NESO made the change because Ofgem found the queue was full of ghost projects. Bids that cost nothing to file, holding slots nobody planned to build.

The physics is real. The cables under south-east England can only carry so much, and stringing new ones takes years. But [Ofgem's own CURATE process](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329207/20261019/microsoft-exits-nscales-uk-flagship-grid-cant-supply-power-until-2030s-ipo-risk.htm), which closed in September 2026, exists to fix the ghost-bid problem. It's a written admission that the queue rules made the wait longer than the cables alone required.

The government called Loughton sovereign. Sovereign meant: Britain's own AI compute, no foreign dependence. What it turns out to mean: a site whose connection date sits with NESO, whose rules come from Ofgem, whose delay gets called a grid problem.

It's not. It's a label with no wire behind it.

[Nscale says it's looking at private power](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/oct/18/microsoft-drops-huge-ai-datacentre-project-in-essex-over-power-supply-concerns). Deals with nearby wind farms, batteries on site, demand response. These are real options, not desperation. But if a site billed as a national asset has to find its own electrons because the grid won't reach it, the sovereignty was the press release. Not the plan.
