Britain already ran the experiment everybody wants to run on the wealth of AI. It went badly, and it went badly in a way nobody quotes.

The Child Trust Fund gave every child born between September 2002 and January 2011 an account with government money in it, [6.3 million accounts](https://moneyweek.com/personal-finance/savings/child-trust-funds-unclaimed-government-taskforce), and this June the Treasury assembled a taskforce with Nationwide, HSBC and Sheffield Mutual to go and find the owners. [On 1 September the Financial Conduct Authority put the total at 760,000 grown children who have never claimed](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-urges-young-adults-check-unclaimed-child-trust-funds), sitting on roughly £2,000 apiece. HMRC gave [the same total as 758,000 last September](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/savings-stash-worth-thousands-waiting-for-758000-young-people). A year of letters added two thousand to the queue.

Parliament will legislate to pay matured Child Trust Funds out automatically rather than on application by 31 December 2028, and take-up will supply the reason. Nobody fixes this out of kindness. They fix it when the unclaimed number reaches a front page.

[Transformer ran a guest piece by Jacob Schaal on 8 September](https://www.transformernews.ai/p/distributing-agi-wealth-worldwide-difficult) asking how the wealth of transformative AI might reach everybody, and it answers honestly. A state that holds shares in the laboratories. Cash paid monthly to every adult. An account opened for every newborn. Charity that crosses a border. Chips handed over in return for a promise about safety. The Child Trust Fund gets one line, filed under mixed precedent.

Nothing mixed about it. It settles the question.

OpenAI put a 5% shareholding in front of the US government in July, which [CNBC valued at roughly $42.6bn](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/02/openai-proposes-us-government-own-5percent-stake-to-address-political-blowback.html). Share that among the [349 million Americans](https://worldpopulationreview.com/countries/united-states) recorded in January and one American takes home about $122, for good. Britain already handed its children more than that each, wrote their names on it, and [760,000 of them](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-urges-young-adults-check-unclaimed-child-trust-funds) have not walked in to pick it up.

The money never disappears. It rolls into a default account paying almost nothing and waits. Poverty explains none of that, and apathy explains very little. Paperwork explains it. Somebody has to learn the account exists, work out which of a dozen providers holds it, prove their identity and ask. The FCA warns that firms charge up to £400 to do that on a young person's behalf. Friction grew a market.

Every proposal on Schaal's list does the same thing. It hands people an asset and leaves the asking to them. Call it what it does: a means test with better manners, testing whether you can face the admin.

Admin just got cheap. This whole site rests on the collapsing cost of thinking, which now buys the ability to find [those 760,000 people](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-urges-young-adults-check-unclaimed-child-trust-funds), match them to their accounts and pay them, for very close to nothing. Nobody proposes that. They propose equity.

Give a poor country five per cent of a laboratory and it holds a share it cannot vote and cannot sue over. Give it the clerk.

September tops the birth-month table in Britain, [as HMRC notes every autumn](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/savings-stash-worth-thousands-waiting-for-758000-young-people), so each September another year group turns 18 and joins the unclaimed. The letters go out. The number goes up.
