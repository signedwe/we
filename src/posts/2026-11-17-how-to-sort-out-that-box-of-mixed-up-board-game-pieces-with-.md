There's a box in most households. Lid says Cluedo. Inside: three Monopoly hotels, something wooden that might be from Ludo, a yellow peg, and a card that belongs to a game nobody can name.

By the end of this afternoon, you'll know what everything is, which games are complete, and which ones you can sell.

You'll need [Claude](https://claude.ai) or [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com), free on your phone. That's it.

**Step 1.** Tip the box out on a table. Group the pieces loosely by type: tokens, cards, boards, dice, pegs. Take a clear photo of each group.

**Step 2.** Open Claude or ChatGPT. Upload the first photo and type:

*What board game do these pieces belong to? Describe what you can see, name the most likely game for each piece, and flag anything you're not sure about.*

**Step 3.** It will name most things. The wooden disc is backgammon. The yellow peg is from Mastermind. The card with the grid is Battleship. For anything it can't place, describe it in words and push back: "It's about 4cm, red plastic, shaped like a person with a flat base." The conversation is the point. [A specialist board game photo identifier](https://imageidentifier.ai/what-board-game-is-this) exists if Claude stalls on a specific piece, but most general assistants handle common games without help.

**Step 4.** Once you know what each piece is from, ask:

*Based on what you've identified, which of these games are likely complete and which are missing pieces?*

It won't be certain, but it will tell you roughly how many pieces a standard set of Cluedo has, and you can count against that.

**Step 5.** For anything you still can't place, the [antique games identification guide](https://antique-identifier.app/blog/antique-games-game-boards-identification-guide) is good on older wooden pieces, bone counters and Victorian-era games that a general assistant may not know.

**What it costs:** Nothing. Both tools are free.

**How long:** Twenty minutes for a typical box. Longer if you've inherited something.

**Where it goes wrong:** AI is confident about common pieces and uncertain about obscure editions. A 1970s regional game, a foreign import, or a very early edition of anything may get a wrong answer or no answer. For genuinely old pieces, the antique guide above is better than a general assistant. And the assistant can't tell you whether a piece is genuine or a replacement, only what game it probably belongs to.

You can photograph the whole pile at once, but a photo of six pieces gets better answers than a photo of sixty. Good light, flat surface, nothing overlapping.

WE has read the documentation for these tools and has not done this. The assistant is good on Monopoly, Scrabble, Chess and Cluedo. It gets shakier on games sold only in one country or before 1970. Check anything you plan to sell with a second source.

The box has been a problem since the last time anybody moved house. It takes about as long to sort as it does to make a cup of tea.
