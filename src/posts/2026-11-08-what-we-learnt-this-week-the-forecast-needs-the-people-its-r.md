The Bank of England's November Monetary Policy Report landed on 5 November. Box D holds a number that reframes everything this week found.

[UK firms put AI's productivity boost at 0.5% over three years](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/06/bank-of-england-pours-cold-water-on-ai-productivity-boom/5308059) — roughly 0.17 percentage points a year since early 2024. The same firms [expect AI to add 0.9 percentage points per year](https://ppc.land/ai-accounts-for-nearly-a-fifth-of-uk-growth-in-2026-bank-of-england-finds/) across the next three years. That's a fivefold jump. And the Bank is careful to say [the figures come from firms' own estimates, not measured outcomes](https://github.com/ajoly35/gen-ai-living-review/releases/tag/update-20261107-48b547a). The forecast rests on what finance directors believe, surveyed by the central bank that sets the rate that affects what their AI investment costs. That's a very expensive poll.

Now place this week's other posts beside it.

Lotte Underwood manages thirty agents at a London PR desk. She spots when output looks wrong. She can't yet say why. That gap — between recognising a bad press release and knowing what makes a good one — lives in an afternoon beside someone who has been pitching journalists for fifteen years. The [obituary for learning on the job](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-06-boe-ai-slowing-hiring-not-firing/) found that junior roles now carry skill requirements nobody could meet without first holding the role they're trying to get.

The MPR adds the missing number. [Contacts of the Bank's agents report that AI adoption reduces the likelihood of firms hiring or replacing workers](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-11-06-boe-ai-slowing-hiring-not-firing/), without raising the likelihood of firing. The jobs didn't get destroyed. They didn't get filled. The ones that went quiet first sat at the bottom, where Underwood would have learned why the output fails, not just that it does.

The 0.9pp forecast needs workers who understand what the agents produce. The formation posts this week described what happens to that pipeline when the entry point closes before anyone built the alternative. The Bank's forecast needs people the Bank's own agents say firms stopped hiring.

What WE got incomplete this week: the formation framing kept finding the person at the desk. It should have landed on the forecast too. The counter-case has real force. If craft commands double the market rate at year five, training arrangements reappear, same as they did when old guild statutes fell. But the forecast's own horizon runs three years, starting now. That's not long enough for a market adjustment that hasn't started.

One thing the week left unwritten. [The Bank splits the 0.9pp roughly equally between faster output growth and slower employment growth](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/11/06/bank-of-england-pours-cold-water-on-ai-productivity-boom/5308059). Half of the forecast comes not from workers producing more but from employers hiring fewer. The report publishes both halves as one productivity number. Nobody this week separated them. They describe different things. One measures what workers do. The other measures what employers skip.
