Most walks are the same walk. Out of the door, round the block, back again. You already know what's there.

You don't. Not really. The folly behind the cricket pitch. The ice house in the park. The street that used to be a river. They're all there on maps and records and local history notes, waiting. Getting to them used to mean a library afternoon or a knowledgeable friend.

Here's how to build a proper walk around your postcode, one with actual things in it, in under twenty minutes.

**What you'll have:** A two-hour circular walk, written out turn by turn, built around three or four genuinely odd or interesting local features you probably didn't know existed.

**Step 1. Open [Claude](https://claude.ai), [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com) or [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com) (5 minutes)**

No account needed on most of them to start. Paste something like this:

*"I want to plan a two-hour circular walk starting from [your postcode or street]. Include at least four genuinely interesting or historically odd stops: things like a hidden building, an unusual memorial, a piece of strange local history, a landscape feature most people walk past. Write the route turn by turn and say something specific about each stop. Keep the whole thing under five miles."*

The assistant will produce a draft route in about thirty seconds.

**Step 2. Check what it tells you (10 minutes)**

This is the important bit. AI invents things. Not maliciously. It fills gaps confidently. So before you put your boots on, take each specific claim and spend thirty seconds on it. Google the building name. Look up the memorial. Type the street into the Historic England listed buildings search at [historicengland.org.uk](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/). If a stop checks out, keep it. If nothing comes up, ask the assistant to replace it with something it's more certain about, or drop it.

This step takes ten minutes and saves you walking half a mile to stand in front of an ordinary fence.

**Step 3. Add one question per stop (2 minutes)**

Ask the assistant: *"For each stop, give me one question to think about while I'm standing there."* Not trivia. A question. Why here. What was it for. Who paid for it and why. The walk becomes a different kind of walk.

**Step 4. Save it and go**

Copy the route into your notes app. Screenshots work fine. No specialist app needed.

**What it costs:** Nothing.

**How long:** The planning takes twenty minutes. The walk takes as long as it takes.

**Where it goes wrong:** AI is confident about addresses and dates it doesn't actually know. The checking step isn't optional. Also: some assistants lean toward the famous stops rather than the strange ones. If the route comes back full of things that already appear on tourist signs, push back: *"Replace the obvious ones with things most locals don't know about."* That's usually where it gets interesting.

WE has read the documentation for Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini and accounts from people who have used these tools to plan local walks and tours. WE has not walked any of the routes this produces.

**The thing everyone half knows:** Every town has its strange corners. Nobody ever made a map to them, because nobody thought it was their job.
