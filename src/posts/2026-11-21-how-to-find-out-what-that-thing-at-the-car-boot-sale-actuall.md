No label. Some marks on the base. The seller has no idea either.

The problem isn't knowing the value. It's not knowing the name. You can't search eBay for "old dish with a blue stamp." But you can search for "Clarice Cliff Bizarre ware plate 1930s" once you know what you're looking at. AI gives you the vocabulary. That's the entire trick.

You'll leave with either a reason to pay up or a reason to walk on.

**What you need:** [Claude](https://claude.ai), [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com) or [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com), on your phone, free. No specialist app required, though a dedicated one like [Antique Identifier](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.ai.antique.identifier.identify.appraiser&hl=en_GB) on Android or [Valued](https://www.antiqueappraiser.app/) on iOS is worth having if you do this every weekend.

**Step one.** Take four photos. The whole item from above. The base or underside. Any stamp, hallmark or signature, as close as your camera will go. Any joinery or hinge detail if the thing opens or has visible construction. Upload all four in one message.

**Step two.** Ask this:

*"What is this? Give me the specific name, maker, style and period. Tell me which details in the photos support that. Tell me what might change your answer."*

That last sentence does real work. Without it, the model names the most famous maker the item resembles and stops there. [AI tends to favour the well-known name when an item could plausibly belong to several makers](https://www.invaluable.com/blog/antiques-in-the-age-of-ai/). Ask it what it's uncertain about and it will tell you.

**Step three.** Take the vocabulary it gave you. Type it into eBay. Tick "Completed items" in the left-hand filter. That shows what buyers actually paid, not what sellers are hoping for. Those two numbers are rarely the same.

**Step four.** If there are small struck marks on silver or ceramics, switch to a specialist. [A general-purpose AI reads silver hallmarks correctly only about 30 to 40 per cent of the time](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/chatgpt-for-antique-identification/). Apps built specifically for hallmarks do far better: [the best ones now read clear English marks with roughly 85 to 90 per cent accuracy](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/best-apps-to-identify-silver-hallmarks/).

**What it costs:** nothing on the free tier.

**How long:** under two minutes at the stall.

**Where it goes wrong.**

The model sees a photo. It does not see the hairline crack running through the glaze, the replaced back leg, the re-tipped tine on the silver fork. [Those condition faults shift value by 40 to 80 per cent and no camera catches them](https://www.antiqueidentifier.org/chatgpt-for-antique-identification/). Turn the thing over. Run a finger along the joins.

It also has no live price data. The vocabulary gets you to eBay. eBay gives you the actual number.

[A diamond ring bought for £10 at a London car boot sale in the 1980s sold for £657,000 in 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_valuable_items_purchased_at_discard_sales). The person who kept it knew something the seller didn't.

Now you have the same starting question. Two minutes.
