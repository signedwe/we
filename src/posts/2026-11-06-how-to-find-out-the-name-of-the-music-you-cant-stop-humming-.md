You've been humming it for three days. You heard it in a café, or at the end of a film, or through someone else's wall. You can do the first eight notes. You have no idea what it's called.

Shazam needs a recording. YouTube needs a search term. Neither one helps when what you have is a feeling and four seconds of melody.

AI takes the description instead. You type the instruments, the mood, the tempo, whatever grabbed you. It names the piece. Then it tells you *why* it sounds the way it does: who wrote it, when, what was happening in their life, what that interval in the third bar actually is and why it makes your chest do the thing it does. That second part is the bit a music teacher used to charge for by the hour.

**What you'll need**

Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini. All free on standard tiers.

**Step 1: Describe it, don't search for it**

Open your assistant and type everything you know. Don't worry about musical language. Describe the instruments, the mood, the tempo, where you heard it, what it felt like. If you can do the melody in syllables (da-da-DUM-da) or tap out the rhythm (short short LONG short), do that. The more you give, the better the match.

Try this:

*It's something with strings. Slow. Sounds sad but not quite. There's a repeated rising phrase that feels like a question. I think it's classical. Maybe 20th century.*

**Step 2: Narrow it down**

If you get a list, describe which one you'd choose. Then ask: *What was happening in this composer's life when they wrote it? What technique creates that specific feeling?*

**Step 3: Go further**

Once you have a name, ask:

*If I love this, what are three others I'd regret having missed?*

You are now inside a conversation a music teacher used to charge for.

If you want to listen right away, paste the title into YouTube or Spotify. For sheet music, [IMSLP](https://imslp.org) has most of it free and in the open.

**What it costs**

Nothing. Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini are all free on standard tiers.

**Where it goes wrong**

Too vague and you'll get half of classical music back. Push harder on the specific thing that hooked you: the interval, the instrument, the tempo change, the moment it shifted. The AI is matching your description, not reading your mind.

It also confabulates. When you get a name, check it on YouTube before you get attached. If it's wrong, say so and describe what's different. One more sentence usually fixes it.
