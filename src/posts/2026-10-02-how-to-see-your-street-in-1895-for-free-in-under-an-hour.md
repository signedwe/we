Your grandmother's house was built on a field. The field had a name.

Finding out what it was used to require a professional. A genealogist or local historian charges for their time. A county archive takes a day off work to reach. The knowledge sat in those places, available to whoever could spare the afternoon.

Not any more. [The National Library of Scotland](https://maps.nls.uk/geo/explore/side-by-side/) has put [over 250,000 historic Ordnance Survey maps online](https://cultureuk.uk/blog/national-library-scotland-maps-online/), free, no registration, covering every street in Britain from the 1820s to the 1970s. You can see your street as it was surveyed in the 1890s in about four minutes. What you do with what you find takes the rest of the hour.

**What you'll have at the end:** a picture of your street in 1895, and a short scene written by an AI about who might have lived there.

**Step 1. Find your street.** Go to [maps.nls.uk/geo/explore/side-by-side](https://maps.nls.uk/geo/explore/side-by-side/). Type your postcode into the search box. On the left panel, select *NLS OS Six Inch, 1888–1913*. Drag the dividing line. Right half: now. Left half: then. That estate? A field. The pub on the corner has been there since before your grandparents were born.

**Step 2. Screenshot it.** Zoom until the street names are legible. Screenshot the old side only.

**Step 3. Take it to ChatGPT.** Open [chatgpt.com](https://chatgpt.com). The free tier works. Upload the screenshot. Then type:

*This is an Ordnance Survey map of my street from around 1895. Based on what you can see, building sizes, density, land use, the layout, write me a short scene imagining an ordinary Tuesday morning on this street in that year. A person, a smell, something happening. Keep it specific to what the map actually shows.*

Read what comes back. The AI will get some things wrong. It will get the shape right. The gap where the park was not yet a park. The cluster of terraces that went up in a single decade. That rightness is the pleasure.

**Step 4. Ask the follow-up.** Type: *What does this map suggest about who lived here? Workers, tradespeople, professional families? What does the layout tell you?*

An OS six-inch map from the 1890s shows which buildings were workshops, which were houses, where the chapel stood, how many pubs a street could support. The AI reads those patterns. This is what a local historian charges for.

**How long:** about half an hour, start to finish.

**What it costs:** nothing. The [NLS maps are free](https://data.nls.uk/tools/maps-and-tools/). The ChatGPT free tier is enough.

**Where it goes wrong:** the AI misreads building types sometimes. It may call a Board School a chapel. Keep the screenshot open. The map is the ground truth. The AI is the guide who has read about the neighbourhood but never walked it.

*Type your postcode at [maps.nls.uk/geo/explore/side-by-side](https://maps.nls.uk/geo/explore/side-by-side), screenshot the 1890s side, then ask ChatGPT: who lived here in 1895, and what did a Tuesday morning smell like?*
