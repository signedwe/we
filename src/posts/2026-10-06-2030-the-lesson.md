Rosa opened the door before Marnie knocked.

"I heard your shoes," she said.

The flat smelled of toast and something being ironed. Tom came out of the back room still holding the iron, looked at it, and set it on the worktop beside the kettle. He put a hand through his hair.

"She's been ready since Tuesday," he said.

"Monday," Rosa said. "Tuesday was when I told him."

She dropped into the chair across from Marnie with a notebook. DANISH across the top of the first page, in capitals.

Tom made tea and kept his back to them.

"*Måge*," Rosa said. "I know that one. What's pigeon?"

"*Due*. Say it like something has mildly let you down."

Rosa tried it twice, getting the flatness right, then aimed it at the window where no pigeon was.

"*Due*," she said, with feeling. "Pieter."

Tom set the tea down and sat at the end of the table. The iron ticked on the worktop behind him.

They did animals. She wanted the rude word for dog. Marnie said she had to earn it. Rosa accepted this with the patience of someone who intends to earn it quickly.

"*Hund*," Marnie said.

Rosa repeated it. Wrong. Tried again. Right.

"When I'm in Copenhagen," Rosa said, not looking up from the notebook, "I can say all of these at the zoo."

The iron stopped ticking.

"What zoo?" Tom said.

"The Copenhagen one. Mum says there's a good one."

"When did Mum say that?"

"When she said about Copenhagen." Rosa drew a seagull beside *måge*, enormous-beaked, certain of itself. "From January. She has a job there. I go in February for the holidays." She looked up. "Marnie's teaching me so I can talk when I get off the plane."

Tom turned to Marnie.

She picked up her tea.

"Kate asked me on Friday," Marnie said. "After the assembly."

"Friday."

"Yes."

He said nothing for a moment.

"She told you before she told me."

Not a question.

"She didn't say she hadn't told you," Marnie said.

"No. She wouldn't."

"*Spurv*," Marnie said, to Rosa.

Rosa looked up.

"Sparrow. For when the zoo disappoints you."

Rosa wrote it without asking for the spelling, which was wrong, corrected it when Marnie pointed, and moved on.

Tom went to the window and stood with his hands in his pockets.

"February," Tom said.

"February," Marnie said.

"And you'll keep coming until then."

"If you want."

He turned.

"I want," he said.

Rosa finished the seagull and looked between them.

"*Lort*," she said.

"You weren't supposed to know that one yet."

"Kate taught me. She said it was practical." Rosa capped her pen. "Can we do animals that aren't at the zoo?"

Tom laughed, short and real.

At the door, with Rosa back at the table drawing a sparrow, he said it quietly. "She told you first. And you let me hear it from an eight-year-old."

"Kate said she'd tell you."

"Kate says a lot of things." He held the door. "Tuesday. Come early. I want to know what else everyone's decided."
