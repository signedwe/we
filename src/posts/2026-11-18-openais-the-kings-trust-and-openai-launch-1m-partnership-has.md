The company that built the tool is now funding lessons on how to use it. At £40 a head.

**16 November 2026.** [OpenAI donates £1m to The King's Trust](https://openai.com/index/the-kings-trust-and-openai/) to train up to 25,000 young people in AI skills for work over 18 months. The charity the late King founded in 1976, which has helped 1.3 million young people since, will go first to those not in school, college or work. Nationally recognised qualifications are coming. Employers, AI specialists and young people are being asked what skills they'll need.

The number isn't in the press release. £1,000,000 divided by 25,000 is £40 per person.

The King's Trust has fifty years of knowing how to find young people nobody else reaches. Its staff, its contacts, its local offices: real and worth something. But £40 isn't a training budget. It's the size of a training budget on a poster.

**18 November 2026.** [Ofqual launches a campaign](https://superpowerdaily.com/posts/ofqual-warns-ai-cheating-can-cost-students-entire-qualifications-as-campaign-begins) warning students across England: use AI in assessed coursework without declaring it and you could lose the whole GCSE or A-level. "Life-altering," says the regulator. There were 100 proven AI plagiarism cases in summer 2025. Smart glasses are now on the watch list.

The seventeen-year-old in Stoke or Sunderland, not in work and not in college, now has two official messages from the same week. The state: use AI wrong and lose everything. The company: here are forty pounds to learn it.

**What the press release leaves out.** No target for how many people get jobs. No plan for an outside review. The employers helping to design the new qualifications don't have to hire anyone who holds them. The "AI expertise" OpenAI contributes alongside the cash has no price on it, no measure, no consequences if it never shows up.

By end of May 2028, when the 18 months close, there will be a qualification and no published study of what anyone did next. I'll be wrong if the King's Trust puts one out before then.

**What the deal produces.** Forty pounds per person. A press release in which the Trust's UK chief executive, Jonathan Townsend, says the goal is to help young people shape the future rather than be shaped by it. He means it. The Trust will work hard with this money.

The problem isn't the charity. It's the price. At £40 per head, what gets bought isn't a training programme. It's the sentence that says one exists.

A qualification no employer is contracted to ask for, at forty pounds a head, in the same week the state warns young people not to use the tool unsupervised.
