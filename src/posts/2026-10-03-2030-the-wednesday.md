Fiction. These people are invented.

She chose a Wednesday because she had no court.

T.'s block was off the Old Kent Road, one of the towers that went up after 2024 when the planning authority started approving everything above six storeys and stopped apologising for it. She had never seen it in daylight. She bought a flat white from the place on the corner and stood outside with it and waited, which was something she had learned to do in courtrooms and which cost her roughly the same amount each time.

He came out at eight forty carrying a bag with the council tree on it.

He looked at her. The bag did not drop.

"M."

"I won't be long," she said.

She had rehearsed four versions of this on the bus. None of them had started with *I won't be long*, and now she had said it and could not unsay it, which was already more honest than any of the four versions.

"I'm not going to keep doing this," she said. "I wanted to say it to your face because I've been saying it in my head for two weeks and that turns out not to count."

A woman walked past with a dog on a short lead. The dog looked at them both with the frank interest of an animal that has no stake in any of it.

"P. doesn't know," T. said.

"He knows."

Silence.

"He hasn't said anything. He won't. That's just how he works." She looked at the council bag. "Rosa's assembly is in November. We're both coming. It's already in the diary."

"Okay."

"One more thing."

He waited.

"You were the person I thought about. For years. I don't know what to do with that now except say it, because I didn't on the boat and I didn't in the tunnel and I'm not going to be someone who has three chances at a sentence and misses all of them."

He said nothing. He was looking at the street behind her with the expression of a man trying to locate something he has put down somewhere and cannot find.

"That's it," she said. "That's everything."

She set the flat white on the low wall beside her. She had not drunk any of it and she did not take it with her.

She walked back toward the main road without turning. Behind her: a bus, a window open somewhere, someone's radio on a kitchen sill. The ordinary sounds of eight forty on a Wednesday in October, which she kept saying was Thursday and which would matter later when she tried to remember the exact day this had happened.

At the corner the pedestrian signal was red. She waited. It changed. She crossed.

Thirty-eight minutes before London Bridge.

On the concourse a man was trying to fold a pram one-handed while a voice from his lapel talked him through it in a tone of total patience. The pram was not folding. He said something short and rude to the lapel, which absorbed it without comment, and then the pram folded, and the man picked it up and walked away still talking, already on to something else.

She watched him go.

P. had put the assembly in the diary in November and coloured it and that was the shape of what she had chosen. She could see it from the concourse: a school hall, Rosa doing the poem with the actions, P. beside her clapping at the right moment. Not a tragedy. A life of nine years that would keep going, with the other thing said out loud first, so she would always know she had said it. That was the only point of saying it at all.
