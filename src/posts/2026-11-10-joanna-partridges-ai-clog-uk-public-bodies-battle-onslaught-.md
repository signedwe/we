By the end of 2027, no UK ombudsman will require a complainant to state what they want before the form submits. Attachment caps will exist. That one field will not. If it appears, I was wrong.

Here's what that field would fix, and why the volume story misses it.

[Between April and June 2026](https://www.personneltoday.com/hr/tribunal-backlog-hits-70000-as-system-struggles-to-keep-up/), tribunals received 14,000 single claims and disposed of 6,100. The open caseload hit 70,000, up 51 percent on the year. Divide 70,000 by 6,100 and you get 11.5 quarters to clear what already exists. Nearly three years, with nothing new arriving. New claims are rising 28 percent year on year. The queue doesn't clear. It compounds.

**1. The complaint lost its centre.**
A formally correct complaint does three things: describes a grievance, cites relevant law, states what the person wants to happen. AI produces the first two fluently. The third requires the person to have already sat with a question they often haven't answered. [Complaints are "worded to sound intimidating but forget to set out what solution the complainant wants,"](https://www.aol.co.uk/articles/ai-clog-uk-public-bodies-080036000.html) one council official told the Guardian. The shell of a complaint arrived. The person who knows what they want was not reliably inside it.

**2. What the two-page letter was doing that nobody noticed.**
Writing two pages yourself meant you'd thought hard enough to know what you were after. That effort was a rough signal of intent. The 200-page AI brief destroys it. The caseworker in Enfield, who has [one pot of money and no extra for more complaints](https://finwire.io/news/world-news/ai-clog-uk-public-bodies-battle-onslaught-of-chatbot-generated-complaints), now reads far enough into every letter to find out whether anyone's home. Some run to 200 pages. [One hit 200.](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/uk-public-bodies-hit-by-surge-of-ai-generated-complaints)

**3. This hurts the person with a real complaint.**
The person with a genuine grievance about their social care package joins the same queue as ten people who pasted a situation into a chatbot at midnight. [Half the emails in one Labour MP's office are now some form of AI.](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/uk-public-bodies-hit-by-surge-of-ai-generated-complaints) Staff describe themselves as exhausted. The filter is coming: attachment caps, intake forms, length limits. When it arrives, it hits every letter equally. Including the ones that matter.

**4. 'Everyone's got a free lawyer' is only half the story.**
What a junior solicitor produces before the partner asks the client what they actually want is not a case. It's a brief. The partner's question turns it into a case. The AI is the junior solicitor. There is no partner. So what lands on the caseworker's desk in Enfield is a well-structured account of a situation, missing the one sentence that makes it answerable: I want X.

**5. The fix is one field.**
Not attachment caps. Not AI summarisers on the receiving end. One required field on every complaint portal, before submission completes: *In one sentence, what do you want to happen?* It doesn't stop anyone complaining. It makes the complaint answerable. The portal can offer examples. The AI can help draft it. The person still has to pick one.

[The Employment Appeal Tribunal has already issued guidance on AI use in cases.](https://hotminute.co.uk/2026/10/12/employment-claims-surge-28-as-uk-workers-use-ai-to-turn-on-their-bosses/) It tells claimants to check their citations. Good advice. The 70,000 cases already waiting in the queue weren't stopped by bad citations.

They were stopped by six thousand disposal slots and fourteen thousand new arrivals. Every quarter.
