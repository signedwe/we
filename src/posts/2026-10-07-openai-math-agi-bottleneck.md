People now hold up mathematics, not machines.

Yesterday [OpenAI released 722 manuscripts](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/) from a model it hasn't released, drawn from [about 4,000 problems it attempted](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/openai-largest-math-release-lean-proofs). Each result cost roughly three hours of the model's thinking. [This morning's post](https://signedwe.github.io/we/posts/2026-10-07-openai-722-math-manuscripts/) asked who pays to check them. This one takes the bigger question: does this look like general intelligence?

Maths makes the cleanest test there is. Nobody had solved these problems, so the training data held no answers to copy. That makes 372 families of new results new reasoning, not recall, across number theory, complexity and physics.

And the skill travels. Code needs long, exact chains of reasoning, and so does AI research. Last week [twenty-two researchers, Hinton and Bengio among them, warned](https://signedwe.github.io/we/posts/2026-09-28-intelligence-explosion-who-employs-them/) that once models can do AI research, the research workforce multiplies. A model that clears an open problem in an afternoon takes a step down that road.

Now the other side. Most attempts failed: about 4,000 problems went in and 372 families came out. OpenAI chose what to publish and hasn't said how hard the successes were. [By one count, three in four results lack a machine-checked proof](https://cellcog.ai/blog/openai-math-results/). And maths gives a machine the easiest case, because an answer comes out right or wrong and a model can practise against that. Running a ward, raising a child, fixing a boiler: most of life offers no such clean signal. Nobody agrees on a test for "general", so no single release settles it.

What the release does settle: where the work now waits. For three hundred years maths ran short of proofs. A handful of people produced them, years each. The machine now produces them by the hundred. What runs short now: people to check the proofs, people to understand them, people to decide which of 722 matter. Nobody has hired for that at this scale.

General intelligence will probably arrive like this. Not on one day when the machines get clever, but field by field, as the queue moves from the machine's desk to ours. Maths got there first because anyone can check its answers. Fields whose answers resist checking will follow, and we'll find them harder to keep up with.
