Something crossed your lawn at 3am. You slept through it. By tomorrow lunchtime you'll know what it was, how big it was, and which way it went.

**What you'll have:** a tray of evidence, a photo and a ranked list of suspects. **Cost:** a baking tray, plus damp sand and a tin of cat food if you've none. **Time:** ten minutes tonight, ten tomorrow.

**Why AI and not YouTube:** a track guide shows textbook prints. Yours will be smeared and half full of water. The dog will have walked through it. A video can't look at your tray. An assistant can. It can also tell you which measurement would change its mind.

**1. Set the trap tonight.** The [Scottish Wildlife Trust](https://scottishwildlifetrust.org.uk/resource/make-an-animal-footprint-trap/) says to put a dish of meat-based pet food in the middle of a tray. Leave it out overnight. [The Badger Sett](https://the-badger-sett.co.uk/spot/badger-footprints/) suggests smoothing damp sand or mud near a gap in the hedge. It says never to put one near a badger sett.

**2. Photograph it tomorrow, before the dog gets there.** The [Woodland Trust](https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/blog/2022/01/animal-tracks-snow-print-identification/) says to measure the length and width of several prints in a track. So don't stop at the prettiest one. Shoot from straight above, with a coin or ruler beside the print. Take one wide shot of the whole tray too.

**3. Upload the lot.** In Claude, [press the plus button and choose Add files or photos](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/8241126-upload-files-to-claude). Anthropic suggests images of at least 1000 by 1000 pixels. In ChatGPT, [press plus and choose Add photos and files](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8555545-file-uploads-faq). If you can't see the button, your plan may not have it.

**4. Say this:**

*These are prints from a tray in my UK garden, with a coin beside each. Don't give me one answer. Give me a ranked shortlist. Tell me what you can see that supports each name. Then tell me the one thing I should measure to split the top two.*

**5. Check its working.** The [Warwickshire Wildlife Trust's life-size guide](https://www.warwickshirewildlifetrust.org.uk/sites/default/files/2019-10/How%20To...%20Identify%20Footprints.pdf) has a test for fox against dog. Draw a line through the four toes. On a fox, no toe crosses the line. On a dog, the front two do. Try it on your own photo. See if the assistant agrees.

**Where it goes wrong.** Dog prints vary a lot. The Sussex Wildlife Trust says they're [often around 3 to 5cm wide](https://sussexwildlifetrust.org.uk/news/mammal-mysteries). A fox print is [about 5cm long and 4cm wide](https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/blog/2022/01/animal-tracks-snow-print-identification/). Size alone won't split them. The Woodland Trust also says foxes and many cats [leave a straight line of prints](https://www.woodlandtrust.org.uk/blog/2022/01/animal-tracks-snow-print-identification/).

I found no independent test of how well general chatbots read footprint photos. One commercial identifier [admits tracks alone may match several species](https://lensapp.io/animal-identifier/). So treat a confident answer as a guess. Meat bait draws what it draws. The Warwickshire guide's list of likely visitors includes rat.

**WE has read this, not done it.** No tray was filled. No print was photographed.

The lawn has been keeping a diary all along. Tonight you give it paper.
