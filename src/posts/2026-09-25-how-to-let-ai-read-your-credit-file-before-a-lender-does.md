Your credit file has been read by strangers. You probably haven't.

[Three agencies hold it: Experian, Equifax and TransUnion.](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/everyday-money/credit/how-to-check-your-credit-report) Each may hold different information. [You have a legal right to see all three, free, whenever you ask.](https://legalclarity.org/how-to-check-your-credit-score-for-free-in-the-uk) It's been that way since 1974. Most people don't know.

At the end of this: all three reports read by AI, with a plain-English list of anything suspect. Cost: nothing.

**Step 1. Pull all three statutory reports.**

These are the free ones. No trial. No subscription.

- [TransUnion](https://www.transunionstatreport.co.uk/CreditReport/AboutYou): register, verify, download.
- [Equifax](https://www.equifax.co.uk/products/credit/statutory-report): register at myEquifax, download. They ask for card details. [They won't charge you for the statutory version.](https://www.checkmyfile.com/help-centre/articles/what-is-a-statutory-credit-report-what-to-know-and-how-to-find-yours)
- [Experian](https://www.experian.co.uk/consumer/statutory-report.html): use stat.experian.co.uk, not the main app. [The app shows your score, not your full report.](https://pocketwise.co.uk/debt/credit-scores/how-to-check-credit-score-free/)

No time for all three today? [ClearScore](https://www.clearscore.com) gives you Equifax free. [MSE Credit Club](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/loans/check-free-credit-report/) gives you TransUnion.

**Step 2. Redact before anything touches AI.**

Black out: account numbers, date of birth, National Insurance number, address, phone. Keep: creditor names, balances, payment history, account status, dates opened, credit limits. Use a PDF redaction tool. Highlighting can be undone.

**Step 3. Paste into Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini.**

Use this prompt:

*You are a UK consumer credit expert. I've removed my personal identifiers. Please list every entry that looks wrong, outdated or suspicious: accounts I don't recognise, defaults older than six years, hard searches I haven't authorised. Then write a short letter I can send to dispute the most serious issue. Plain English throughout.*

**Step 4. Check every flag yourself before acting.**

[AI spots patterns fast. It also gets things wrong.](https://www.thecreditpeople.com/credit-score/can-ai-fix-your-credit-score-for-free) Cross-check anything it flags against your own statements first.

**Step 5. Challenge real errors.**

Each agency has an online dispute form. [Name the account, say what's wrong, say what it should say. Attach evidence.](https://moneyguide.org.uk/how-to/dispute-credit-report-error/) [The agency must respond within 28 days.](https://www.clearscore.com/learn/credit-score-and-report/how-to-fix-credit-report-mistakes-fast) While they investigate, the entry is flagged and won't affect new applications.

If they refuse: [complain to the ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/credit/) or the [Financial Ombudsman.](https://www.fightingback.uk/discover/fix-credit-report-errors) Both free. [The Ombudsman can order corrections and pay compensation.](https://www.fightingback.uk/discover/fix-credit-report-errors)

**Where it goes wrong.**

Paying a debt doesn't wipe it. [A correctly recorded default stays on your file for six years from the default date.](https://civilhelp.co.uk/debt-money/credit-reference-disputes) It shows as 'satisfied.' It stays. Don't dispute a correct entry.

The most common error clients miss isn't a fraud account. It's a financial link to an old partner. A closed joint account, still tying two credit histories together. The AI won't flag it as wrong. The data is accurate. The relationship isn't. The form to fix it is a Notice of Disassociation. Almost nobody knows it exists.

**The number nobody prints.**

Each statutory report covers six years of history. Three agencies, three reports: 18 years of data, from three independent sources, none required to agree. Most people check zero in a given year.

The file belongs to you. [You can check it as often as you like.](https://www.iehub.co.uk/guides/your-credit-file-explained/) Checking never harms your score.
