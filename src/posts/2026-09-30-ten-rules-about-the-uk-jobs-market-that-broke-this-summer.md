The safe jobs went first.

**10. The junior role is training.** It was. [Vacancies for AI-exposed roles fell 37% since ChatGPT launched in 2022](https://www.mexc.com/it-IT/news/560155), against 26% for other jobs. The junior doesn't learn by doing now. They apply into silence.

**9. Software developers are vulnerable.** Were. [By mid-2026, software-developer vacancies had risen 14%](https://fortune.com/2026/08/02/ai-two-speed-job-market-uk-software-manufacturing-experience-entry-level/). Almost all of it was senior roles tied to AI. The junior developer is still underwater. The senior one is fine.

**8. Blue-collar work is most at risk.** [Manufacturing postings are down 58% since June 2022](https://fortune.com/2026/08/02/ai-two-speed-job-market-uk-software-manufacturing-experience-entry-level/). Not AI. Energy costs, supply chains, and a tax shock in April. The robot took the blame for the government's bill.

**7. More output means more hiring.** [UK firms posted 10% fewer vacancies in July 2026 than in January 2025](https://fortune.com/2026/08/02/ai-two-speed-job-market-uk-software-manufacturing-experience-entry-level/), while output kept climbing. The economy made more with fewer people. The extra went nowhere near a payslip.

**6. AI exposure predicts job loss.** [Bloomberg Economics found that vacancies in AI-exposed roles were already falling before ChatGPT launched](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-16/ai-wrongly-blamed-for-britain-s-job-losses-analysis-suggests) and have risen since summer 2024. The link looked like cause. It wasn't, or not cleanly.

**5. Retraining fixes it.** [Adult learners in further education fell from 3.3 million in 2012-13 to 1.8 million in 2024-25](https://post.parliament.uk/research-briefings/post-pn-0769/). The country most in need of new skills spent a decade making them harder to get. The badge costs nothing. The time does.

**4. The new jobs replace the lost ones.** [Gravitee forecasts 100,000 AI agents in the UK workforce by end of 2026](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ai-agents-set-to-replace-thousands-of-uk-jobs-by-end-of-2026). [65% of large employers planned to cut staff before year's end](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/ai-agents-set-to-replace-thousands-of-uk-jobs-by-end-of-2026). The new roles want five years of experience. The people who lost their jobs have two.

**3. Youth unemployment is a youth problem.** [Youth unemployment in London hit around 25%](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/ai-put-jobs-of-uks-highest-paid-workers-at-risk). Highest in the UK. White-collar employers stopped hiring at the bottom. The rungs didn't bend. They came off.

Here's the number nobody put together: AI-exposed jobs fell 11 points faster than the rest since 2022 (37% versus 26%). That gap is what it costs to be twenty-two and chasing an entry-level marketing or accounting role this year.

**2. Experience is the answer.** [The strongest parts of the market want specialist AI skills or years of experience](https://fortune.com/2026/08/02/ai-two-speed-job-market-uk-software-manufacturing-experience-entry-level/), says Indeed. Both take time. You can't buy time without a job to practise in. By 2030, a whole cohort will have skipped the years where you make cheap errors and a senior corrects them at the next desk. That correction is what experience is. The error is cheap now. The correction is gone.

**1. The safe job is the one nobody predicted would go first.** The [POST Parliament briefing published this month](https://post.parliament.uk/research-briefings/post-pn-0757/) finds that mid-to-high earners — software engineers, accountants, analysts — face more AI exposure than builders or electricians. Everyone said the machine would come for the shelf-stacker. It came for the graduate trainee. No van needed. No scaffolding. A laptop and a subscription.

The shelf-stacker is still there. The marketing coordinator is not.
