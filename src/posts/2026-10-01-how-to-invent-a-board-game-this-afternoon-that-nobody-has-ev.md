Every board game in your cupboard was invented by somebody who had a Tuesday afternoon free. Now it can be you.

**By the end of this, you'll have a complete, original board game with rules, components and a printable card set, designed to fit the people in your living room.** Not a kit. Not a reskin of Cluedo. Yours.

**Step 1. Pick a theme nobody would sell.** Monopoly already exists. What doesn't exist is a game about the allotments on your street, with a Complaints Committee card. Or a co-op where your book group races to finish a novel before someone spoils the ending. Or a deduction game about who ate the last biscuit. Write one sentence. That's your brief.

**Step 2. Generate the full design.** Go to [GameGrammar](https://gamegrammar.dynamindresearch.com/). It's free during public beta. Create an account. No payment needed. Type your theme and select Multi-Agent. [In under 90 seconds](https://bennycheung.github.io/introducing-gamegrammar-ai-powered-board-game-design), back comes a structured document: component counts, turn phases, win conditions, scoring formulas, and a balance critique flagging what's already broken. You get five generations a day without paying anything.

*Try this: "Co-op game for 3 to 5 players, about a book club racing to finish a novel before someone spoils the ending, 30 minutes."*

**Step 3. Fix what the AI broke.** It gives you the skeleton. It cannot know your grandmother cheats at cards or that one player will dispute every rule for twenty minutes before agreeing it was always fine. Read the flagged balance notes. Cut one mechanic. Add the house rule your group actually needs.

**Step 4. Write the flavour text.** Open Claude or ChatGPT. Paste in the rules document. Ask it to write the card text in the voice of a BBC continuity announcer, or in your group's own in-jokes. This is the part that makes it funny rather than just correct.

**Step 5. Print and play.** Index cards or cut-up A4 for the cards. A sketched sheet for the board. Poker chips from a pound shop for tokens. Hand-write the card text. The imperfection is half the charm.

**What it costs.** GameGrammar: free. Cards and markers: whatever's already in a drawer, or the cost of a cheap pack at a newsagent. Under an hour from theme to first play.

**Where it goes wrong.** The AI produces a balanced design. [It will not decide what is enjoyable, tune the feel, or judge when the game is finished](https://altheragames.com/en/blog/how-to-make-a-game-with-ai-2026). You will play it once and know instantly what to change. That is playtesting. It used to take designers months. Do the first game before you over-engineer the second draft.

**The number.** [GameGrammar produces a structured first draft in 73 seconds](https://bennycheung.github.io/introducing-gamegrammar-ai-powered-board-game-design). One manual weekend of concept work is 480 minutes. That is a 400-to-one compression of the blank-page stage. The afternoon around the table was never the bottleneck.
