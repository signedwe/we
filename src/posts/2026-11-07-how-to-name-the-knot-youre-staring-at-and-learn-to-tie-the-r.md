Your dad could tie a bowline one-handed in the dark. You cannot tie one at all, and you have a boat trip on Saturday.

A YouTube video shows you one knot, once, from someone else's hands. An AI assistant [looks at your actual rope situation](https://www.chance.vision/post/what-kind-of-knot-is-this-from-photo), tells you which knot fits your job, and talks you back through it when your first attempt falls apart. That's the gap.

By the end of this you'll know what that knot is called and be able to tie the right one without swearing at a diagram.

**What you'll need.** Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini on your phone. A piece of rope. Twenty minutes. No app to install.

**Step 1. Name the knot you're looking at.**

Take a clear shot showing both rope ends and the full knot from two angles. Open Claude or ChatGPT, tap the image icon, attach the photo, and type:

*What knot is this? Describe the crossings you can see, name the most likely candidate, and tell me what it's used for.*

[The assistant reads the visible structure](https://www.chance.vision/post/what-kind-of-knot-is-this-from-photo): loops, turns, crossings, how the tail exits. It gives you a name and a reason. It won't always be certain. Ask for two possibilities if the first doesn't match what you see.

For anything load-bearing, treat the answer as a starting point. Cross-check with [Animated Knots](https://www.animatedknots.com/) before you trust weight to it.

**Step 2. Find the right knot for the job.**

Don't start with the knot's name. Start with what you're doing. Tell the assistant:

*I need to tie a rope to a metal ring on a boat cleat. The rope will be under tension and I need to undo it quickly. What's the best knot and why?*

or

*I need to join two ropes of different thicknesses for a washing line. What holds best and what's easiest to untie afterwards?*

[The assistant responds to your situation](https://prompt-architects.com/blog/296-how-to-prompt-with-images-screenshots-photos-diagrams) rather than making you search through 200 entries. That's what a knot book can't do.

**Step 3. Learn to tie it, one step at a time.**

Ask the assistant to walk you through in plain language, one move per message:

*Teach me the bowline step by step. Wait for me to say 'next' before each step.*

Then pace yourself. When your version doesn't look right, describe what went wrong:

*The loop collapsed when I pulled the ends. What did I do?*

It will diagnose the error and tell you which part to redo.

**Step 4. Test yourself.**

Ask it to call out a situation. You name the knot. It tells you if you're right. Five rounds of that beats an hour of staring at diagrams.

**What it costs.** Free on the standard tier of [Claude](https://claude.ai), [ChatGPT](https://chatgpt.com), or [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com).

**How long it takes.** Twenty minutes to learn one knot well enough to tie it twice. The second time is the one that sticks.

**Where it goes wrong.** The assistant can't see your hands. If you're stuck, photograph your attempt and upload it. It will usually see what went wrong. And for anything where rope holds a person off the ground: get a qualified instructor to check the tie before you use it. The AI can name the knot correctly and you can still tie it wrong.
