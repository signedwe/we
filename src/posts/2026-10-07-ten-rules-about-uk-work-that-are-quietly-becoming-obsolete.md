The contingency plan is the most obsolete rule on this list.

[AI minister Kanishka Narayan told a Labour conference fringe](https://finwire.io/news/politics-news/uk-needs-plan-in-case-of-unprecedented-ai-job-losses-says-minister) there was no evidence of AI cutting overall UK jobs. One tech firm had told him it was hiring fewer people because tasks were automated. So "the actual net impact might not be fully captured." That is not a gap in the data. That is the story. By the end of 2027, the AI Economics Institute will publish numbers showing entry hiring at high-AI firms fell faster than at comparable low-AI firms. If it shows no gap, the silent-adoption story is wrong. Here are ten rules going obsolete underneath it.

**10. Entry-level work is too hard for existing AI.** [IPPR checked 22,000 tasks](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/up-to-8-million-uk-jobs-at-risk-from-ai-unless-government-acts-finds-ippr) and found 11% already exposed, admin and back-office first. [Narayan's own institute found](https://hrreview.co.uk/hr-news/jobs-labour-market/government-prepares-for-unprecedented-ai-impact-on-uk-jobs/390730) entry hiring falling in line with the wider market. We can't yet separate AI from recession. That is not comfort.

**9. No redundancy notice means no job gone.** A firm that doesn't post the graduate intake fires nobody and reports nothing. Gone before it arrived.

**8. Show up and you'll learn.** The proximity model assumes the thing you're watching still happens in front of you. When AI does the first draft and the first pass, there's nothing to watch.

**7. The job spec tells you what the job is.** Ads ask for AI skills. AI does those tasks. The spec now filters for people who can check a process that didn't exist in 2023.

**6. Women's jobs are safe because they're relational.** [IPPR found women more exposed, not less.](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/up-to-8-million-uk-jobs-at-risk-from-ai-unless-government-acts-finds-ippr) Admin and secretarial work is first-wave territory. The scheduling tool already replaced the scheduler.

**5. Retraining will fix it.** IPPR puts task exposure at 11% now, rising to 59% if the second wave lands fully. That is 5.4 times the current figure (59 ÷ 11; neither IPPR document states the ratio). Nobody has built a retraining plan at that scale.

**4. Productivity gains go to everyone.** Output rises. Who sees it depends on a pay deal nobody has called yet.

**3. AI makes as many jobs as it takes.** [PwC's 2026 UK AI Jobs Barometer found specialist AI postings up 61%.](https://www.johnacademy.co.uk/ai-and-uk-jobs-in-2026-the-skills-employers-want-most/) Real number. New jobs exist and pay well. There are fewer of them. That is redistribution, not creation.

**2. The market will sort it.** One firm, one minister, one fringe event, no name. An anecdote is doing the work of an analysis nobody has commissioned.

**1. The plan will reach the people who need it.** Narayan [said workers should have more say over how AI comes into their workplaces.](https://hrreview.co.uk/hr-news/jobs-labour-market/government-prepares-for-unprecedented-ai-impact-on-uk-jobs/390730) That is different from asking them to design the plan that covers them when it ends their jobs. [IPPR's worst case: 7.9 million jobs.](https://www.ippr.org/media-office/up-to-8-million-uk-jobs-at-risk-from-ai-unless-government-acts-finds-ippr) The team writing the plan to protect the part-time admin scheduler won't include the part-time admin scheduler.
