Every Tuesday for two years, and you're still crossing your legs on the turn. A coach would spot it in thirty seconds. They'd also charge you for the fifty-five minutes of paddling that follows.

By the end of this, you'll have a technique score, a list of what's actually wrong, and drills. From a phone video. Free.

**Why this beats YouTube.** YouTube shows you a perfect stroke. It cannot look at yours. That gap is the whole thing, and it's the one a search cannot close.

**What you need.** A pool, a friend with a phone, thirty seconds of swimming.

**Step 1. Film it right.**

Ask your friend to stand at the pool end, not the side. Chest height. Thirty seconds of continuous freestyle. Let you swim into frame rather than following you. Side-on also works. Don't film underwater: no tool reads it reliably from a phone.

*Say this: "Stand at the end, chest height, don't pan. Let me swim into shot."*

**Step 2. Two routes, both free.**

**Route A: Gemini (Android or iPhone, free tier).** [Google's Gemini accepts video uploads on its free tier](https://imaginario.ai/wide-lens/artificial-intelligence/can-chatgpt-analyze-video/), up to five minutes per clip. Open Gemini on your phone, tap the attachment icon, upload your clip. Then type: *"Watch my freestyle. Tell me my three biggest technical problems, what causes each one, and give me one drill per problem."* It reads the footage. Not a description of it.

**Route B: SportsReflector (iOS, free tier).** [Download SportsReflector](https://sportsreflector.com/) from the App Store. Select Swimming, upload your video. The app uses pose estimation to [track 17 body landmarks per frame](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/sportsreflector), scores your form on a 0 to 100 scale, and flags specific problems with drills attached. The [free plan covers core analysis and basic drills](https://www.capterra.com/p/10040191/SportsReflector/). No credit card required.

**Step 3. Do the one thing.**

Not three things. One. The AI will hand you five. Pick the first, use it for the whole next session, film again. The second problem usually sorts itself.

**Where it goes wrong.** Gemini catches pattern and context but can miss mechanics in under half a second. SportsReflector needs above-water footage. Underwater it sees nothing useful. Neither tool knows you are on your fourteenth length running on nothing. That changes everything about your catch.

**The number nobody quotes.** Standard phone video runs at 30 frames per second. Thirty seconds of footage at [17 body landmarks tracked per frame](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/sportsreflector) gives 15,300 data points from one poolside clip. A coach at the lane end catches maybe three or four observations per length by eye. The machine's advantage isn't intelligence. It's counting.

**What it costs.** Gemini: free. [SportsReflector free tier](https://sportsreflector.com/reviews): free, with a 14-day trial before Pro at $19.99 a month.

**How long.** Filming: 2 minutes. Analysis: under 5 minutes.
