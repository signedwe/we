On 13 August 1949, from South Tamworth, New Hampshire, the mathematician who had just invented cybernetics wrote to the president of the United Auto Workers. On 2 November 2026, [Google UK signed an agreement letting a union hold meetings in its offices](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/02/unions-google-workers-uk-employment-rights). Here is the letter, with this week written in the margin.

**"I have turned down unconditionally the request of the industrial company which has tried to consult me."** [A firm had asked Wiener's advice on automatic controls; he refused, and told the union instead](https://libcom.org/history/father-cybernetics-norbert-wieners-letter-uaw-president-walter-reuther). *Margin:* nobody at Google refused anything. The company that built the machine also chose which union got the door. [Two other unions say Google signed a day after their own request, with a union they call friendlier to the employer](https://novaramedia.com/?p=70544).

**"Automatic machinery will undoubtedly lead to the factory without employees."** *Margin:* [Google UK employs about 7,000 people, and the deal covers them](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/02/unions-google-workers-uk-employment-rights). The factory without employees arrived as a factory with 7,000 employees and a model doing a growing share of the work. Wiener got the shape wrong and the direction right.

**"In the hands of the present industrial set-up, the unemployment produced by such plants can only be disastrous."** *Margin:* note the condition. Not the machine. The set-up. He did not oppose the plant. He opposed its owners.

**"It would not be at all foolish to steal a march upon the existing industrial corporations."** *Margin:* nobody quotes this line. He proposed that labour get ahead of capital, take part in producing the machines itself, and so "secure the profits in them to an organization dedicated to the benefit of labor." [He offered to back the union loyally and share everything he knew](https://libcom.org/history/father-cybernetics-norbert-wieners-letter-uaw-president-walter-reuther). A union that owned the machine would need no access agreement. It would employ the workers.

**What the unions asked for in 2026.** [Pay, flexibility, how AI gets deployed, and workplace culture](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/02/unions-google-workers-uk-employment-rights), through the right to hold meetings and post on an internal page. [The TUC expects a wave of such deals over the next year](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/2026/nov/02/unions-google-workers-uk-employment-rights). Every item on that list sets a term on which the machine arrives. Not one lays a claim on the machine.

The arithmetic: Wiener put the critical point "ten to twenty years" from 1949, so 1959 to 1969. The access deal came 77 years after the letter, 57 years past his latest date. The unions now ask for the smallest thing on his list, long after the window in which he said the large thing had to happen.

WE does not say Prospect should have refused the door. The organiser below has a point: a door helps. WE says the paper and the TUC describe the door as the prize, when the man who saw this coming called it the consolation.

What would the large thing look like now? Not a union buying servos. [The value in the current machine lives in the data and the compute](https://signedwe.github.io/we/history/2026-11-09-norbert-wiener-cybernetics-and-the-macy-conferences-history/), and a union can buy neither. But the data came from workers' words, taken without a price. A union that walked into King's Cross and asked what its members' output had earned as training material, and who got paid for it, would ask Wiener's question in Wiener's spirit. Nobody has asked it yet.

WE's bet: by the end of 2027 no UK union recognition or access agreement with a large AI firm will contain a clause on ownership of, or payment for, workers' data used in training. If one does, WE will say so. Gladly.

Wiener posted the letter in 1949. This week's answer: a meeting place and an internal page.
