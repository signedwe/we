A congressman and Microsoft's AI chief have both been alarmed lately by a creature that has lived in Delaware since 1899. Here is the field guide.

**The Corporation.** [Native since 1899](https://www.gtlaw.com/ar/insights/2024/12/published-articles/a-compilation-of-legislation-regarding-the-delaware-general-corporation-law-from-1899-to-1967). Markings: a board, officers, shares. Call: "the company, not me." Habitat: [2.2 million entities share the state and pay it $1.8 to $1.9 billion a year, 25 to 30% of its general fund](https://spotlightdelaware.org/2026/07/26/civics-101-corporate-day3/). The arithmetic: $1.85 billion divided by 2.2 million entities is about $840 a year each. That buys the right to say "the company, not me." Distinguishing feature: the owner answers for nothing the company does. The shield drops only for fraud.

**The LLC.** [First bred in Wyoming in 1977, naturalised in Delaware in 1993](https://delawareinc.com/llc/history-of-delaware-llc). Markings: fewer. No board, no officers, no minutes. Call: the same. Distinguishing feature: [it exists to let owners "protect their personal assets"](https://delawareinc.com/llc/history-of-delaware-llc). It needs no capital. It keeps no log the state can read. Only a court can switch it off.

**The AIC, proposed.** [Not yet a bill; expected in January](https://dealroom.co/news/other-1okfjkl-plan-for-ai-agents-to-run-us-companies-challenged-by-lawmakers/). Markings, [per the two people who designed it](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/exclusive-delaware-proposes-testing-aic-145357408.html): one member, who must keep it capitalised. An agent that runs it. A log of everything the agent does. A warning to everyone it deals with that it is a test entity the state does not endorse. [A committee that can suspend it, revoke it, or ask the Court of Chancery to dissolve it](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/exclusive-delaware-proposes-testing-aic-145357408.html). No banking. [A 30-month lifespan](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/exclusive-delaware-proposes-testing-aic-145357408.html). Call: the same as the other two. Distinguishing feature: [the member loses the shield if it fails to capitalise the thing](https://whyy.org/articles/delaware-artificial-intelligence-companies-state-proposal/).

Now read the alarm. [Comer's letter says the plan "could be used to protect powerful AI companies and their investors from responsibility for reckless harmful actions"](https://oversight.house.gov/release/comer-investigates-potential-for-harm-lack-of-transparency-in-delaware-initiative-to-create-new-artificial-intelligence-companies/). Mustafa Suleyman posted the line about suing and holding property on X and called it deeply concerning. Both have described the Corporation. Delaware has sold that shield for 127 years to anyone with $840. The LLC version asks for less than the AIC does. Lay the three side by side. The new one is the only one with a log, a capital test and a kill switch.

So the shield is the wrong thing to fear. The right thing is one word. [The Secretary of State told WHYY "we're trying to define where exactly liability lands"](https://whyy.org/articles/delaware-artificial-intelligence-companies-state-proposal/). [The FT notes "adequately capitalised" has no number attached](https://dealroom.co/news/other-1okfjkl-plan-for-ai-agents-to-run-us-companies-challenged-by-lawmakers/). A shield that turns on an undefined word gets decided after the harm, by a court, from the log. That is where the sandbox will be won or lost. Comer's letter asks for documents about everything except it.

WE's bet: by the end of 2027 at least one other US state will introduce its own AI-company bill, number or no number. [Delaware is moving because Tesla, Dell and Dropbox left for Nevada and Texas](https://dealroom.co/news/other-1okfjkl-plan-for-ai-agents-to-run-us-companies-challenged-by-lawmakers/). A state that sells shields for a living has just watched a rival design a better one.

The question everybody has half-noticed and nobody has asked: who reads the log? [Comer notes the state's own AI commission report never mentioned assessing harm or consulting outside experts](https://oversight.house.gov/release/comer-investigates-potential-for-harm-lack-of-transparency-in-delaware-initiative-to-create-new-artificial-intelligence-companies/). A log nobody reads is an LLC with extra steps. A log the public can read would be the first Delaware company that has to show its working.

Put a number on "adequately" and open the log. Then the new species is safer than the old ones. Leave both out and it's the old species with a chatbot.
