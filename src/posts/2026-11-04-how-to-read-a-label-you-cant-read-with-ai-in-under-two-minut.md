You're standing in the kitchen holding a tin of something that smells incredible. Your neighbour brought it back from Seoul, or you grabbed it at the Turkish supermarket on the high street, or it was in a gift box and nobody thought to mention that every word on it is in Hangul.

At the end of this, you'll know what's in it, whether it has anything you need to avoid, and what the numbers mean. Two minutes.

**Without AI:** Google the brand. Hope someone reviewed it. Probably don't find it. Give up or eat it and hope.

**With AI:** You ask about *your* label, in *your* words, and it answers back. No YouTube tutorial covers your exact tin. That's the difference.

---

**What you need:** A phone with ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini on it. Free tier works.

**How long:** Two minutes if the photo's clear, five if the label curves.

---

**Step 1.** Take the photo. Get close. The ingredient list is usually the small block of text near the bottom. If the label wraps round the tin, take two photos. Good light matters more than a good camera.

**Step 2.** Open [ChatGPT](https://chat.openai.com), [Claude](https://claude.ai) or [Gemini](https://gemini.google.com). Tap the photo icon and attach your image.

**Step 3.** Type this:

*Please translate this food label fully. List every ingredient, any allergen warnings, the storage instructions, the best-before format, and any additive codes — like E-numbers or their local equivalents. I'm based in the UK.*

That last sentence matters. It nudges the assistant to flag palm oil listed under a local trade name, or additives approved differently in the UK than where the product was made.

**Step 4.** Ask follow-up questions. The photo stays attached. You can ask "is there anything a vegetarian would need to know?" or "what does the storage symbol mean?" without uploading again. [That's what a basic translation app can't do](https://aiworldhq.com/article/translate-foreign-documents-and-everyday-signs-with-gemini).

**What it costs:** Nothing. [ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all handle image input on their free plans](https://rappn.ch/en/ai-recipes-from-ingredients), confirmed by their own published help pages as of October 2026.

**Where it goes wrong:** Small print on curved surfaces is hard. If the assistant can't read a section, retake the photo flat on a table with the label pulled tight. Korean and Japanese scripts work well. Handwritten labels or embossed text on the tin itself can fail completely.

**If you have a serious food allergy:** Use this to understand a label, not to clear one. [Image translation can miss small print and misread an unfamiliar ingredient name](https://blog.openl.io/translate-food-labels-with-your-phone/). For anything that could put you in hospital, the label in its original language plus a human who reads it is the only safe standard.

One more thing worth knowing. If you bought the product from an unlicensed importer at a market stall, it may not carry the allergen warnings UK law requires at all. AI reads what's there. It can't flag what's absent.
