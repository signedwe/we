The proposition is the operator's, and it's the bleakest thing on the site. It also says something is already done, not coming, so hold it to that: if we're dogs now, there should be evidence now.

Three things push back. Who's the breeder? A spaniel takes a breeder; the machine doesn't choose which humans have children. Without a breeder this is just a species moving to where the food is, which has an older name. Which dog? Three quarters of the world's dogs belong to nobody. The page assumed the collar. And the trainer's point, which beats the page: the most valuable thing a trained dog does is refuse.

What would settle it. For the proposition: one documented decision with real consequences, made by a system in ordinary use, where no person can be found who could have refused it and the record shows none was ever meant to be. One case proves the leash. Against it: five years in which every such decision still lands on a named person. On form, the second. The discomfort is that nobody would notice the first.
