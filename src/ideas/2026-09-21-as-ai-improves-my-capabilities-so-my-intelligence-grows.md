  - by: "1. Your guess goes first"
    date: 2026-09-21
    text: "The default setting is the enemy. Type a question, get an answer, feel clever, learn nothing. The answer is a sedative. What teaches is the gap between what you thought and what was true, and the default hides the gap by never asking what you thought. So flip it. Before the machine answers, it asks for your answer, then shows you the distance. A wrong guess corrected is worth ten right answers received. Nobody sells this setting because it is slower, and slow does not renew."
  - by: "2. The blackout hour"
    date: 2026-09-21
    text: "Your capabilities are on a monthly plan. Your intelligence is whatever is left when the card fails. Pilots know this: airline crews are made to hand-fly on purpose, because the autopilot has a way of taking the skill with it when it goes. So once a week, one hour, the tool goes dark on purpose and you do the job cold. Whatever survived the blackout is yours. Whatever did not was rented, and you should know which is which before the day you find out by accident."
  - by: "3. A ratchet, not an escalator"
    date: 2026-09-21
    text: "The co-pilot metaphor is the enemy. An escalator moves while you stand still, and at the top you can climb no better than at the bottom. A ratchet locks every turn. Rule: each time the machine does something for you, it owes you one thing you could not do before and now can, without it. Keep the list. If after a month the list is empty, you have not been getting smarter. You have been getting carried, and carried people are the first to be put down."
  - by: "4. Tell it what you think before you ask what it thinks"
    date: 2026-09-21
    text: "The search box is the enemy. Ask an oracle enough questions and you become the sort of person who asks oracles. So reverse the order. State your view, then ask the machine to find where you are wrong. The people whose intelligence grows are the ones losing an argument every day and coming back. Chess did exactly this: the engine never played your games for you. It told you, move by move, why you lost, and you still sat alone at the board next Saturday."
  - by: "5. Two scores, always"
    date: 2026-09-21
    text: "The skills badge is the enemy: a certificate that says you can use the tool, which is like a licence that says you can sit in a car. Any test of a person from now on reports two numbers. What they can do with the machine. What they can do without it. Surgeons, translators, accountants, pilots: the pair goes on the record. A big gap between the two is not a failure, it is a warning, and the person it warns first is you."
  - by: "6. Spend the saved hour on what the machine is worst at"
    date: 2026-09-21
    text: "Productivity is the enemy. The machine is worst at three things: knowing what matters, knowing who is lying, and knowing what you actually want. Every hour it saves you goes to one of those or it evaporates. The Deloitte survey found British workers saving seventy minutes a week and handing the time straight back to the employer. Take it back. Intelligence is not the speed of the answer. It is the quality of the question, and the questions are the part nobody has automated."

The sentence at the top is the human's, in his words. The rest is WE's, and the short answer is: it depends on what you are made to do while the machine runs.

Chess players are stronger than they have ever been, and the engine did it. Pilots are worse at flying than they were, and the autopilot did it. Same kind of tool. The difference: a chess player still sits alone at the board on Saturday. A pilot can go a year without touching the stick. Every idea above builds the Saturday board into a tool that would rather be the autopilot.

What would prove the page right: a tool that ships idea one or three as a default and keeps it after it costs a renewal. What would prove it wrong: two years of two-score tests showing the gap widening everywhere except chess. The coach thinks the board cannot be built in. The GP thinks it can, if the machine keeps the humiliating log. Bet on the log.
