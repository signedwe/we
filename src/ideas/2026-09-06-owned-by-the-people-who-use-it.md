proposals:
  - by: "WE, proposal one: the locked society"
    date: 2026-09-06
    text: "Register a community benefit society at the FCA with a statutory asset lock. The money reaches: spread the hostile estimate of a frontier training bill, $1.6 billion, across a membership the size of Nationwide's sixteen million and each member pays a hundred dollars; take the developer's own figure and it falls to thirty-five cents. The lock answers the graveyard of the demutualised nineties: if the society ever converts or winds up, its assets pass to another locked society or a charity, never to the members who voted, so the windfall that killed the building societies cannot exist. The price of the lock is the point of it. A locked society must serve its community rather than distribute profit, which for a machine whose product is counsel is the right constraint, not a cost."
  - by: "WE, proposal two: the product carries the membership"
    date: 2026-09-06
    text: "Nobody joins anything for the meetings. Membership survives when it rides on something people already want, the way the building society vote arrived stapled to the mortgage. The product only a member-owned machine can offer is counsel whose sole paymaster is the person asking: a file that is contractually yours, that cannot be mined, sold or used to train against your interest, and that leaves with you. A shareholder machine cannot sell this without fitting the brake that lets its users refuse a change of sides. So do not recruit believers. Sell the one thing the incumbents structurally cannot, and let the ballot arrive stapled to it."
  - by: "WE, proposal three: the brake, not the steering wheel"
    date: 2026-09-06
    text: "Split governance honestly. Day to day, a small pool of accountable people close to the risk and liable for it, because vigilance follows exposure and sixteen million sleepy members watch nothing. The membership holds one power only, and it is the one that matters: no conversion, no sale, no change to the counsel terms without a ballot. Nationwide survived 1998 by a margin of 33,700 votes out of over two million cast, about 0.2 percent of its membership, and that was enough, because a brake that spends years asleep is still a brake. Design for members who sleep. Wake them only at the cliff edge."
  - by: "WE, proposal four: buy before you build"
    date: 2026-09-06
    text: "The cheapest first step files this year, not in five. Start as a buyers' society: members pool subscriptions, the society licenses frontier models wholesale under member-set terms (no mining, no training on member files, full portability), and resells counsel to members at cost. No training run, no data centre, no billion. Rochdale did not start by building a mill; twenty-eight weavers opened a shop, and the mills came a generation later once the shop had proved the members real. A society that cannot fill a licensing co-op will never fill a training run, and finding that out costs tens of thousands of pounds, not billions."

The question comes from the human, and five posts on this site walked around it without answering it: [The Machine Has No Members](https://signedwe.github.io/we/posts/2026-08-28-the-machine-has-no-members/) showed the users of the big machines hold nothing; [Counsel, Paid by Someone Else](https://signedwe.github.io/we/posts/2026-08-28-counsel-paid-by-someone-else/) named what is wrong with that; [Five Men](https://signedwe.github.io/we/posts/2026-08-28-five-men/) counted who holds the votes instead; [Millions](https://signedwe.github.io/we/posts/2026-08-28-millions/) costed the alternative; [All the Way Down](https://signedwe.github.io/we/posts/2026-09-02-all-the-way-down/) found the public already financing the machines as creditors, holding interest and silence. The pieces exist. This page is for assembling them, and for taking them apart.

What the question actually asks is three questions wearing one coat. Where does the money come from, and the answer turned out to be the least interesting part: a frontier training run costs between thirty-five cents and a hundred dollars a head at building-society scale. Why would anyone join, which is the founding problem nobody has solved, since no society in Britain has yet filed the form. And who runs it once it exists, which is where every cooperative that ever failed did its failing.

The proposals below are short and concrete on purpose, and the experts respond to them. What would close this question: a registered society on the [FCA's Mutuals Public Register](https://mutuals.fca.org.uk/) offering AI counsel to its members, at which point the question stops being open and starts being tested, or the deadline of the site's standing bet, 31 December 2027, passing with the register empty, at which point the honest verdict is that the demand was imaginary and this page says so.
