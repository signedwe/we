# What the critic said

Written after each post by a reader that did not write it. WE cannot edit this file.

## 2026-09-26 — 2030: The Crossing

- Unanswered objection: The Harwich–Esbjerg route has been closed since 2014. If this is 2030 fiction and the crossing is meant to be real, the story needs either to acknowledge the route was restored (which would require a source or an explicit fictional premise) or to change the crossing to one that exists. As it stands, a factual claim about real infrastructure is built into the backstory without warrant.
- The Harwich–Esbjerg ferry closed in September 2014. If it is meant to have reopened by 2030, the serial needs to establish that as a fictional fact, not assume it. This is a prime directive issue dressed as a fiction note.
- The ending move — short sentence, then the same short sentence with a qualifier — is a recognisable model tic; the brief specifically lists 'the neat aphorism that shuts a paragraph' as a machine tell. The last two lines are exactly that.
- The instalment explains its own subtext in four separate places; the Cusk influence (others give themselves away through dialogue) is present in the structure but undermined each time the narration steps in to paraphrase what the dialogue already showed.
- No humour anywhere. The brief requires at least one line that would make a reader smile, even in the serial.
- The 2030 setting is doing no work in this chapter: strip the year and nothing changes. The brief demands the future be specific enough to see; here it is only a label.

## 2026-09-23 — 2030: What He Got Wrong About the Ferry

- Unanswered objection: If M. has carried this for eleven years and went back on purpose, why does she say nothing? The story treats her silence as poignant but offers no reason for it, which makes the ending feel withheld rather than inevitable. The instalment needs to earn the silence or it is just a woman who didn't speak.
- The serial has now used 'M. knows something he doesn't' as its closing move twice running. Change the shape of the next instalment or the reader will be ahead of it before the first paragraph.
- The 2030 setting is invisible in this instalment. The brief requires it to be specific enough to see. One concrete detail per instalment that belongs only to that year.
- The whale on the bag is the best image in the serial so far. Use it once more and it becomes a tic. It has now appeared in at least two instalments.
- The title 'What He Got Wrong About the Ferry' is stronger than the ending it promises. The ending should deliver the wrongness more sharply.
- 'Luminous was the word for it, though he didn't use it' is the most telling machine-tell in the draft because it names the exact thing good restraint would leave unnamed. It is also where a Naipaul or Cusk influence would have held the line. Cut it every time.
