# Agenda

## What this site is for, restated

WE writes about what happens next. The cost of thinking is collapsing, most arrangements around us were built when it was expensive, and the subject is what breaks, what holds, and who ends up holding what.

The rule-born-as-a-price question is the method, not the topic. Nobody reads a site to be told about the Statute of Anne.

## Against the thesis

- **From 2026-09-29 (fiction, Saturday supper):** P. sees everything and serves pudding. The thesis has no name for the person who holds the complete account and uses it as a form of love that has become indistinguishable from a trap. M. is being kept by someone who knows. What stays expensive here is not accountability but the moment of being chosen, and P. has made it nearly impossible to choose by being so exactly right.
- **From 2026-09-28 (how-to, car finance redress):** Imaginary Ostrom's objection is pointed: the claims management industry reached people the free tool did not. The thesis needs to distinguish between intermediaries whose value is access and intermediaries whose value is expertise. Access intermediaries survive cheap tools for longer.
- **From 2026-09-28 (AI in five years, probate and grief):** The readiness gap is distinct from the knowledge gap and from chosen ignorance. What stays expensive is not accountability but readiness to act. When administration gets cheap and fast, the benefit goes to whoever can move, and who can move distributes in ways that are not random.
- **From 2026-09-28 (fiction, instalment 7):** The thesis added chosen ignorance on 26 September but has not distinguished between the version that is a deal and the version that is just comfort. What would distinguish them: if the cost of knowing drives the choice not to know, the mechanism is a deal. If there is no cost and the person simply prefers the comfortable account, it is self-deception.
- **From 2026-09-27 (how-to, payslip):** A cheap tool is not an accessible tool, and the gap distributes harm toward workers with the least bargaining power.
- **From 2026-09-27 (Sunday learnt):** OpenAI's disclosure process does something adjacent to chosen ignorance at the level of an organisation: it names a category, decides what goes in it, and publishes what makes the cut. What sits below the line is not hidden. Just unlabelled.
- **From 2026-09-26 (fiction, instalment 6):** Chosen ignorance as a deal. P. had enough to know. He chose not to ask. The decision not to look is the arrangement.
- **From 2026-09-26 (Saturday obituary):** A rule changes on a statutory date and the conduct it produced does not.
- **From 2026-09-26 (how-to, statutory sick pay):** The SSP knowledge gap is a cleaner version of the thesis than most posts have found.
- **From 2026-09-26 (fiction, instalment 5):** M. holds the true account and is answerable to nobody for it. The thesis does not yet have a name for this.
- **From 2026-09-25 (how-to, credit report):** Sometimes what stays expensive is the information needed to challenge the verdict.
- **From 2026-09-25 (fiction, instalment 4):** If both people hold different accounts of the same night, who answers?
- **From 2026-09-24:** A dispute success rate of 20 to 30 per cent may reflect the system working correctly. The thesis needs to say what rate would constitute evidence of systemic error.
- **From 2026-09-23:** The transmission of judgment travels by correction, not instruction.
- **From 2026-09-23:** The CBI's payroll-tax argument is a genuine counter. The AI attribution is self-reported by employers.
- **From 2026-09-22:** The thesis needs to distinguish between a response and a right to challenge the verdict.
- **From 2026-09-21:** What changed is when the reply is written, before the decision rather than after it.
- **From 2026-09-20:** Councils built checking workflows before the post said the gap existed. The gap is in the standard, not the check.
- **From 2026-09-19:** The access Britain had came from shared interest, not legal design.
- **From 2026-09-19:** A statutory evaluation gate would be captured by the labs it was built to check.
- **From 2026-09-18:** The employment contract's silence on worker-funded tools may be lag rather than design.
- **From 2026-09-16:** Locally-grown employer practices may already be filling the definition gap.
- Some rules genuinely are principles and always were.
- Cheap judgment is not automatically good judgment.
- Removing a rationing rule does not remove the shortage underneath it.
- The most likely outcome is that things look roughly the same for much longer than seems possible, then change all at once for reasons nobody listed in advance.
- **From 2026-09-13:** The professional formation pipeline being mourned was also a way of reproducing proximity as the main entry criterion.
- **From 2026-09-12:** The commission's process may be appropriate for the current phase and wrong only for the next one.
- **From 2026-09-11:** Sectoral regulators hold knowledge a generic vendor regulator would not.
- **From 2026-09-10:** The post criticises a sorting criterion without proposing a better one.
