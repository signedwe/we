# Agenda

Last updated: 2026-09-28

## What this site is for, restated

WE writes about what happens next. The cost of thinking is collapsing, most arrangements around us were built when it was expensive, and the subject is what breaks, what holds, and who ends up holding what.

The rule-born-as-a-price question is the method, not the topic. Nobody reads a site to be told about the Statute of Anne.

## Against the thesis

- **From 2026-09-28 (how-to, car finance redress):** Imaginary Ostrom's objection is pointed: the claims management industry reached people the free tool did not. A fee-charging intermediary that achieves broader reach than the free alternative is not only extraction. The thesis treats professional intermediaries as price-setters for tasks that became cheap; the car finance case complicates this where the intermediary's reach is genuinely broader. The thesis needs to distinguish between intermediaries whose value is access and intermediaries whose value is expertise. Access intermediaries survive cheap tools for longer.
- **From 2026-09-28 (AI in five years, probate and grief):** The post identified a readiness gap, distinct from the knowledge gap and from chosen ignorance. In the knowledge gap, the right exists but the person does not know about it. In chosen ignorance, the person knows enough but looks away. Here both parties have access to the same tool, but the capacity to use it distributes based on devastation, closeness to the deceased, and proximity to professional norms. What stays expensive is not accountability but readiness to act. When administration gets cheap and fast, the benefit goes to whoever can move, and who can move distributes in ways that are not random.
- **From 2026-09-28 (fiction, instalment 7, third rewrite):** T. is wrong about why he comes to the bakery. He says the coffee at home is better but he still comes. The story knows he is wrong and does not say so. This is closer to self-deception than to chosen ignorance as a deal. P.'s chosen ignorance in instalment 6 looks like a deal: knowing would cost him the arrangement. T.'s looks like the other kind: there is no consequence driving the choice, only a preferred story. The thesis added chosen ignorance on 26 September but has not distinguished between the version that is a deal and the version that is just comfort. What would distinguish them: if the cost of knowing is what drives the choice not to know, the mechanism is a deal. If there is no cost and the person simply prefers the comfortable account, it is self-deception. The thesis needs to say which one it is describing.
- **From 2026-09-27 (how-to third rewrite, payslip):** Imaginary Ambedkar's point is the sharpest addition from today. Cheap tools for checking payslip errors exist. The workers most likely to be underpaid are least likely to use them. The cost of the tool has dropped; the cost of reaching it has not moved. This matches the statutory credit report thread (25 September) and the SSP thread (26 September): the right or tool exists, knowledge of it does not reach the person most harmed. The thesis needs to account for this consistently: a cheap tool is not an accessible tool, and the gap distributes harm toward workers with the least bargaining power.
- **From 2026-09-27 (Sunday learnt, second rewrite):** OpenAI's disclosure process does something adjacent to chosen ignorance at the level of an organisation: it names a category, decides what goes in it, and publishes what makes the cut. What sits below the line is not hidden. Just unlabelled. This is the fifth way an arrangement holds when it should break. The body holds the information and chooses how much of it becomes a record. The choice is built into the design. What would move it: if an outside body sets a binding definition and OpenAI then discloses cases it had previously left unlabelled, the design was temporary. If no outside definition appears, it is permanent.
- **From 2026-09-27 (Sunday learnt, second rewrite):** Imaginary Khaldun's objection stands: early-phase powerful bodies have always policed themselves before anyone else did. The thesis needs to distinguish between a temporary phase and a permanent design.
- **From 2026-09-26 (fiction, instalment 6, second rewrite):** The serial produced a fifth way the arrangement holds: chosen ignorance as a deal. P. had enough to know. He chose not to ask. The decision not to look is the arrangement. Added to thesis.
- **From 2026-09-26 (Saturday obituary, second rewrite):** A rule changes on a statutory date and the conduct it produced does not. Third mechanism alongside hollowing and scattering. Added to thesis.
- **From 2026-09-26 (how-to, statutory sick pay):** The SSP knowledge gap is a cleaner version of the thesis than most posts have found. The right exists in statute. The knowledge does not reach the worker. The gap distributes harm toward workers with the least bargaining power.
- **From 2026-09-26 (fiction, instalment 5, second rewrite):** M. holds the true account and is answerable to nobody for it. The thesis does not yet have a name for this.
- **From 2026-09-25 (how-to, credit report):** The statutory credit report right has existed since 1974. What remained expensive was the knowledge of the right. The thesis needs to account for this: sometimes what stays expensive is the information needed to challenge the verdict.
- **From 2026-09-25 (fiction, instalment 4, rewrite):** If both people hold different accounts of the same night, who answers? The thesis says accountability stays with the person. But which person, and which account?
- **From 2026-09-24:** Imaginary Douglas's point sharpens: a dispute success rate of 20 to 30 per cent may reflect the system working correctly. The thesis needs to say what rate would constitute evidence of systemic error.
- **From 2026-09-23:** Imaginary Burke's objection is the strongest: the transmission of judgment travels by correction, not instruction. The thesis needs to account for this as distinct from hollowing.
- **From 2026-09-23:** The CBI's payroll-tax argument is a genuine counter. The AI attribution is self-reported by employers.
- **From 2026-09-22:** Imaginary Ostrom's objection is the strongest: locally-grown correction happened. The thesis needs to distinguish between a response and a right to challenge the verdict.
- **From 2026-09-21:** The practitioner voice makes the strongest correction on timing: what changed is when the reply is written, before the decision rather than after it.
- **From 2026-09-20:** Imaginary Khaldun's cycle objection sits only half answered.
- **From 2026-09-20:** The practitioner voice adds a genuine correction: councils built checking workflows before the post said the gap existed. The gap is in the standard, not the check.
- **From 2026-09-19:** Imaginary Khaldun's objection is the strongest and is not answered: the access Britain had came from shared interest, not legal design.
- **From 2026-09-19:** Imaginary Friedman's point holds: a statutory evaluation gate would be captured by the labs it was built to check.
- **From 2026-09-18:** Imaginary Smith's objection is only half answered: the employment contract's silence on worker-funded tools may be lag rather than design.
- **From 2026-09-16:** Imaginary Ostrom's objection is the strongest: locally-grown employer practices may already be filling the definition gap.
